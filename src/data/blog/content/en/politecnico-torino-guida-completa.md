# Polytechnic University of Turin: complete guide for students

The Polytechnic University of Turin (Politecnico di Torino) is one of the leading academic institutions in Italy and Europe in the fields of engineering, architecture, and design. With a history rooted in the 19th century and a consolidated international reputation, the university stands out for its high-quality education, extraordinary graduate employment rates, and excellent research and industry collaboration opportunities. Officially founded in 1906, the Politecnico now attracts over 38,000 students from more than 100 countries, offering a multicultural and stimulating environment where technological innovation and academic tradition merge to prepare the professionals of the future.

## History and Foundation: From Origins to Modernity

The origins of the Polytechnic University of Turin date back to 1859, when the Royal School of Application for Engineers (Regia Scuola di Applicazione per gli Ingegneri) was established, a pioneering institution created to train the technicians needed for the material construction of unified Italy. This school, located at Valentino Castle, became a reference point for training engineers and architects who contributed to building the infrastructure of the new Kingdom of Italy.

July 1, 1906 marked a decisive turning point: the Royal School of Application for Engineers merged with the Royal Italian Industrial Museum, giving birth to the Royal Polytechnic of Turin. The official inauguration took place at Valentino Castle, in the presence of political authorities, prominent professors, and representatives from the industrial world. From its first academic year, the university welcomed about 500 students from across the country, immediately signaling its national vocation. The new institution was born with the declared objective of uniting applied research and higher education, responding to the needs of industrial Turin in full growth, which required engineers prepared for the mechanical, automotive, and electrical industries.

Throughout the 20th century, the Politecnico consolidated its reputation and expanded its educational offerings. After the bombings of World War II destroyed the historic headquarters, the university was rebuilt in 1958 at its current location on Corso Duca degli Abruzzi, a modern complex designed to house classrooms, laboratories, and research spaces. In subsequent years, the Politecnico continued to evolve, establishing in 1986 the School for Industrial Production Experts, which in 2000 was authorized to grant degrees, representing the first example of a "European" degree coordinated among different universities. In 2009, the Tashkent campus in Uzbekistan was inaugurated, marking the international expansion of the university.

## Academic Programs and Departments: An Articulated Structure

The Polytechnic University of Turin is distinguished by a broad and diversified educational offering, organized around 11 departments covering all areas of engineering, architecture, design, and territorial planning. The main departments include:

- **Architecture and Design**
- **Control and Computer Engineering**
- **Electronics and Telecommunications**
- **Energy**
- **Environmental, Land and Infrastructure Engineering**
- **Management and Production Engineering**
- **Mechanical and Aerospace Engineering**
- **Structural, Geotechnical and Building Engineering**
- **Applied Science and Technology**
- **Mathematical Sciences**
- **Interuniversity Department of Regional and Urban Studies and Planning**

The university offers a total of over 100 study paths including bachelor's degrees, master's degrees, doctoral programs, university masters, and continuing education programs. Bachelor's degree programs are available in both Italian and English and range from computer engineering to mechanical, aerospace, biomedical, management, energy, and environmental engineering, in addition to courses in architecture, design, and territorial planning. Master's degrees, totaling 36 programs, deepen the skills acquired during the bachelor's and include innovative paths such as Architecture for Sustainable Design, Automotive Engineering, and Systemic Design.

Particularly appreciated are the courses delivered entirely in English, which attract students from around the world and foster a multicultural environment. These include Civil and Environmental Engineering, Computer Engineering, Mechanical Engineering, and Architecture Construction and City. Additionally, the university offers doctoral research programs through the Doctoral School (ScuDo) and the Alta Scuola Politecnica (ASP), established in collaboration with the Polytechnic University of Milan, which represents an excellence path for meritorious students.

The educational offering is constantly updated to respond to labor market needs and contemporary technological challenges. Computer Science, Mechanical, Aerospace, Biomedical, Management, and Automotive courses attract 73% of new enrollees, confirming the attractiveness of these sectors. Architecture and Territorial Planning also show positive trends, with nearly 2,000 enrolled in admission tests for the Architecture course in academic year 2024/2025.

## Admission and Entrance Test: The TIL

All degree programs at the Polytechnic University of Turin have limited enrollment, meaning that to access them it is necessary to pass an admission test called **TIL (Test d'Ingresso Online - Online Entrance Test)**. The TIL is mandatory and consists of an individual computerized test, with questions automatically and randomly selected for each candidate, thus ensuring that all tests have equal difficulty.

### TIL Test Versions

The test is divided into different versions depending on the field of study:

| Version | Field of Study |
|---------|----------------|
| TIL-I | Engineering |
| TIL-D | Design and Communication |
| TIL-P | Territorial Planning |
| TIL-A | Architecture |

Each test consists of **42 questions** divided into four disciplinary sections (mathematics, physics, logic, and verbal comprehension) and candidates have **1 hour and 30 minutes** to complete it. The scoring system provides:

- **+1 point** for each correct answer
- **0 points** for each unanswered question
- **-0.25 points** for each error

### Scores and Admission Thresholds

For Engineering courses, the minimum threshold for ranking is **30/100**, while the guaranteed score for enrollment in the first-choice course is set at **60/100**. For Design, Planning, and Architecture courses, thresholds vary: for example, for Design and Planning the guaranteed threshold is **70/100**, while for Architecture it is **60/100**.

### Orientation Training Project

The Politecnico offers an "Orientation Training Project" aimed at final-year high school students, consisting of a cycle of preparatory lessons in mathematics, physics, and specific subjects. Those who participate in this project, attending at least 70% of the lessons, can take the TIL in an early session (February-March) instead of the standard sessions held between May and September. The participation fee for the project is **25 euros**.

### Record Numbers

Admission tests have recorded record numbers in recent years: for academic year 2024/2025, nearly **15,500 students** participated in the tests, marking a 28% increase from the previous year. Of these, over 3,500 were international students, plus another 1,000 admitted through recognition of international certifications such as SAT, ACT, GRE, and GMAT.

**Available places for academic year 2024/2025:**

## Tuition Fees and Financial Aid

The cost of education at the Polytechnic University of Turin is structured to ensure equity and accessibility, with a system of benefits based on family income measured through the ISEE indicator.

### Fee Structure

Student contributions consist of two installments:

1. **First installment**: comprehensive contribution varying according to income bracket and type of enrollment (full-time or part-time)
2. **Second installment**: includes the regional fee for EDISU right to study (€140), stamp duty (€16), and accident insurance (approximately €5), for a total of **161 euros**

For first-year students, the maximum comprehensive contribution is approximately **2,601 euros per year**, but most students pay lower amounts thanks to the benefits provided.

### No-Tax Area

Students with an ISEE equal to or below **30,000 euros** fall into the so-called "no-tax area" and are exempt from paying university fees, only having to pay the regional tax, stamp duty, and insurance. For students with ISEE above 30,000 euros, contributions increase progressively up to the maximum for those who do not submit ISEE or have high income.

### Scholarships and Benefits

The Politecnico provides numerous scholarships and financial benefits for deserving students and those in economic hardship:

- **EDISU scholarships** (Regional Body for the Right to University Study): financial support, dining services, housing, and study spaces
- **Paid part-time collaborations** for students
- **Graduation prizes**
- **Scholarships funded by companies and private foundations** (Intergea, Intesa Sanpaolo Onlus, other industrial entities)
- **International mobility grants** (Erasmus+)
- **Benefits for educational material purchases**
- **Total exemptions** for foreign students receiving Italian Government scholarships and for students with disabilities or DSA certification

### Cost of Living in Turin

Regarding the cost of living in Turin, students should budget between **600 and 900 euros per month** for food, housing, transportation, books, and social life. Adding university tuition and cost of living expenses, a first-year student can expect to spend on average between **10,000 and 12,000 euros per year**, depending on their economic conditions and lifestyle.

## Student Services and University Life

The Polytechnic University of Turin pays great attention to student well-being and support, offering a wide range of services designed to facilitate the university experience.

### Main Services

- **University Language Center (CLA)**: language courses and internationally recognized certifications (IELTS for English, PLIDA for Italian)
- **Career Center**: support in career guidance and job opportunity search
- **Tutoring and mentoring system** for freshmen through the "buddy program"

### University Residences

Regarding housing, students can choose from various university residence solutions. The Politecnico collaborates with EDISU for the allocation of places in university residences to low-income and academically meritorious students.

#### Camplus Bernini
Located near the main campus and Porta Susa Station, with 7,000 sqm organized in single and double rooms with private bathrooms, study rooms, gym, and relaxation areas.

#### Carlo Mollino Residence
On Corso Peschiera, the result of collaboration between Camplus and the Politecnico, featuring advanced technology and attention to energy saving, with 105 beds and a 5-a-side football field on the roof.

#### Renato Einaudi University College
With various sections including the Crocetta Section near the Politecnico's main campus and equipped with a sports campus housing CUS Torino.

#### Artigianelli Residence
Offers single and double rooms with private bathrooms, Wi-Fi included, 120 sqm of study room and 24-hour reception, with prices starting from **545 euros per month** for doubles and **680 euros** for singles, all included.

### University Life in Turin

University life in Turin is rich in cultural and social opportunities:

- **Student associations** and CUS (University Sports Center) sports groups
- **Cultural events** and initiatives promoted by the university and the city
- **Museums, theaters, art house cinemas, bookstores, historic cafes**
- **International festivals**: Turin International Book Fair and Portici di Carta
- **Urban parks**: Valentino Park overlooking the Po River becomes a gathering and outdoor study spot

The university area around the Politecnico, between the **Crocetta** and **San Salvario** neighborhoods, is animated by venues, bars, and meeting points frequented by students. **Piazza Vittorio Veneto**, one of the largest arcaded squares in Europe, and the **Murazzi** along the Po River are iconic places of Turin's student nightlife.

## Scientific Research and Excellence Laboratories

The Polytechnic University of Turin is recognized as a "Research University," with a strong orientation toward applied research and technological innovation. Research activities are organized by the 11 departments and focus on four main areas:

1. Civil and Environmental Engineering
2. Architecture and Industrial Design
3. Industrial Engineering, Management, and Mathematics for Engineering
4. Information Technologies

### Laboratories

The university has over **100 experimental laboratories** dedicated to education, advanced research, and third-party testing:

- **DISAT Department**: over 80 laboratories at the Turin headquarters and 15 at the Alessandria site, specialized in chemistry, physics, chemical engineering, and materials engineering
- **Comfort Lab**: center of excellence focused on research and development of advanced materials for clothing and sports equipment, with collaborations with Ferrino, Sparco, and Scarpa
- **Center for Sustainable Future Technologies (CSFT)**: developed in collaboration with the Italian Institute of Technology (IIT), dedicated to developing sustainable technologies for hydrogen production and storage, CO2 capture, and offshore renewable energies. The laboratories occupy approximately 5,000 square meters

### Industry Collaborations

The Politecnico actively collaborates with major industrial entities and national and international research institutes. Each year the university signs approximately **800 collaboration contracts** with industries, public and private institutions.

Historic industrial partners:
- **Stellantis** (formerly FCA): contributes 30% of the teaching activities of the Automotive Engineering course
- **Leonardo SpA** and **Avio Aero**: projects in the aerospace and defense sectors
- Numerous companies from the Piedmontese and national production fabric

## Internationalization and Erasmus+ Program

Internationalization represents a fundamental pillar of the Polytechnic University of Turin's strategy, which has promoted student mobility and collaborations with universities worldwide for decades.

### International Network

The university collaborates with a vast international network that includes:
- **338 Erasmus+ agreements** with program member countries
- **74 agreements** with non-European countries
- Numerous partnerships for **double degree** programs

### Student Mobility

Each year approximately **1,000 students** from the Politecnico participate in international mobility programs for study or internship through Erasmus+ and bilateral agreements. The most popular destinations include France, Spain, Germany, and Latin American countries.

### International Students

The Politecnico welcomes approximately **5,900 international students** each year, equal to **16% of total enrollment**, one of the highest percentages in Italy. The university offers extensive support services:

- Housing assistance
- Administrative procedures (visas, residence permits, health insurance)
- Language mediators
- Welcome activities

### Mobility Requirements

Erasmus+ mobility programs provide study periods of minimum **2 continuous months** (60 days) and maximum **12 months**, with the possibility of obtaining financial contributions to support the costs of staying abroad.
