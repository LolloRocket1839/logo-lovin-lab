# IMU 2026 on Rental Properties in Turin: Investor Guide

> **TL;DR:** IMU on rental properties in Turin remains unchanged for 2026. The standard rate for second homes is 10.6‰. For regulated contracts (canone concordato), an automatic 25% national reduction applies, bringing the effective rate to 7.95‰. For canone concordato with a resident tenant, Turin applies a preferential rate of 5.75‰ × 0.75 = 4.31‰ effective. The difference between 10.6‰ (free market) and 4.31‰ (regulated with resident) can be worth €700–1,200/year on a typical two-room flat.

---

IMU (Imposta Municipale Propria) is Italy's main property tax. For rental market investors, it is one of the highest-impact cost items, but also the one with the greatest optimization potential. Turin offers one of Italy's most favorable IMU structures for landlords using regulated contracts.

## The 2026 IMU structure: what hasn't changed

The 2026 Budget Law (L. no. 199/2025) did not modify the IMU framework. The reference legislation remains L. 160/2019, which established the current IMU regime. The national base rates are:

- **Primary residence** (ordinary categories): exempt
- **Second homes / rental properties**: base rate 8.6‰, maximum municipal rate 11.4‰

## Turin's IMU rates (2025, confirmed for 2026)

Turin sets its own IMU rates annually. The 2026 rates are expected to mirror 2025, as municipalities rarely change their deliberations year over year.

| Property/contract type | Turin deliberated rate | National reduction | Final effective rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Second home / free market (4+4) | 10.6‰ | — | 10.6‰ |
| Canone concordato — non-resident or transitional tenant | 10.6‰ | −25% (national) | 7.95‰ |
| Canone concordato — resident tenant + student contract | 5.75‰ | −25% (national) | 4.31‰ |
| Social housing (ATC) | 6.0‰ | — | 6.0‰ |

**Important:** the 25% reduction for canone concordato is automatic and applies nationally (art. 1, para. 760, L. 160/2019). It triggers regardless of municipal deliberations on all contracts under art. 2 para. 3, art. 5 para. 1, and art. 5 paras. 2–3 of L. 431/1998.

## How to calculate IMU: the formula

IMU follows this formula:

**IMU = Cadastral Income × Revaluation Coefficient × Category Multiplier × Deliberated Rate × (months owned / 12)**

For residential properties (category A, excluding A/10):
- Cadastral income revaluation: +5% (coefficient 1.05)
- Category A multiplier: 160

Therefore: **IMU = Cadastral Income × 1.05 × 160 × rate**

### Practical example: two-room flat in San Salvario

Property: 55 m² two-room flat, cat. A/3, cadastral income €450/year, Turin.

**IMU tax base:** €450 × 1.05 × 160 = €75,600

| Contract type | Effective rate | Annual IMU |
|---|---|---|
| Free market 4+4 | 10.6‰ | €802/year |
| Canone concordato (non-resident tenant) | 7.95‰ | €601/year |
| Canone concordato (resident tenant) | 4.31‰ | €326/year |

Annual difference between free market and canone concordato with resident tenant: **€476/year**. Over a 10-year investment horizon, this adds up to approximately €4,760 in net savings.

## Compliance: how to notify the municipality

To benefit from Turin's preferential IMU rate for canone concordato with a resident tenant, the owner must:

1. **Register the contract** with the Agenzia delle Entrate within 30 days of signing
2. **Submit the intended use declaration** to Turin municipality via the TorinoFacile portal by 30 April of the following year
3. **Keep copies** of the registered contract and attestazione di conformità (bollinatura) for potential audits

Without notifying the municipality, the IMU software applies the standard rate (10.6‰) instead of the preferential one.

## IMU 2026 deadlines

| Deadline | Payment | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 16 June 2026 | IMU advance | 50% of IMU calculated with previous year's rates |
| 16 December 2026 | IMU balance | Adjustment with 2026 deliberated rates |

Payment via F24 form (tax code 3918 for non-primary residence properties) at any bank, post office, or via the Agenzia delle Entrate online portal.

## Other available IMU reductions

- **50% reduction** for: properties on free loan to first-degree relatives, historic or artistic properties (D.Lgs. 42/2004), properties declared uninhabitable
- **25% reduction** for canone concordato (automatic)
- **Exemption** for primary residence (ordinary categories)

## IMU and cedolare secca: how they interact

A common misconception: cedolare secca does **not** replace IMU. The two taxes are independent:

- **Cedolare secca** replaces IRPEF, surtaxes, registration tax, and stamp duty on the contract
- **IMU** is a property tax on ownership, due regardless of the rental income tax regime

IMU reductions for canone concordato apply whether you use cedolare secca or ordinary IRPEF.

## Overall tax optimization: the big picture

For a student housing investor in Turin, the optimal structure that minimizes the total tax burden is:

1. **Contract:** university student transitional contract (canone concordato, L. 431/1998 art. 5 para. 2)
2. **Cedolare secca:** 10%
3. **IMU:** Turin rate 5.75‰ × national reduction 0.75 = 4.31‰ effective
4. **Renovation bonus:** 50% (primary residence) or 36% (second homes)

The only additional complexity vs free-market contracts is obtaining the bollinatura (€40–80 one-time) and notifying the municipality by April.
