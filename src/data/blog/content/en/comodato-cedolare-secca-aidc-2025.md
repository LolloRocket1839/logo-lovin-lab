# Comodato and cedolare secca: why it fails for long-term leases (and what the 2025 AIDC challenge changes)

Can a comodatario use cedolare secca? The short answer: only for short-term leases (≤30 days). For ordinary contracts (4+4, 3+2, student leases) the structure is legally unviable. But the AIDC Norma 233/2025 has opened a doctrinal crack worth analysing.

This article explains why the "società semplice → free comodato to partner → ordinary lease with cedolare secca" scheme does not work under current law. And why the debate is not closed.

---

## Art. 3 D.Lgs. 23/2011: the bright line at "diritto reale"

The foundational provision is unambiguous. Art. 3, paragraph 1, reserves the [cedolare secca](/blog/cedolare-secca-2026-investitori) option to "the owner or holder of a real property right (diritto reale di godimento) over residential units let for residential use."

The qualifying real property rights are a closed list under Italian civil law: usufruct, use, habitation, surface rights, emphyteusis, and easements. These are rights *in rem*, opposable *erga omnes*, that transfer legal possession of the property.

Comodato is instead a personal right of enjoyment (Art. 1803 Civil Code). It creates only an obligatory relationship between the parties. The comodatario receives mere *detenzione* (physical holding), not *possesso* (legal possession). Cassazione ruling 5588 of 2 March 2021 confirmed this clearly: comodato produces "obligatory effects" only, never "real effects."

### Direct consequence

Art. 26 TUIR attributes property income (redditi fondiari) to those who "possess properties as owners, emphyteuta, usufructuary, or other real right holder." The comodatario is not among them.

AE Resolutions 381/E and 394/E of 14 October 2008 established that when a comodatario rents the property on a standard lease, rental income is attributed to the comodante (the owner). The comodatario is fiscally "transparent."

---

## The Agenzia delle Entrate explicitly said no

The question was posed directly in Parliamentary Question 5-05182 of 27 July 2011. The Agenzia answered negatively: cedolare secca is available "only for natural persons who possess properties as owners or holders of real rights of enjoyment."

Comodato is a "personal right of enjoyment that grants holding of the property, but not its possession." Conclusion: the comodatario is barred from accessing the regime.

Circular 26/E of 1 June 2011 reinforced this by also excluding sublets from cedolare secca. The related income is classified as redditi diversi (Art. 67, paragraph 1, letter h, TUIR), not property income.

---

## Art. 4 D.L. 50/2017: the exception strictly for short-term leases

The 2017 provision is the cornerstone that enables the short-term workaround and, by implication, blocks the long-term one. Art. 4, paragraph 3, extends the provisions to "contracts for consideration concluded by the comodatario having as object the enjoyment of the property by third parties."

But paragraph 1 defines the conditions: contracts not exceeding 30 days, for residential use, between natural persons, outside business activity.

The Agenzia itself explains: "The novelty of D.L. 50/2017 lies in the possibility of exercising the cedolare secca option for income from sublet contracts and comodato contracts, if of duration not exceeding 30 days."

The explicit limitation to ≤30 days creates an irrefutable *argumentum a contrario*. If the legislature had wanted comodatari to access cedolare secca on ordinary leases, it would have said so. Circular 24/E of 12 October 2017 confirms the scope: the comodatario's eligibility exists within and only within the short-term lease framework.

---

## AIDC Norma 233/2025: a theoretical but dangerous door

On 6 November 2025, the Associazione Italiana Dottori Commercialisti (AIDC) published Norma di Comportamento 233, titled "Lease of building by the holder under lease or comodato."

### The AIDC thesis

The AIDC argues that the principle established by D.L. 50/2017 for short-term leases is not exceptional but systemic. When a comodatario lets the property "for any duration," the resulting income is redditi diversi (Art. 67, paragraph 1, letter h, TUIR) taxable in the hands of the comodatario. The comodatario can then opt for cedolare secca.

According to the AIDC, "there are no reasons of systemic interpretation or legislative provisions that could justify a different income attribution" based on contract duration.

### Doctrinal reactions

Reactions have been sharply divided. Commercialista Telematico criticised the position in November 2025, warning that "it would suffice to grant a property in free comodato to a person with no other income to shift taxation of the rent to a less wealthy taxpayer."

The article characterised D.L. 50/2017 as an "exceptional and sectoral provision" introduced for "simplification and traceability" that "cannot serve as a general rule."

### Three critical limitations

1. AIDC Norms have no binding legal force. They represent the professional association's doctrinal opinion, not law.
2. The Agenzia delle Entrate has not adopted this interpretation for long-term leases. Established practice remains firmly against it.
3. No court ruling or interpello supports cedolare secca for a comodatario on ordinary leases.

Adopting this position would almost certainly trigger a tax assessment and require litigation with no favourable precedent.

---

## Why the società semplice structure fails for ordinary leases

The proposed scheme works like this: the SS owns the property → free comodato to a natural-person partner → the partner signs a 4+4 or 3+2 lease → the partner opts for cedolare secca.

This structure fails on multiple fronts:

1. **The SS cannot opt for cedolare secca** because it is not a natural person
2. **The partner/comodatario does not hold a real right**, so Art. 3 D.Lgs. 23/2011 excludes them
3. **For long-term leases, property income belongs to the comodante** (the SS) per Resolutions 381/E and 394/E/2008
4. **The D.L. 50/2017 exception applies only to contracts ≤30 days**

Il Sole 24 Ore examined this exact structure and concluded: "Società semplici would not be admitted to the cedolare secca regime for ordinary residential leases."

### The structure that works

SS → comodato to partner → partner signs a short-term lease (≤30 days) → partner opts for cedolare secca at 21% (first property) or 26% (second property onwards). The partner declares income as redditi diversi in Quadro RL. The SS declares only the cadastral income as property income.

---

## Sublet and comodato: identical treatment

There is no meaningful difference between a subletter and a comodatario for cedolare secca purposes. Both produce redditi diversi (Art. 67, paragraph 1, letter h, TUIR). Both were excluded from cedolare secca before 2017. Both gained access through Art. 4, paragraph 3, D.L. 50/2017, but only for short-term leases.

The AIDC Norma 233/2025 treats both identically, arguing both should have access regardless of contract duration.

---

## What remains for long-term leases

Without the cedolare secca workaround, the tax treatment for ordinary leases through a società semplice is significantly more burdensome. Rental income is classified as property income (Arts. 25-43 TUIR).

The taxable base is the greater of the contractual rent (reduced by 5%) and the revalued cadastral income (+5%). For agreed-rent contracts (3+2), a further 30% reduction applies.

Income is attributed to each partner in proportion to their profit-sharing quota (Art. 5 TUIR, fiscal transparency). Each partner is taxed at their marginal IRPEF rate (23-43%) plus regional and municipal surcharges. The effective rate can exceed 47%.

The gap is substantial: cedolare secca at 21% (or 10% for agreed rent) versus IRPEF up to 43% plus registration tax. This explains the strong incentive to explore workarounds, but the current legal architecture does not permit the comodato structure for long-term leases.

---

## Conclusion

The legal landscape is clear in its current state but contains a seed of potential evolution. Under all established sources, the comodatario cannot exercise cedolare secca on leases exceeding 30 days.

The AIDC Norma 233/2025 has introduced an intellectually coherent but practically untested argument. No binding authority supports it and the abuse potential has drawn serious criticism.

The actionable options are: use short-term leases (≤30 days) through the comodato structure to access cedolare secca, accept IRPEF taxation via fiscal transparency on long-term leases, or explore alternative structures such as transferring real rights (e.g., usufruct) to individual partners.

The AIDC position is worth monitoring but should not be relied upon for current tax planning without a very high risk tolerance.
