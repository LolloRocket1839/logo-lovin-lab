# Turin at Night: Safety Guide for Students {#intro}

Turin has a surprisingly vibrant nightlife. Five distinct zones, hourly night buses, and aperitivos that last well past midnight. But you need to know where to go and what to avoid.

This guide covers everything: nightlife zones, night transport, areas to avoid, and emergency contacts. Written by people who live here.

---

## The 5 nightlife zones of Turin {#nightlife-zones}

### San Salvario

The absolute epicenter. Largo Saluzzo, Via Baretti, and surroundings. Democratic and under-30. Aperitivos from 7 PM, clubs filling after 1 AM, closing at 4-4:30 AM.

### Quadrilatero Romano

Between Piazza Statuto and Piazza Castello. Wine bars, cocktail bars, and live music in a historic setting. Closing around 3 AM. More sophisticated atmosphere.

### Piazza Vittorio Veneto

The central hub. One of the largest squares in Europe. Convergence point for all Night Buster night buses. Perfect to start or end your evening.

### Murazzi del Po

The summer hotspot. Concerts and DJ sets along the river. Very active from June to September.

### Vanchiglia

Piazza Santa Giulia and Campus Luigi Einaudi. The alternative, university scene. More intimate, cheaper, more "local."

### Typical timing

Aperitivo/apericena runs from 7 to 9 PM. Clubs open around 11 PM. They fill up after 1 AM. Close between 3:30 and 5 AM. Peak days: Thursday, Friday, Saturday. Pre-sale entry: €10-20 (usually includes one drink).

## Rules and nighttime regulations {#regulations}

Since March 2025, there is a **permanent ban** on takeaway alcohol sales after 9 PM in 20 specific zones. These include San Salvario, Vanchiglia, Piazza Vittorio, and Piazza Santa Giulia.

Fines range from €50 to €300.

The ban targets minimarkets and takeaway sales. Bars and restaurants can still serve alcohol at tables and counters.

In 2025: 545+ Municipal Police interventions in nightlife zones, with 222 fines issued.

## Night transport: Night Buster and metro {#transport}

### Metro M1

| Day | Last run |
|-----|----------|
| Tuesday-Thursday | 12:00 AM |
| Friday-Saturday | 1:00 AM |

Times change frequently due to CBTC upgrade works (completion expected spring 2026). A replacement bus M1S covers the route when the metro is closed.

### Night Buster (GTT night buses)

They run every Friday, Saturday, and eve of holidays. Central hub: Piazza Vittorio Veneto.

- Departures from suburban terminals: from midnight (last at 4 AM)
- From Piazza Vittorio: from 1 AM (last at 5 AM)
- Frequency: every 60 minutes
- Ticket: €2.00 (same as daytime, no surcharge)

## Areas to avoid at night {#areas-avoid}

### Barriera di Milano
Most frequently cited as dangerous. Widespread dealing and crime. Active red zone.

### Porta Palazzo
Lively market by day. Dealing and crime at night. Active red zone.

### Via Nizza arcades / Porta Nuova Station
Pickpocketing and dealing. Giardino Sambuy unsafe at night.

### Aurora / Valdocco / Lungo Dora
Oscillating between gentrification and decay. Active red zone.

### Le Vallette / Falchera / Rebaudengo
Isolated suburbs. Dealing near Parco Stura.

### San Salvario (late night)
Under the Via Nizza arcades and around Largo Saluzzo after 3 AM.

### Piazza Santa Giulia / Piazza Bengasi
Added to red zones in March 2025.

### Parco del Valentino
Avoid after closing time. Especially isolated paths.

## Safety: services, apps, and contacts {#safety}

### 112 Where ARE U App

The essential tool. Automatically sends your GPS location to 112. Has a "silent call" function for emergencies when you can't speak. Multilingual. Works across all Italian regions.

### Active safety services

- **Presenza Amica** at Porta Nuova: Mon-Fri 7:30-11 PM (tel. 327.3275692)
- **Anti-violence offices UniTo:** 4 campus locations (Telefono Rosa and E.M.M.A. ONLUS)
- **Anti-violence Center:** Via San Marino 22/A (tel. 011.011.31500)
- **ASL Città di Torino:** 011-530666
- **Helpline 1522:** 24/7, free, multilingual (12 languages)
