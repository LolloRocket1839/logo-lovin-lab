# Real Student Housing Yields Turin 2026 {#intro}

Student housing in Turin outperforms every other residential segment. The numbers are clear: 9-13% gross yields with room-by-room rentals, compared to 4.8-7.7% for traditional rentals.

This guide analyzes real yields zone by zone, the gross-to-net calculation, near-zero vacancy, and how Turin compares to other Italian university cities.

## Regulated vs. free market: regulated wins {#regulated-vs-free}

Regulated rent with 10% flat tax generates a net income nearly identical to free-market rent with 21% flat tax. Despite a ~15% lower rent.

In Turin the gap between free market and regulated is small. Regulated rent offers lower fiscal risk, greater tenant attractiveness, and significantly lower IMU.

The comparative simulation shows both around 5.4% net. Regulated is slightly favored when lower vacancy and higher demand are considered.

## Near-zero vacancy {#vacancy}

Turin has ~50,000 empty apartments (15% of total). But student housing has demand that far exceeds supply.

Key figures:
- ~40,000 non-resident students seeking housing
- Supply covers only 20-30% of demand
- 22% of Turin rental contracts are for students (highest share among Italy's large cities)
- Estimated average vacancy: 0.5-1 month/year for well-located apartments
- Near-full occupancy from September to June in prime zones

Turin represents 9.1% of national room demand (up from 8.8% in 2024). Third hub after Milan and Rome.

## Renovation bonus and 10-year projection {#projection}

From 2025 the renovation bonus for second homes is 36% (max €96,000), split into 10 annual installments. On a €40,000 renovation, the deduction is €14,400 = €1,440/year for 10 years. This adds roughly 0.8-1.2 percentage points to annual net yield.

### 10-year projection: 3-room San Salvario

- Initial value: €195,000
- Regime: regulated + flat tax 10%
- Appreciation: 2.5%/year

### Main risks

- Price stagnation (Turin has historical flat periods)
- Increase in institutional student housing (28,000 new beds in Italy by 2027)
- Regulatory changes
