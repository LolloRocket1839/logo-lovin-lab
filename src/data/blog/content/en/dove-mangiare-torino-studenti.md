# Where to eat in Turin: the definitive guide for students on a budget

*Updated: November 2025 | Reading time: 12 minutes*

Living in Turin as a student means facing a daily question: where do I eat today without going broke? Between university canteens, markets, kebab shops, and ethnic restaurants, there are many options. But which ones are truly affordable? Where can you eat well while spending little?

In this guide, I'll explain exactly where to eat in Turin based on your budget, organized by type of meal. All places have been tested, all prices are updated to November 2025, and everything is written by students who live here.

## Realistic budget: how much you need to eat in Turin {#budget}

First of all, let's clarify the numbers.

### Monthly food budget

**Super-careful student (€150-200/month):**
- Shop at markets
- Cook almost always
- Eat out 1-2 times/week
- Aperitivo 1-2 times/month

**Average student (€200-280/month):**
- Mix market/supermarket
- Cook often, eat out when short on time
- Lunch out 2-3 times/week
- Aperitivo 2-3 times/month

**Comfortable student (€280-400/month):**
- Shop at supermarket
- Eat out often (5-8 times/week)
- Aperitivo 1-2 times/week
- Occasionally more expensive restaurants

### Average cost per meal

| Type of meal | DIY | Budget out | Medium out |
|--------------|-----|------------|------------|
| **Breakfast** | €0.50-1 | €2-3 | €3.50-5 |
| **Lunch** | €2-3 | €4-8 | €10-15 |
| **Dinner** | €3-4 | €5-12 | €15-25 |
| **Aperitivo** | - | €7-10 | €12-18 |

## Breakfast: where and how much {#colazione}

Breakfast is where you can save the most (or spend unnecessarily).

### Option 1: at home (€0.50-1.50)

**The most economical:**
- Bread + jam: €0.40
- Moka coffee: €0.10
- Milk + cereal: €0.80
- Rusks + nutella: €0.60

**Where to buy:**
- **Lidl/MD:** The cheapest (cookies €1.20, coffee €3/pack)
- **Esselunga:** Better quality but +30% prices

### Option 2: University bar (€2-3)

Almost all universities have internal bars with controlled prices:

**Bar Palazzo Nuovo (UniTO):**
- Brioche + coffee: €2.20
- Toast + cappuccino: €3

**Bar Politecnico:**
- Croissant + coffee: €2.50
- Focaccia + juice: €2.80

**Bar Campus Einaudi:**
- Croissant + coffee: €2.30
- Sandwich + orange juice: €3.50

### Option 3: Regular bar (€3-5)

If you want a "real" breakfast at the bar:

**The best for value:**

**Caffè Mulassano** (Centro)
- Brioche + cappuccino: €3.80
- Elegant atmosphere

**Fiorio** (Centro, Via Po)
- Brioche + coffee: €4
- Historic café since 1780
- Famous for gelato and pastries
- Elegant and historic atmosphere

**Bar Norman** (Centro, Via Pietro Micca)
- Large artisan brioches
- Brioche + coffee: €3
- Historic café since 1918

**Caffè Basile** (Cenisia)
- Near Politecnico
- Croissant + cappuccino: €2.80
- Cozy, free wifi

**Pasticceria Abrate** (San Salvario)
- Excellent dry pastries
- Coffee + pastries: €3.50
- Perfect for "important" breakfast

**Orso Caffè** (San Salvario)
- Quality specialty coffee
- Brioche + coffee: €3.50
- Modern and cozy atmosphere
- Perfect for students who love quality coffee

**Maggiora** (Crimea)
- Historic Turin pastry shop
- Brioche + cappuccino: €3.20
- Excellent artisan sweets
- Classic and elegant atmosphere

**💡 Tip:** Have breakfast at home on normal days (€0.50), bar when you have a free first hour or want to study out (€2.50-3). Savings: €40-60/month.

## Markets: where to shop smart {#mercati}

Markets are the secret to eating well while spending little. Fruit and vegetables cost 40-60% less than supermarkets.

### Main markets

**Porta Palazzo** (Aurora)
- **When:** Monday-Friday 7:30-14:00, Saturday until 19:00
- **What:** The largest market in Europe
- **Prices:** The lowest ever
- **Tip:** Go after 13:00 for discounts up to 50%
- **Verdict:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Unmissable for savings

**Piazza Madama Cristina** (San Salvario)
- **When:** Wednesday and Saturday morning
- **What:** Fruit, vegetables, clothes
- **Prices:** Very economical
- **Tip:** Most convenient for students in San Salvario
- **Verdict:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Perfect for students

**Crocetta** (Via Nizza)
- **When:** Tuesday and Friday morning
- **What:** Fruit, vegetables, flowers
- **Prices:** Slightly higher but excellent quality
- **Verdict:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Superior quality

### Price examples (market vs supermarket comparison)

| Product | Market | Supermarket | Savings |
|---------|--------|-------------|---------|
| Tomatoes (1kg) | €1.50 | €2.50-3 | 40-50% |
| Bananas (1kg) | €1 | €1.80 | 45% |
| Lettuce | €0.50-1 | €1.50 | 50-65% |
| Peppers (1kg) | €1.50 | €3 | 50% |
| Apples (1kg) | €1.50 | €2.50 | 40% |

**Monthly savings:** €40-80/month if you buy fruit and vegetables at the market

## University canteens: how they work {#mense}

EDISU canteens are reserved for students with low ISEE, but anyone can access by paying full price.

### Rates (2024-2025)

**With ISEE:**
- Band 1 (ISEE < €13,000): €2.60
- Band 2 (ISEE €13,000-€20,000): €3.30
- Band 3 (ISEE €20,000-€25,000): €4.60
- Band 4 (ISEE > €25,000): €5.50

**Without ISEE/out of state without application:**
- Full price: €6.50

### How it works

1. **Request EDISU Card** online on the EDISU website
2. **Load credits** online or at canteens
3. **Choose the menu** (first + second + side + bread + fruit)
4. **Pay with the card**

### Where the canteens are

**Palazzo Nuovo** (UniTO Centro)
- Hours: 11:45-14:30 and 18:45-20:30
- Capacity: 300 seats
- **Tip:** Go before 12:30 or after 13:30 to avoid queues

**Campus Einaudi**
- Hours: 12:00-14:30 and 19:00-21:00
- Modern, spacious
- More varied menu

**Lungo Dora Siena** (near CLE)
- Hours: 12:00-14:30
- Smaller, less crowded

**Venaria Reale** (for Politecnico/Agraria students)
- Hours: 11:45-14:30

### Is it worth it?

**YES if:**
- You have low ISEE (you pay €2.60-4.60)
- You have little time between classes
- You don't feel like cooking

**NO if:**
- You pay full price (€6.50) - better a kebab or pizza
- You have time and enjoy cooking
- You don't like canteen food (variable quality)

## The 10 commandments of the student who eats well {#comandamenti}

### 1. Use the market for fruit and vegetables
**Savings:** €40-80/month
- Bring a reusable bag
- Go towards the end of the market for discounts
- Bring cash (many don't accept cards)

### 2. Batch cook 1-2 times/week
**Savings:** €80-120/month
- Prepare 4-6 portions of pasta/rice/curry
- Freeze in single containers
- Defrost the night before

### 3. Use aperitivo smartly
**Strategy:** Go early (18:30-19:00), fresh and abundant buffet = full dinner for €7-9

### 4. Compare discount stores
- **MD/Lidl:** Save 30-40% vs Esselunga
- **In & In:** Low prices, ok quality
- **Eurospin:** Great for pasta, preserves

### 5. Apps and offers
- **Too Good To Go:** Unsold food at -70%
- **TheFork:** Restaurants with 20-50% discounts
- **Supermarket flyers:** Plan based on offers

### 6. Breakfast at home
**Savings:** €40-60/month compared to daily bar

### 7. Bring lunch from home 2-3 times/week
**Savings:** €60-100/month
- Dinner leftovers
- Cold pasta with vegetables
- Prepared sandwiches

### 8. Use "end of day" supermarkets
- After 19:00, fresh products discounted 30-50%
- Meat, fish, bakery

### 9. Share purchases
- Buy family sizes with roommates
- Split the cost of oil, salt, spices
- **Savings:** €20-30/month

### 10. One "luxury" per week
- Nice restaurant budget: €15-25
- Keeps motivation high
- Really enjoy the experience
