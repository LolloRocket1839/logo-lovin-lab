# Turin in December: Christmas lights, hot chocolate and Baroque magic

December turns Turin into one of Europe's most captivating winter destinations. The city's Baroque architecture provides a dramatic backdrop for contemporary light installations, while centuries-old food traditions fill the Christmas markets. For a broader view of when to visit, see our [guide to Turin in every season](/blog/torino-ogni-stagione-turisti). As the former capital of the Savoy dynasty and unified Italy's first capital, Turin has always combined architectural grandeur with cultural ambition. December 2025 offers a particularly rich program of events.

## Luci d'Artista: 28 years of light art in public spaces

Since 1998, **Luci d'Artista** has replaced traditional Christmas decorations with works by internationally recognized artists. The 2025 edition runs from October 24 through January 11, 2026, with 32 installations spread across the city center.

The festival's premise is simple: contemporary art belongs in public spaces, not just museums. Residents and visitors encounter world-class installations while walking to work or grabbing a coffee. Urban planners across Europe have studied the initiative as a model for democratizing art access.

This year's edition features four new works. **Tracey Emin's "Sex and Solitude"**, a neon piece donated by Fondazione Arte CRT, will be displayed in the Royal Gardens. The installation explores Emin's signature themes of intimacy and vulnerability through hand-lettered text. She has said that neon creates an emotional response: "Neon and argon gases make us feel something positive, that's why we find them in funfairs, casinos, and bars."

At **OGR Torino**, **"Mummer Love"** brings together Soundwalk Collective, Patti Smith, and Philip Glass in a multisensory tribute to Rimbaud. Visitors walk through the East Courtyard while ambient recordings from Rimbaud's house in Harar, Ethiopia blend with Sufi chants, Glass's piano, and Smith reciting from *Illuminations*.

## Piazza Castello: the heart of December celebrations

Piazza Castello sits at the center of Turin's festive program. This 40,000-square-meter square has served as the city's core since Roman times, when the cardo and decumanus intersected here. The current Baroque layout dates to architects Ascanio Vittozzi and Filippo Juvarra, who shaped the space for the House of Savoy in the 17th and 18th centuries.

In December 2025, the **Mercatino di Natale** (Christmas Market) fills the piazza with wooden stalls selling handmade jewelry, carved toys, local ceramics, and Piedmontese food specialties: cheeses, truffle products, cured meats, and traditional sweets.

On December 31st, the square hosts the **"Torino Wasn't Built in a Day"** New Year's Eve concert. The lineup features **Morcheeba** (whose 2000 hit inspired the event's title), **Rose Villain** (nominated for Sanremo 2025), and **Malika Ayane** (13 platinum certifications), who also serves as the evening's presenter.

## Teatro Regio: Romeo and Juliet in December

**Teatro Regio**, facing Piazza Castello, presents **"Romeo e Giulietta"** from December 5-14. The Prague National Theater Ballet performs Prokofiev's score with John Cranko's choreography (reprised by Jane Bourne), Václav Zahradník conducting, and scenic design by Jürgen Rose.

The company includes dancers from 18 nationalities performing Prokofiev's complete score, not the concert suites. The music ranges from violent dissonance to lyrical tenderness, with folk rhythms and modernist harmonies throughout.

On December 3 at 6 PM, a conference-concert at Piccolo Regio Puccini offers background on the production.

## Xmas Comics & Games: contemporary pop culture in December

Beyond the city's traditional classical arts institutions, December 2025 also features **Xmas Comics & Games**, scheduled for December 13-14 at the Oval Lingotto Fiere venue. This event represents the contemporary pop culture dimensions of Turin's cultural programming—celebrating comics, video games, cosplay, and internet personalities (YouTubers) within a festive framework. The convention format includes artist meet-and-greets, cosplay competitions and parades, dedicated gaming areas with tournaments, and vendor exhibitions. This programming acknowledges that contemporary culture includes digital media, participatory fandom communities, and creative self-expression through costume and performance, extending Turin's December cultural offerings beyond classical arts into contemporary visual culture.

## Christmas markets and food traditions

Beyond Piazza Castello, the **Christmas Village** near Palazzo Madama includes an ice-skating rink, live music, Santa's cabin, and additional market stalls. The main skating venue is the **Palavela**, the Olympic arena from 2006.

**Gianduiotto**, Turin's hazelnut chocolate, dates to the 17th century when local artisans combined imported cocoa with Langhe hazelnuts. The distinctive gold-wrapped candies remain a symbol of Piedmontese confectionery.

For **Cenone di Capodanno** (New Year's Eve dinner), families eat **cotechino** with lentils just after midnight. The coin-shaped lentils are meant to bring prosperity in the new year.

December is also peak **white truffle** season. Restaurants serve **tajarin** (hand-cut pasta) topped with shaved *tartufo bianco d'Alba*. Street vendors sell roasted chestnuts, *vin brulé*, and **bicerin**, Turin's layered chocolate-coffee drink.

Beyond haute cuisine, December streets feature vendors selling roasted chestnuts, warm mulled wine (*vin brulé*), and **bicerin**, Turin's distinctive chocolate-coffee beverage. The bicerin—prepared with specific proportions of hot chocolate, strong coffee, and milk—represents another manifestation of Turin's chocolate-working heritage while providing the convivial warmth necessary for winter plaza exploration.

## Baroque architecture and historic context: understanding December's urban backdrop

To fully appreciate Turin's December celebration, understanding the architectural context proves essential. As former seat of the House of Savoy and first capital of unified Italy, Turin possesses one of Europe's most coherent Baroque urban ensembles. The **Royal Palace of Turin** (Palazzo Reale), redesigned by Filippo Juvarra in Baroque style and completed in 1721, remains Italy's most important residence of the Savoy dynasty. Inside, the famous **Scissors Staircase** designed by Juvarra demonstrates extraordinary architectural engineering—featuring four ramps that create an almost suspended spatial effect.

The **Church of San Lorenzo**, designed by Guarino Guarini (1624-1683), represents another supreme example of Baroque innovation. Built to commemorate Duke Emanuele Filiberto's triumph in the 1557 Battle of San Quintino, the church impresses through its curved façade and stunning interior frescoes. Its geometric dome demonstrates innovative engineering that influenced subsequent European architecture.

The **Basilica of Superga**, perched on hills overlooking Turin and completed between 1717-1731 by Filippo Juvarra, stands as one of Baroque's supreme achievements. The basilica's majestic dome and grand colonnades create a silhouette visible from throughout the city, and the structure itself served as model for the Panthéon in Paris. The basilica carries tragic historical weight as the site of the 1949 disaster claiming the Torino football team when their plane crashed into the building's rear wall.

During December, Luci d'Artista's contemporary light installations dialogue with these Baroque structures, creating visual conversations across centuries. The tension between Baroque ornamentation and contemporary minimalist light art, between historical monumentality and contemporary artistic practice, generates the unique character of Turin's December experience. The historic architecture provides the physical stage upon which contemporary art installations perform, creating what amounts to an extended meditation on continuity and change, tradition and innovation.

## Conclusion: Turin's December as synthesis of history, art, and celebration

Turin's December 2025 offers far more than seasonal tourism appeal; it represents a comprehensive expression of European urban culture that seamlessly integrates centuries of history with contemporary artistic practice, classical traditions with pop culture, and formal civic celebration with intimate seasonal traditions. The city acknowledges its status as a historic royal capital while embracing its contemporary identity as a center for artistic innovation and cultural experimentation.

For those interested in urban culture, architectural history, contemporary art, classical performing arts, pop culture, culinary traditions, and festive celebration, December provides an extraordinary opportunity to experience a European city functioning at the full range of its cultural capacities. Turin does not present December as a single-themed tourist experience; rather, it offers simultaneous parallel programming across multiple registers and aesthetic registers, allowing visitors to craft their own experiential combinations according to personal interests and preferences. The city's commitment to making contemporary art accessible through public space, combined with its preservation of centuries-old traditions, its hosting of world-class classical performances, and its embrace of contemporary pop culture, establishes December as an ideal season to understand what makes Turin one of Europe's most distinctive and sophisticated cities.
