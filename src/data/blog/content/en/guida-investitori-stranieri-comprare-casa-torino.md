# The foreign investor's complete guide to buying property in Turin (2026)

*From codice fiscale to closing day: everything you need to buy, rent, and profit from real estate in Italy's highest-yield major city.*

---

Turin offers something rare in Western Europe. You can buy a residential property under €100,000 with gross rental yields of 5 to 10%. Average prices sit around €2,000/m², roughly 60% cheaper than Milan. Rental demand is driven by over 100,000 university students, growing tourism, and a diversifying economy.

This guide walks you through every step of buying property in Turin as a non-Italian buyer.

---

## Can foreigners buy property in Italy?

**EU/EEA/Swiss citizens** face no restrictions whatsoever. You have identical rights to Italian nationals. No limits on properties, no residency requirements for purchasing.

**Non-EU citizens** can buy under the reciprocity principle (*condizione di reciprocità*). You can purchase if your home country allows Italians to buy under similar conditions. Most Western countries qualify: the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Australia, and Japan. Non-EU citizens with a valid *permesso di soggiorno* (residence permit) can buy freely regardless of reciprocity.

Important: buying property in Italy does **not** automatically grant you residency rights or a visa. However, Italy's *Elective Residency Visa* (for those with passive income) and various investor visa programs can provide pathways.

---

## Step 1: get your codice fiscale

The *codice fiscale* is Italy's tax identification number. It's a 16-character alphanumeric code required for every financial and legal transaction. You need one before you can open a bank account, sign a contract, pay taxes, or connect utilities. It is free.

**Three ways to get it:**

**At an Italian consulate abroad.** Submit Form AA4/8 (digital format) with a passport copy and proof of address in the consular district. Processing takes 1 to 4 weeks depending on the consulate. This is the most common route for buyers who haven't yet travelled to Italy.

**At any Agenzia delle Entrate office in Italy.** Bring your passport and completed Form AA4/8. EU citizens typically receive it the same day. Non-EU citizens can also apply through the *Sportello Unico per l'Immigrazione* or *Questura*.

**Via power of attorney.** Delegate someone in Italy to submit Form AA4/8 on your behalf. Takes 1 to 2 days.

---

## Step 2: open an Italian bank account (recommended, not required)

An Italian bank account is not strictly necessary. You can pay via international wire transfer. But it makes ongoing tax payments, utility bills, and rent collection vastly simpler.

Banks with good foreign-buyer experience include UniCredit (Italy's largest, mortgage rates from roughly 3.50% for non-residents), Intesa Sanpaolo (roughly 3.80%), and BNL (part of BNP Paribas, well suited for EU citizens). You'll need your passport, codice fiscale, and proof of address and income.

**Mortgage note:** non-resident foreigners can typically borrow up to 60 to 70% LTV (versus 80% for residents). Given Turin's low absolute prices, many investors buy in cash and avoid mortgage costs entirely. For a €75,000 apartment, you might finance €45,000 to €52,500. Consider using currency transfer specialists like Wise rather than bank-to-bank transfers for better exchange rates.

For more details, read our [complete guide to mortgages for property investors](/blog/mutui-investitori-immobiliari-guida-completa).

## Step 3: find and evaluate a property

**Where to search:** Idealista.it and Immobiliare.it are Italy's two dominant property portals. Gate-away.com caters specifically to international buyers. Local agencies (*agenzie immobiliari*) often have unlisted inventory and local market knowledge. In Turin, where every neighborhood has different dynamics, this is invaluable.

**What to evaluate as an investor:** Turin's neighborhoods produce radically different yields. Aurora and Barriera di Milano offer 7 to 10% gross at €1,100 to €1,600/m². The city centre yields 2.5 to 4.7% at €4,000+/m². The sweet spot for most investors is a bilocale (two-room apartment) of 40 to 60 m² in neighborhoods like Vanchiglia, Lingotto, Borgo Vittoria, or Pozzo Strada. These areas deliver yields of 6 to 8% with strong tenant demand.

To explore the best zones, check our [guide to real estate investing in Turin](/blog/investire-real-assets-torino-2025).

**Due diligence essentials:** hire a *geometra* (surveyor/architect) to verify structural integrity, check for unauthorized works (*abusi edilizi*), and confirm cadastral compliance. A lawyer should run a 20-year title search at the *Conservatoria dei Registri Immobiliari* and verify no liens, mortgages, or legal disputes exist.

---

## Step 4: make an offer (proposta d'acquisto)

Your written binding offer is submitted through the real estate agent. It includes the offered price, proposed timeline, and a deposit of typically €5,000 to €10,000 as *caparra*. Once the seller accepts, both parties are bound.

**Critical for mortgage buyers:** include a *clausola sospensiva* (mortgage contingency) making the offer conditional on loan approval. Without this clause, you lose your deposit if financing falls through.

---

## Step 5: sign the preliminary contract (compromesso)

The *compromesso* is the detailed binding agreement that sets all terms: price, completion date, payment schedule, and property description. You pay a deposit of 10 to 30% of the purchase price as *caparra confirmatoria*.

The legal weight of the *caparra confirmatoria* is significant. If the buyer defaults, the deposit is forfeited. If the seller defaults, the deposit must be returned doubled. This symmetry protects both parties.

The *compromesso* must be registered at the Agenzia delle Entrate within 20 days. Registration costs €200 fixed plus 0.5% of the deposit amount (credited against final transfer taxes). Optionally, the *compromesso* can be registered at the land registry (*trascrizione*) for approximately €750 to €1,500. This protects against the seller selling the same property to someone else before completion.

---

## Step 6: close the deal (rogito)

The *rogito* (also called *atto di vendita*) is the final deed, signed before a *notaio* (notary public). The *notaio* is mandatory under Italian law. There is no closing without one.

**What the notaio does:** verifies ownership and clean title, confirms no outstanding debts or liens, drafts and authenticates the deed, reads it aloud in full, collects all transfer taxes on behalf of the state, and registers the transfer at the Land Registry (*Catasto*) and *Conservatoria* within 30 days.

The *notaio* is impartial. Chosen and paid by the buyer but representing neither party. Expect fees of 1 to 2.5% of the declared value, roughly €2,000 to €2,500 plus 22% VAT for a €50,000 to €100,000 property.

**If you don't speak Italian:** a sworn interpreter (*interprete giurato*) is mandatory at the rogito. Cost: approximately €250 to €500. The interpreter translates the deed and the notaio's reading in real time.

**Payment:** the remaining balance is paid via traceable bank transfer or *assegni circolari* (banker's drafts). Cash payments are prohibited. All property transactions must be fully traceable.

---

## Buying remotely: the procura (power of attorney)

If you cannot attend the *rogito* in person, you can grant a *procura speciale* (special power of attorney) to a representative who will sign on your behalf. The procura must specifically identify the property, the price, and the terms. A generic power of attorney is not sufficient.

**For Italian citizens abroad:** execute at the Italian consulate. The Italian *notaio* drafts the text; you sign before the consular official. Cost: approximately €50 to €150.

**For non-Italian citizens:** sign before a public notary in your country, obtain a certified Italian translation, and secure an Apostille (for Hague Convention countries) or double legalization (for non-Hague countries). Total cost: approximately €350 to €750 plus translation (€500 to €800) plus apostille fees.

Some EU countries (Belgium, Denmark, France, Ireland, and Germany) have bilateral agreements that exempt documents from apostille requirements.

## Transfer taxes: what you'll pay

### Buying from a private seller (most resale properties)

Tax is calculated on *valore catastale* (cadastral value), which is typically 30 to 50% of market value. This is a major advantage for the buyer.

| Tax | Prima casa | Seconda casa |
|-----|-----------|-------------|
| Imposta di registro | 2% of cadastral value (min. €1,000) | 9% of cadastral value (min. €1,000) |
| Imposta ipotecaria | €50 | €50 |
| Imposta catastale | €50 | €50 |

### Buying from a developer (new build or recently renovated)

Tax is calculated on the actual sale price.

| Tax | Prima casa | Seconda casa |
|-----|-----------|-------------|
| IVA | 4% of sale price | 10% of sale price |
| Imposta di registro | €200 | €200 |
| Imposta ipotecaria | €200 | €200 |
| Imposta catastale | €200 | €200 |

### Prima casa eligibility for foreign buyers

To qualify for the lower *prima casa* rates, you must not already own another property purchased with *prima casa* benefits. You must establish residency in the municipality within 18 months. You must not sell within 5 years (or reinvest proceeds within 1 year). The property cannot be in a luxury category (A/1, A/8, A/9). Non-Italian foreigners generally must establish actual physical residency.

## Annual ongoing taxes

### IMU (property tax)

Primary residence is exempt (except luxury categories). All second homes and non-resident owners pay. Turin's 2025 rates: seconda casa at **1.06%** (maximum national rate). *Canone concordato* rentals receive a mandatory 25% discount (effective rate roughly 0.57%).

Calculation formula: *rendita catastale* × 1.05 × 160 × municipal rate. Payment in two installments (June 16 and December 16) via F24 form.

### TARI (waste tax)

Mandatory for all property owners and occupants. Based on property size and number of occupants. Typically **€200 to €400/year** in Turin for a 50 to 80 m² apartment.

### Income tax on rental income

**Option A: ordinary IRPEF.** Progressive rates of 23% (up to €28,000), 35% (€28,001 to €50,000), and 43% (over €50,000). From 2026, the second bracket drops to 33%. Regional and municipal surtaxes add roughly 2.5 to 4.1%. Only 95% of gross rent is taxable (5% flat deduction). But you also pay *imposta di registro* (2% of annual rent, split with the tenant) and *imposta di bollo*.

**Option B: [cedolare secca](/blog/cedolare-secca-2026-investitori) (flat tax).** 21% for standard contracts, **10% for *canone concordato*** contracts in Turin, or 26% for second-property short-term rentals. Replaces IRPEF, surtaxes, *imposta di registro*, and *imposta di bollo*. The 10% rate is almost always the most advantageous option and is available for student contracts and agreed-rent contracts in Turin.

## Complete cost breakdown: buying a €75,000 apartment in Turin

Scenario: foreign non-resident buying a resale apartment as *seconda casa* from a private seller. *Rendita catastale* estimated at €300 (typical for a small apartment). No mortgage.

| Cost item | Amount |
|-----------|--------|
| Purchase price | €75,000 |
| Imposta di registro (9% of cadastral value ~€39,690) | €3,572 |
| Imposta ipotecaria + catastale | €100 |
| Notaio (fees + VAT) | €2,000 to €2,500 |
| Agency commission (3% + 22% VAT) | €2,745 |
| Geometra (technical report) | €800 to €1,200 |
| Lawyer (optional but recommended) | €1,500 to €2,500 |
| Sworn interpreter | €250 to €350 |
| Compromesso registration | €380 to €500 |
| Bank transfer and miscellaneous fees | €150 to €500 |
| **Total additional costs** | **€11,497 to €13,967** |
| **Total all-in investment** | **€86,497 to €88,967** |
| **Additional costs as % of price** | **15.3 to 18.6%** |

If qualifying for *prima casa*: transfer taxes drop by approximately €2,572.

### Annual holding costs (if not rented)

IMU: roughly €673/year. TARI: €250 to €350/year. Condominium fees: €600 to €1,200/year. **Total: approximately €1,523 to €2,223/year.**

### Rental income scenario

Renting at €500/month (€6,000/year) under different tax regimes:

| Tax regime | Tax owed | Net income |
|------------|----------|------------|
| IRPEF ordinary (23% bracket) | ~€1,311 | €4,689 |
| Cedolare secca 21% | €1,260 | €4,740 |
| **Cedolare secca 10%** (canone concordato) | **€600** | **€5,400** |

The 10% *cedolare secca* on a *canone concordato* contract produces the highest net return. €5,400 on a €75,000 investment equals a **7.2% net cash-on-cash yield** on the purchase price alone.

## Property management options in Turin

If you're not based in Turin, professional management is essential. The main options:

**Full short-term rental management** (listing creation, guest communication, cleaning, check-in/out, Questura reporting, tourist tax collection): typically **15 to 25% of rental income**. Local providers include Wonderful Italy for vacation rentals and various specialized agencies.

**Student rental management** (tenant sourcing, contract preparation, maintenance coordination, rent collection): typically **8 to 12% of rental income** or a fixed monthly fee.

**Property caretaking** (for non-rental periods): approximately **€100 to €300/month** covering inspections, mail, minor maintenance, and utility management.

**Administrative and fiscal management** (tax filing, utility management, condominium representation): approximately **€500 to €1,500/year**.

## Why Turin specifically?

Turin stands apart from other Italian investment destinations for several reasons. Entry prices averaging €2,000/m² are approximately 60% below Milan (€5,000+/m²) and 40% below Bologna (€3,300/m²). Rental yields outperform both.

The city hosts over 100,000 university students at [Politecnico di Torino](/blog/politecnico-torino-guida-completa), [Università di Torino](/blog/universita-torino-guida-completa), and five other institutions. This creates structural demand for rental housing that is effectively recession-proof.

Turin's economy is diversifying beyond its automotive heritage into aerospace, AI, robotics, and food technology. And critically, Turin is one of only a handful of Italian cities where long-term rental yields consistently outperform short-term rental yields. Your investment doesn't depend on the volatile tourism market.

For the latest market data, read the [Savills report on student housing in Italy](/blog/student-housing-italia-savills-2025).

## Post-purchase checklist

After closing, complete these steps:

1. **Transfer utilities** (gas, electricity, water) into your name at the local providers
2. **Register for IMU** at the Comune di Torino
3. **Register for TARI** (waste tax) at the Comune
4. **Set up rent collection** (Italian bank account or property manager)
5. **File Italian tax returns** annually if generating rental income (Modello Redditi PF, deadline June 30 of the following year)
6. **If claiming prima casa:** establish residency within 18 months
7. **If renting short-term:** obtain CIN (Codice Identificativo Nazionale), register with Regione Piemonte, and set up AlloggiatiWeb for guest reporting
