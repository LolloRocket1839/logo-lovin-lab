# Selling your property to investors in Turin: complete guide

Are you trying to sell an apartment in Turin? You might not have considered it, but real estate investors could be your ideal buyers. They don't look for the perfect home to live in. They look for numbers that work.

This guide explains how an investor thinks, what they actually look for, and how to present your property to sell it quickly and at the right price.

## What investors actually care about

An investor doesn't buy with their heart. They buy with a spreadsheet.

The first questions they ask are always the same: what's the yield? What are the holding costs? How quickly can I start earning rental income?

The key metric is **gross yield**. Here's how to calculate it:

> Gross yield = (annual rent / purchase price) × 100

If your apartment is worth €100,000 and produces €6,000 in annual rent, the gross yield is 6%. In Turin, investors look for at least 6-8% gross yield in university areas.

The good news? Unlike a traditional buyer who judges the wall colours, an investor judges the numbers. An "ugly" property with good yield sells more easily than a beautiful apartment with weak numbers.

## The "rental ready" concept

A **rental ready** property is an apartment ready to be rented from the day after closing. This means it has:

- Certified and compliant systems
- Urban planning and cadastral conformity
- A reasonable energy class (at least E or higher)
- Functional furnishings (for student rentals)

For an investor, "rental ready" means zero days of vacancy after purchase. Every month without a tenant is lost yield.

If your property is already rented, even better. Selling an apartment with a current tenant is an advantage: the investor buys an active cash flow.

## How to calculate and present yield

When preparing your listing or speaking with potential investor buyers, always present the numbers clearly:

| Item | Value |
|------|-------|
| Asking price | €85,000 |
| Current or potential monthly rent | €450 |
| Annual rent | €5,400 |
| **Gross yield** | **6.35%** |
| Annual condo fees | €1,200 |
| Annual IMU (property tax) | €600 |
| Estimated net yield | ~4.2% |

An experienced investor will do their own calculations anyway, but presenting the numbers yourself shows competence and speeds up negotiations.

## Writing a listing that speaks to investors

The traditional listing ("bright three-room apartment with balcony") doesn't work with investors. Here's what to write:

**Effective title:** "Income-producing three-room in Cenisia – 7.2% gross yield"

**Elements to include:**
- Calculated gross yield
- Current or estimated rent (with source)
- Occupancy status (vacant, rented, empty)
- Condo fees and property tax
- Energy class and system status
- Proximity to universities or transport

**Elements to avoid:**
- Emotional descriptions ("cosy nest")
- Excessive focus on aesthetic finishes
- Omitting fixed costs

## Yield-driven pricing strategy

Instead of setting the price based on comparable sales in the area, you can work backwards: start from the target yield.

If the annual rent is €6,000 and the target investor seeks 7% gross:

> Price = annual rent / target yield = €6,000 / 0.07 = €85,714

This logic is called **yield-driven pricing** and it's how funds and professional investors think.

In Turin, for university areas like Cenisia, San Salvario, and Vanchiglia, gross yields range between 6% and 9%. For a detailed estimate of your property, consult our [property valuation guide](/blog/valutazione-immobiliare-torino-guida-completa).

## Selling a tenanted property

Selling with a current tenant isn't a disadvantage. For many investors it's a decisive advantage:

- **Immediate cash flow**: the investor earns from day one
- **Reduced risk**: the yield isn't theoretical but real
- **No startup costs**: no furnishing or tenant search expenses

**Note:** the tenant has pre-emption rights only in 4+4 contracts (art. 38 L. 392/1978). For transitional and student contracts, pre-emption doesn't apply. Check carefully before listing.

If the property is rented with a [student contract and dual-season strategy](/blog/contratto-studenti-affitto-breve-strategia), the yield can be even more attractive.

## Where to find investors

Investors don't search on general property portals like private buyers. Here's where to reach them:

1. **Groups and communities** of real estate investors on Facebook, Telegram, and specialised forums
2. **Specialised agencies** in income properties (not traditional agencies)
3. **Real estate crowdfunding platforms** as a visibility channel
4. **Accountants and wealth advisors** who manage client portfolios
5. **Judicial auctions**: many investors attend auctions and are also open to the open market

## Required documentation

A serious investor asks for documents immediately. Prepare them in advance to avoid slowing down the sale:

- **Updated cadastral survey**
- **Cadastral floor plan** matching the actual state
- **EPC** (Energy Performance Certificate) currently valid
- **System certifications** (electrical, gas, plumbing)
- **Condominium regulations** and latest assembly minutes
- **Condo fee statements** for the last 2 years
- **Current lease agreement** (if applicable)
- **F24 receipts** for IMU and cedolare secca
- **Title deed** (purchase, inheritance, donation)
- **Mortgage registry search** to verify no encumbrances

Having everything ready demonstrates seriousness and speeds up the closing timeline. The investor has already done their due diligence: don't make them redo it because documents are missing.
