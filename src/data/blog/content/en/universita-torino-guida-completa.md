# The University of Turin: A Legacy of Excellence and Innovation

The University of Turin (Università degli Studi di Torino, or UniTo) stands as one of Italy's most distinguished institutions of higher learning, combining over six centuries of academic tradition with a dynamic commitment to modern research and innovation. Founded in **1404** and currently educating approximately **83,000 students** across **27 departments**, the university has established itself as a cornerstone of Italian academia and a significant contributor to European intellectual life.

In November 2024, Turin was awarded the title of **European Capital of Innovation 2024-25**, a distinction that reflects not only the city's dynamic ecosystem but also the university's integral role in fostering cutting-edge research, technological advancement, and sustainable urban solutions.

## Historical Foundations and Evolution

### The Early Years and Establishment

The University of Turin emerged from the turbulent political landscape of early 15th-century Italy. In **1404**, Prince Ludovico di Savoia-Acaja, seeking to establish a center of learning within his territories, successfully petitioned Pope Benedict XIII for the establishment of a *studium generale*. The papal bull that formally created the university marked the beginning of an institution strategically positioned at the crossroads between the Alps, Liguria, and Lombardy.

The university's early years were characterized by significant challenges. Between the 1420s and 1430s, epidemics and political crises repeatedly disrupted operations, forcing the institution to relocate temporarily to **Chieri** (1427-1434) and **Savigliano** (1434). When the university finally returned to Turin in 1436, three core faculties were established: **Theology**, **Arts and Medicine**, and **Law**.

### Renaissance Connections and Distinguished Alumni

The university's early prestige attracted some of Europe's most notable scholars. In **1506**, the renowned humanist **Erasmus of Rotterdam** obtained his degree from the University of Turin, cementing the institution's reputation as a significant center of Renaissance learning.

### The Reforms of Victor Amadeus II

A transformative moment in the university's history occurred in **1720-1721**, when Victor Amadeus II initiated comprehensive reforms that would establish Turin as a model for other European universities. The inauguration of a prestigious new building in **Via Po**, close to Piazza Castello, symbolized the university's elevated status.

## Contemporary Structure and Academic Organization

### Departmental Framework and Schools

Today, the University of Turin operates through a sophisticated organizational structure comprising **27 departments** that encompass virtually all academic disciplines except engineering and architecture. These departments are organized into **six schools**:

| School | Focus Areas |
|--------|-------------|
| Medicine | Medical and surgical sciences |
| Human Sciences | Philosophy, humanities, psychology |
| Management and Economics | Economics, business, management |
| Agriculture and Veterinary | Agricultural sciences, veterinary |
| Sciences and Innovative Technologies | Natural sciences, computer science |
| Law and Political Sciences | Law, political sciences |

The university offers approximately **160 undergraduate and postgraduate degree programs**, serving a diverse student body.

### English-Taught Programs

The university's commitment to internationalization is evident in its growing portfolio of English-taught programs. Currently, UniTo offers **24 undergraduate and postgraduate degrees entirely in English**, covering fields as diverse as:

## Distinguished Alumni and Nobel Laureates

### Nobel Prize Winners

The University of Turin has produced **three Nobel Prize laureates**, all in Physiology or Medicine:

#### Rita Levi-Montalcini (Nobel 1986)
Graduated with the highest distinction in medicine and surgery in 1936, awarded the Nobel Prize for the discovery of **nerve growth factor (NGF)**. Her remarkable career included conducting research in a makeshift bedroom laboratory during Fascist Italy's persecution of Jews. She became a Senator for Life in 2001 and continued her research activities until her death at age 103.

#### Renato Dulbecco (Nobel 1975)
Received the Nobel Prize for his groundbreaking work on **tumor viruses** and their interactions with genetic material.

#### Salvador Luria (Nobel 1969)
Honored for discoveries concerning the **replication mechanism and genetic structure of viruses**.

## International Partnerships and Global Engagement

### The UNITA Alliance

The University of Turin plays a leadership role in one of Europe's most ambitious higher education collaborations. **UNITA – Universitas Montium**, launched in 2020, represents an alliance of **12 European universities from 7 countries**, bringing together nearly **250,000 students** and **22,000 staff members**.

**UniTo coordinates this project**, which aims to create an inter-university, transnational campus. The six thematic research axes are:

1. Renewable energies
2. Circular economy
3. Cultural heritage
4. Global health
5. Digital transition
6. Inclusive societies

### Global Partnership Network

- **~500 formal cooperation agreements** with institutions worldwide
- Active participation in **Erasmus+** mobility programs
- Double degree programs
- Collaborative curriculum development

## Medical Education and Healthcare Partnerships

### Medical Program Structure

The University of Turin's Medicine and Surgery program spans **6 years**:

**Preclinical phase (3 years):**
- Anatomy, physiology, biochemistry
- Pharmacology, molecular biology, pathology
- Laboratory work at Turin city campus

**Clinical training phase (3 years):**
- Rotations in internal medicine, surgery, pediatrics
- Obstetrics and gynecology, emergency medicine
- Training at Turin University Hospital

## Turin: European Capital of Innovation 2024-25

In November 2024, Turin was awarded the title of **European Capital of Innovation 2024-25** by the European Commission, winning a prize of **€1 million** to continue developing its innovation ecosystem.

### Innovation Hubs

Turin hosts numerous incubators and accelerators connected to its universities:

- **I3P** (Politecnico Incubator) - among Europe's best university incubators
- **2i3T** (University of Turin Incubator) - specialized in academic spin-offs
- Numerous accelerators and coworking spaces
- Industry-university collaborations

### 2i3T: UniTo's business incubator

**[2i3T](https://2i3t.it)** is the University of Turin's business incubator, dedicated to commercially valorizing the results of academic research. The name "2i3T" represents the three pillars of its mission: **Incubator**, **Enterprises** (Imprese), and **Technology Transfer**.

**Services offered:**
- Support for creating **academic spin-offs**
- **Incubation programs** for innovative startups
- **Technology transfer** and patent support
- Mentorship and investor networking
- Shared workspaces and laboratories

2i3T has contributed to the creation of numerous successful startups in biotechnology, life sciences, agritech, and social innovation sectors. In 2025, the startup **NanoMuG**, incubated at 2i3T, won the 21st edition of the **Start Cup Piemonte Valle d'Aosta**.

## Conclusion

The University of Turin represents an extraordinary case of an academic institution that has navigated six centuries of history while maintaining relevance and vitality. From its founding in 1404 to its current position as coordinator of European university networks and a world-class research center, UniTo has consistently demonstrated the ability to adapt to changing needs while remaining true to its fundamental mission of advancing knowledge and serving society.

For students considering higher education in Italy, the University of Turin offers a compelling combination: historical prestige supported by contemporary research excellence, affordable living costs in one of Europe's most livable cities, and global connections that open doors to careers worldwide.
