## The Politecnico masterplan: 300 million for an innovation ecosystem

[Politecnico di Torino](/blog/politecnico-torino-guida-completa) unveiled a masterplan worth over 300 million euros in 2025. The goal is clear: transform from a scattered campus into a system of interconnected hubs by 2030. Rector Stefano Corgnati wants to attract companies, talent, and capital into a single ecosystem.

The university has over 38,000 students. 60% come from outside Turin. 19% are international. Total campus area aims to reach 300,000 sqm by 2030.

### The new towers on Corso Castelfidardo

The most iconic project involves two ten-story towers in the heart of the Politecnico campus. That's 14,000 sqm and an investment between 56 and 60 million euros. Funding is split three ways: one third from the university, one third from the European Investment Bank, one third from private partners.

The first tower will house the departments of management, mechanical, and energy engineering. The feasibility study was due by late 2025.

### Three Innovation Hubs

The masterplan also includes three thematic Innovation Hubs. Focus areas: digitalization, aerospace, and sustainable mobility. The idea is to host spin-offs, startups, and R&D centers from companies wanting proximity to the university.

These hubs will change the economic fabric of Cenisia and Cit Turin. The impact on property values is already visible.

> **Sources**: Politecnico di Torino, Academic Year Opening 2025-2026 (polito.it); Rinnovabili.it, April 2025; Mole24, June 2025.

## The Valentino campus: architecture, design, and PNRR funding

The second major project is the Campus of Architecture, Design, and Planning in the Valentino area. It revolves around the renovation of Torino Esposizioni, the historic pavilions designed by Sottsass and Nervi.

Politecnico will invest around 50 million for the campus. The Nervi pavilion will host 12,500 sqm of classrooms, labs, ateliers, and study spaces. Opening is planned for 2028.

The entire Valentino Project has over 100 million euros in PNRR funding. It includes the renovation of 20 hectares of parkland: removing 65,000 sqm of asphalt, installing permeable surfaces, and planting 555 new trees. The project won the City'Scape Award 2025 at Milan's Triennale.

A green corridor will connect the Valentino Castle (UNESCO World Heritage) to the new academic spaces. It will pass through the future Central Civic Library and the renovated Teatro Nuovo.

For those living in the [San Salvario](/blog/san-salvario-guida-studenti) area, the impact will be significant. The influx of architecture and design students will boost housing demand.

> **Sources**: campusvalentino.polito.it; masterplan.polito.it; City of Turin; TorinoClick.

## The design district in Mirafiori

In the south of the city, on the former Fiat factory grounds, Politecnico has already established the Design and Sustainable Mobility District. This is where automotive engineering and design courses are held.

Next door is the Competence Industry Manufacturing 4.0, an excellence center for technological innovation. Future plans include the Sustainable Mobility and Manufacturing Hub, with restaurants, sports facilities, and student housing.

Stellantis has invested in converting the plant to electric and hybrid vehicles. The 500 Hybrid production was brought back to Mirafiori in 2025.

> **Sources**: TorinoOggi, October 2024; PassioneAutoItaliane, December 2025.

## [University of Turin](/blog/universita-torino-guida-completa): the city of science

The University of Turin operates across approximately 120 locations on seven main campuses. Its infrastructure investments are historic in scale.

### Grugliasco: the city of science takes shape

The Grugliasco Science Campus is the most advanced project. The investment is 160 million euros, entirely funded by Intesa Sanpaolo Group. The green area covers 228,600 sqm. The new building complex will occupy around 90,000 sqm.

It will host the Departments of Chemistry, Life Sciences, and Systems Biology. They'll join the existing Agricultural and Veterinary Sciences. Official opening is planned for autumn 2026.

The university population in Grugliasco will grow from 5,000 to over 10,000 students. A truly European campus with urban park, public square, and auditorium. Housing demand in the western suburbs will increase significantly.

### Palazzo Nuovo and the Cavallerizza Reale

In central Turin, the humanities campus is undergoing a 20+ million euro renovation. Palazzo Nuovo will get renovated lecture halls, new student spaces, and a reorganized library hub. The Cavallerizza Reale restoration is designed by architect Cino Zucchi.

### The biotechnology hub on Via Nizza

Along the city's medical corridor, the former Scalo Vallino will become the Biotechnology and Translational Medicine Research Hub. Total investment exceeds 106 million euros. It strengthens the scientific identity of the southern axis.

> **Sources**: TorinoOggi, February 2026; City of Turin; UniTO.

## New industrial and urban regeneration hubs

University expansion intertwines with a broader transformation. The new General Urban Plan, approved by the city council after 30 years, identifies five strategic macro-areas.

### Innovation Center: the Porta Susa-OGR-Politecnico axis

The area from Porta Susa to OGR to Politecnico is designated as the "Innovation Center." OGR, former railway workshops, have become a cultural and tech hub. The former RAI skyscraper is being redesigned by Carlo Ratti Associati for primarily residential use. The old Porta Susa station will get a new hotel.

### Civic Forum: Lingotto and the Health Park

The area from Lingotto to Lingotto Fiere, from the future Health Park to Palazzo del Lavoro, forms the "Civic Forum." Lingotto station is being fully renovated.

### Manifattura Tabacchi: university and cultural hub

In the Regio Parco district, the former Manifattura Tabacchi (46,000 sqm, 25 buildings) will become a cultural, archival, and university hub. The winning project is by Eutropia Architettura and Pininfarina Architecture.

It includes an Archival and Cultural Center for the Ministries of Justice and Culture. Plus a university center with student housing, services, and advanced training classrooms. Demolition work has already begun.

### Metro Line 2 as a catalyst

Metro Line 2 is the infrastructure enabling many of these transformations. The first section runs from Rebaudengo to Porta Nuova: 7.7 km and 10 stations. Funding: 1.83 billion euros.

Construction starts autumn 2026. Pre-operation expected by late 2032. Full service between late 2032 and early 2033. The systems and rolling stock contract was awarded to Hitachi Rail for 481 million.

The southern extension to Politecnico (and eventually Mirafiori-Orbassano) requires an additional 450 million not yet allocated. But the route is already designed.

> **Sources**: Quotidiano Piemontese, January 2026; Torino Cronaca, January 2026; City of Turin; Pambianco Design, October 2024; Il Sole 24 Ore, October 2024.

### Market numbers 2025-2026

As of January 2026, the average sale price in Turin reached 2,154 €/sqm. That's a 5.54% year-over-year increase. Residential rents rose to 12.47 €/sqm/month (+3.06%). Studios are the most dynamic segment, with a 16% appreciation in the last quarter.

Property demand grew 37% in the first half of 2025. Supply remained essentially flat (+4%).

Student rental prices in Turin are around 450-500 euros per month for a single room. Much less than Milan (659 €) and Bologna (over 500 €). Average gross rental yield sits at about 5.1%. **Crocetta-Cenisia (Politecnico area)**
Cenisia is up 2.6% at 2,345 €/sqm. Driven by proximity to Politecnico's Energy Center and OGR. Will benefit further from new towers and Innovation Hubs.

**San Salvario-Valentino**
With the Valentino Campus mega-project, San Salvario is up 3.5% at 2,050 €/sqm. Architecture and design student influx boosts demand.

**Vanchiglia-Vanchiglietta**
Proximity to Campus Einaudi and Nuvola Lavazza keeps this area among the most dynamic. Prices at 2,680 €/sqm (+3%) in Vanchiglia and 2,250 €/sqm (+2.3%) in Vanchiglietta.

**Lingotto-Nizza Millefonti**
The southern axis benefits from the renovated Lingotto station and future Health Park. Lingotto leads growth at +7.8% with average prices of 1,650 €/sqm.

**Mirafiori North and South**
Mirafiori North attracts demand from pricier Santa Rita, with prices at 1,650 €/sqm (+7.8%). Mirafiori South grows 6.8% (1,570 €/sqm). The Design District drives increasing demand.

**Aurora-Barriera di Milano-Regio Parco**
Northern peripheries are the real surprise. Aurora is up 7% (1,520 €/sqm), Barriera di Milano +5.4% (1,080 €/sqm). The Manifattura Tabacchi project and Metro 2 route make Regio Parco-Rebaudengo one of the most promising areas for medium-term investment.

**Grugliasco**
The upcoming City of Science opening will generate strong housing demand. Prices are still significantly below city averages.

## Student housing and PNRR: the race for beds

The national PNRR target remains 60,000 new university student beds. Total funding: 1.2 billion euros. The plan revision approved by the European Commission in November 2025 adjusted timelines.

About 30,000 beds must be ready by July 15, 2026 through the original tender (DM 481/2024). For the remaining 30,000, a new 599-million fund was established under Cassa Depositi e Prestiti. Completion by May 15, 2027.

Around 1,000 applications were submitted for over 100,000 candidate beds, with 60,000 already approved. In Turin, several projects are underway: residences at Manifattura Tabacchi, new student housing in Mirafiori, and facilities in the Grandi Motori area. The HOMA cooperative has 622 beds in its pipeline for Turin alone.

As Rector Corgnati put it: students coming to Turin should know in advance where they'll live. A different culture is needed. Student housing is essential.

## Turin 2030: the big picture

Turin's recognition as European Capital of Innovation 2024-2025 and Smart Tourism Capital 2025 is no accident. The city is building a network where academic hubs, innovation districts, and new economies connect through a deeply renewed infrastructure system.

The alliance between Politecnico, University, major companies like Leonardo and Thales Alenia Space, and incubators has created an ecosystem attracting students and qualified professionals in aerospace, cybersecurity, AI, and advanced technical consulting.

The lesson for real estate is clear. Neighborhoods at the intersection of university expansion, new transport infrastructure, and reconverted industrial hubs offer the best appreciation prospects. Those who position themselves before these projects reach full maturity (by 2028-2030) will capture the most significant opportunities.

Turin remains accessible compared to Milan. The average price per sqm in Turin (2,154 €) is about 62% lower than Milan (5,615 €). For those evaluating [student neighborhoods](/blog/dove-vivere-torino-studenti-politecnico), the transformation zones are the ones to watch.

Turin is no longer just the city of Fiat. It's an urban laboratory where academia, industry, and regeneration merge into a model that could set the standard for Europe.
