# Cedolare secca 2026: complete guide to tax changes for property investors

Cedolare secca represents one of the most advantageous tax regimes for residential property owners in Italy. In 2026, the regulatory landscape has undergone significant changes that directly affect the convenience of this tax choice for real estate investors.

This article analyzes the changes introduced by the **2026 Budget Law** and the latest implementing decrees, providing a comprehensive understanding of rates, applicability conditions, and strategic implications for those investing in the Italian real estate market.

The changes involve an **increase in the standard rate from 21% to 26%** under specific conditions, with significant repercussions on net investment returns. Understanding these dynamics is essential for maximizing returns and properly planning investment strategies.

---

## 1. Cedolare secca: regulatory foundations

### 1.1 Definition and legal framework

Cedolare secca is an optional tax regime, introduced by **Decree-Law of December 6, 2011, no. 201** (converted into Law of January 27, 2012, no. 9), which allows residential property owners to opt for a substitute tax for IRPEF and IRAP[^1].

This regime applies exclusively to:

- Residential property lease agreements
- Properties classified in cadastral categories A/2, A/3, A/4, A/5, A/6, A/7, A/8, A/9 (excluding A/1)
- Inventory properties and instrumental properties with specific limitations[^2]

The choice of cedolare secca is **voluntary and irrevocable** and is exercised at the time of the income tax return. For 2026, the fundamental principles established by Article 1, paragraphs 9-29, of Decree-Law of December 6, 2011, no. 201 remain valid.

### 1.2 The advantage of cedolare secca: administrative simplification

The main advantage of cedolare secca lies in administrative and tax simplification:

- **IRPEF substitutivity**: The tax replaces IRPEF on the rental income portion, allowing the income not to be declared in the RB section of the tax return[^3]
- **IRAP elimination**: For individual property owners, IRAP on rental income is eliminated[^2]
- **No registration taxes**: Registration tax on lease payments is not due (reduced to 0.5% only at contract signing)[^3]
- **Tax stability**: The tax remains fixed for the entire duration of the contract, making taxation predictable

These characteristics make cedolare secca particularly attractive for investors who want transparent and predictable taxation.

## 2. Cedolare secca rates in 2026

### 2.1 The main change: increase from 21% to 26%

The **2026 Budget Law** (Law of December 27, 2025, no. 207) introduced a significant change to cedolare secca rates. Starting from January 1, 2026, rates have been revised based on specific conditions[^4]:

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Standard rate** | 26% |
| **Regulatory provision** | Article 1, paragraph 38, of the 2026 Budget Law |

Effective January 1, 2026, the standard cedolare secca rate has increased from 21% to 26% for lease agreements that do not fall under preferential conditions[^4].

This rate applies to:

- Lease agreements signed after January 1, 2026 for which the owner opts for cedolare secca
- Agreements in force as of December 31, 2025, whose renewal (explicit or tacit) occurs after January 1, 2026
- Owners who do not benefit from the exemptions provided by law

> **Practical implication**: A property generating gross annual income of €10,000, subject to standard cedolare secca in 2026, will generate a tax of €2,600 (instead of €2,100), resulting in a net reduction of €500 annually (5% impact on profitability).

### 2.2 The reduced rate of 10% (social housing)

Alongside the increase in the standard rate, the law has confirmed and clarified the conditions for benefiting from a **reduced rate of 10%**[^4].

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Reduced rate** | 10% |
| **Regulatory provision** | Article 1, paragraph 39, of the 2026 Budget Law |

This preferential rate applies when the property owner:

1. Is a natural person (not a company or legal entity)
2. Is not a commercial entrepreneur in the real estate sector
3. Signs a free-rent agreement (not regulated by local agreements)
4. Resides in a municipality with high housing tension
5. Commits to allocating at least 75% of the rent to common funds dedicated to housing support (social housing)

This benefit was initially introduced by **Decree-Law of November 18, 2021, no. 181** (converted into Law of January 20, 2022, no. 4) and consolidated by the 2026 Budget Law[^5].

> **Practical implication**: The same property would generate a tax of only €1,000 (instead of €2,600) if it benefits from the reduced 10% rate, with an annual advantage of €1,600 compared to the standard rate.

### 2.3 Comparative rate table 2026

| Type of agreement | Rate | Gross income | Tax due | Net income |
|-------------------|------|--------------|---------|------------|
| Standard cedolare (non-preferential) | 26% | €10,000 | €2,600 | €7,400 |
| Preferential cedolare (social housing) | 10% | €10,000 | €1,000 | €9,000 |
| Standard declaration + IRAP (comparison) | 43% (IRPEF 23% + IRAP 19.5%) | €10,000 | €4,300 | €5,700 |

## 3. Transition period and applicability

**Critical question for investors**: How does the increase from 21% to 26% apply to existing agreements?

### 3.1 Agreements signed before January 1, 2026

According to the 2026 Budget Law, Article 1, paragraph 38, for agreements signed before January 1, 2026, if the owner has already opted for cedolare secca at 21%, **the rate remains at 21% until the agreement expires**.

However, this protection applies only if:

- The agreement was properly registered at the time of signing
- The option for cedolare secca had already been exercised
- There has been no early termination of the agreement

### 3.2 Tacit or explicit renewal after January 1, 2026

The situation becomes more complicated with contract renewals. According to **Revenue Agency Circulars no. 39/E of 2022 and no. 18/E of 2023**, renewal (whether explicit or tacit) of a contract constitutes a **contractual novation**[^6].

Therefore:

- If a contract expires on December 31, 2025 and tacitly renews on January 1, 2026, the new 26% rate applies
- If the owner and tenant sign a new lease agreement (even for the same property), from the post-January 1, 2026 signing date, the new 26% rate applies

This rule creates a **strategic incentive** for owners: terminate agreements and sign new ones by December 31, 2025 (although practically impossible for many).

> **Operational advice**: Owners with agreements expiring at the end of 2025 should verify the renewal timing. If it's possible to formalize the renewal by December 31, 2025, it may be advantageous to preserve the 21% rate.

## 4. Strategic implications for investors

### 4.1 Impact analysis on profitability

The increase from 21% to 26% results in a significant reduction in net returns. Let's consider a concrete example:

#### Case study: property investor in Turin

**Pre-2026 scenario (cedolare secca at 21%):**

- Residential property purchased in 2023
- Gross annual rental income: €12,000
- Cedolare secca due: €12,000 × 21% = €2,520
- Net income: €9,480
- Net return on invested capital: 5.9% (on €160,000 base)

**Post-January 1, 2026 scenario (cedolare secca at 26%):**

- Same property
- Gross annual rental income: €12,000
- Cedolare secca due: €12,000 × 26% = €3,120
- Net income: €8,880
- Net return on invested capital: 5.55% (on €160,000 base)
- **Annual loss: €600 (6.3% less compared to 2025)**

This differential becomes even more significant for portfolios with multiple properties.

### 4.2 Cedolare secca vs standard declaration convenience

Despite the increase, cedolare secca remains competitive compared to the standard taxation regime for most investors:

**Comparative analysis (gross income €12,000):**

| Tax regime | Rate | Tax | IRAP | Total tax | Net income |
|------------|------|-----|------|-----------|------------|
| Cedolare 2026 | 26% | €3,120 | — | €3,120 | €8,880 |
| Cedolare 2025 | 21% | €2,520 | — | €2,520 | €9,480 |
| Standard declaration | 23% (IRPEF) | €2,760 | on gross income | €5,100 | €6,900 |

> **Critical observation**: Even with the increase to 26%, cedolare secca remains significantly more advantageous than standard declaration (38.8% reduction in total tax). This consideration is essential for investors evaluating whether to abandon the preferential regime.

### 4.3 High-income investors: specific comparison

For investors with high overall income (highest IRPEF bracket of 43%), the convenience changes:

**Scenario: investor in the 43% IRPEF bracket**

| Tax regime | Effective rate | Tax on €12,000 | Net income |
|------------|----------------|----------------|------------|
| Standard cedolare 2026 | 26% | €3,120 | €8,880 |
| Standard declaration | 43% (IRPEF) + 19.5% (IRAP) = 62.5% | €7,500 | €4,500 |

Even in this scenario, cedolare secca at 26% remains extremely advantageous.

### 4.4 Strategy for investors: timing optimization

**Operational recommendation for investors with expiring agreements:**

1. **Before December 31, 2025**: If an agreement expires at year-end, verify the possibility of formalizing the renewal by December 31 to preserve the 21% rate[^4]
2. **Short-term rental evaluation**: For property owners in tourist areas (e.g., Turin, with increasing visitor flows), consider the convenience of short-term rentals

## 5. Cedolare secca and short-term rentals

### 5.1 Distinction between residential and short-term rentals

A frequent question among investors concerns the applicability of cedolare secca to short-term rental contracts (Airbnb, Booking, etc.). The answer is **no**: cedolare secca applies exclusively to residential lease agreements, not to short-term rentals[^7].

**Definition of "residential lease"** (according to Law of December 9, 1998, no. 431): An agreement signed for a minimum continuous period of 180 days, for which registration and payment of registration tax is required[^7].

### 5.2 Taxation of short-term rentals

For owners operating short-term rentals (agreements lasting less than 180 days), taxation follows the standard regime:

- **Standard IRPEF**: Income is taxed according to progressive brackets (23%, 27%, 38%, 41%, 43%)
- **IRAP**: If the owner is not categorized as an entrepreneur, taxation varies based on circumstances[^8]
- **Tourist tax**: Collected and paid by the owner/intermediary (varies by city)

> **Implication for Turin investors**: An owner who converts a residential lease to a short-term rental will not have access to cedolare secca, but may benefit from other deductions (furnishing, cleaning, maintenance expenses) in the standard declaration[^8].

## 6. Administrative and documentary obligations

### 6.1 Exercising the cedolare secca option

The option for cedolare secca must be exercised at the time of the income tax return (via the Redditi form or through CAF/professional). In the case of the first year, the option must be exercised in the declaration for the first year in which income is received[^1].

**Required documents:**

- Registered lease agreement
- Registration receipt from the Revenue Agency
- Communication of the option in the tax return (RB or RG section, depending on property category)

### 6.2 Agreement registration

Even with cedolare secca, the lease agreement must be registered with the Revenue Agency within 30 days of signing (or within 20 days if using the online RLI form)[^2].

The registration tax due is **€16** for any rent amount, if the agreement is registered via the RLI (Computerized Lease Registry) form[^2].

### 6.3 IMU and TASI declaration

Cedolare secca does not replace the obligations to declare IMU (Municipal Property Tax) and TASI (Tax on Indivisible Services), which remain the responsibility of the property owner[^3].

## 7. Special cases and exceptions

### 7.1 Shared properties and cedolare secca

An investor who rents out part of a property in which they reside (maintaining residence in the same unit) may opt for cedolare secca on the rented portion, provided that the latter is autonomous and distinct[^1].

### 7.2 Real estate companies and cedolare secca

Cedolare secca is reserved for **natural persons**. Real estate companies (S.R.L., S.P.A., S.S.) cannot opt for cedolare secca; they are subject to the standard taxation regime (IRPEF for partnerships, IRES for corporations)[^1].

### 7.3 Properties belonging to real estate investment funds

Real estate investment funds (FCII), which invest in residential properties, cannot benefit from cedolare secca. Taxation occurs at the individual investor level according to standard rules[^9].

## 8. Future prospects and strategic considerations

### 8.1 Regulatory stability 2026-2027

The 2026 Budget Law provides no indication of further changes for 2027. However, the legislative history of cedolare secca shows a propensity of the legislator to modify rates based on revenue needs[^10].

Investors should consider:

- **Conservative hypothesis**: Anticipate further increases in the coming years
- **Tax diversification**: Do not concentrate all rental income in a single tax regime
- **Continuous monitoring**: Follow Revenue Agency circulars for application clarifications

### 8.2 Effect on demand for residential rentals

Theoretically, the increase in cedolare secca from 21% to 26% could incentivize owners to:

1. Increase rental rates to compensate for the higher burden
2. Invest less in maintenance and improvements
3. Exit the residential rental market in favor of other forms of investment

However, the rigidity of real estate prices and market competition suggest that the rate increase will primarily be absorbed by the reduction in net returns[^10].

## 9. Conclusions and recommended actions

### 9.1 Summary of 2026 changes

The 2026 Budget Law introduced a significant increase in the standard cedolare secca rate from 21% to 26%, effective January 1, 2026. This change applies:

- To agreements signed after January 1, 2026
- To agreements renewed (explicitly or tacitly) after January 1, 2026
- With the exception of agreements signed by December 31, 2025, which maintain the 21% rate until natural expiration

At the same time, the preferential 10% rate remains confirmed for owners who allocate at least 75% of rents to common social housing funds.

### 9.2 Cedolare secca remains advantageous

Despite the rate increase, cedolare secca remains one of the most advantageous tax regimes available for property investors:

- **Compared to standard declaration**: 38.8% reduction in overall tax (cedolare at 26% vs standard declaration at 23% + IRAP 19.5%)
- **Tax predictability**: Tax remains constant for the entire duration of the agreement
- **Administrative simplification**: No need to complete complex RB sections

### 9.3 Recommended actions for investors

1. **Verify the expiration of existing agreements** and evaluate whether it's advantageous to formalize renewal by December 31, 2025
2. **Compare cedolare secca at 26% with the standard regime**, particularly for investors in higher IRPEF brackets
3. **Maintain cedolare secca as the primary tax regime**, except in special cases of investors with high overall income
4. **Properly document all agreements** by registering them within the required deadlines and formalizing the cedolare secca option in the tax return
5. **Monitor legislative developments** in view of possible further changes

## Glossary of terms

**Cedolare secca**: Substitute tax for IRPEF and IRAP, applicable to free-rent residential leases.

**Residential lease**: Lease agreement for residential properties with a minimum duration of 180 days, registered with the Revenue Agency.

**Contractual novation**: Transformation of an existing contract into a new one, which entails the application of the regulations in force at the date of signing the new contract.

**IRPEF**: Personal Income Tax (progressive taxation from 23% to 43%, based on income).

**IRAP**: Regional Tax on Productive Activities (standard rate 19.5%, applied to non-residential owners and companies).

**Registration tax**: Tax due at the time of signing an agreement; for lease agreements registered online via RLI, it amounts to €16 regardless of the amount.

**Social housing**: Housing programs designed to provide housing at reduced rates to individuals in economic difficulty.
