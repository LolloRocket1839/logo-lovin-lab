# Property Valuation in Turin: Complete Guide to Estimating Your Home's Value

Are you thinking of selling your home in Turin and want to know what it's really worth? This guide explains everything you need to know about property valuation in the Piedmontese capital, from official methods to factors that influence price, to free tools for getting a reliable estimate.

## What is Property Valuation

Property valuation is the technical process that determines the **market value** of a property, i.e., the most likely price at which it can be sold under normal conditions. It's not just an opinion, but an analysis based on objective data, established methodologies, and knowledge of the local market.

In Turin, where the real estate market has specific dynamics linked to the presence of **90,000 university students** and 7 universities, an accurate valuation is essential for:

- Selling at the right price without underselling
- Avoiding overestimation and staying on the market for months
- Planning real estate investments
- Applying for mortgages or financing

## The 3 Main Valuation Methods

### 1. Comparative Market Method

This is the most commonly used method for residential properties. It's based on comparison with **similar properties recently sold** in the same area. Analysis includes:

- Price per square meter of recent sales
- Comparable characteristics (size, floor, condition)
- Differences to correct (balcony, exposure, garage)

### 2. Replacement Cost Method

Calculates how much it would cost to rebuild the property today, subtracting depreciation for age. Mainly used for:

- Unique or historic properties
- Insurance appraisals
- Industrial or special buildings

### 3. Income Method (Capitalization)

Values the property based on the **income it can generate** from rent. Very relevant in Turin for:

- Apartments in university areas (San Salvario, Vanchiglia, Cenisia)
- Investors in the student rental market
- Calculating gross and net returns

## How to Calculate Your Property's Value

### Step 1: Find the OMI Valuation for Your Area

The Revenue Agency publishes semi-annual OMI valuations. For Turin you can consult them at:
- [Borsino Immobiliare](https://www.borsinoimmobiliare.it/quotazioni/piemonte/torino)
- [Geopoi OMI](https://www.agenziaentrate.gov.it/portale/web/guest/schede/fabbricatiterreni/omi)

### Step 2: Calculate Commercial Surface Area

Don't confuse walkable surface with commercial surface:

```
Commercial surface = 
  Internal surface × 1.00 +
  Uncovered balconies/terraces × 0.25 +
  Covered terraces × 0.35 +
  Exclusive garden × 0.15 +
  Garage × 0.50 +
  Cellar × 0.25
```

### Step 3: Apply Correction Coefficients

Multiply the OMI base price by coefficients for:
- Floor
- State of conservation
- Brightness
- Energy class
- View

## When You Need a Professional Appraisal

Online estimates are useful for an indicative idea, but for formal situations you need a **sworn appraisal** from a professional:

- **Purchase/sale**: the notary may request it
- **Mortgage**: the bank appoints an independent appraiser
- **Inheritance and donations**: needed for tax calculation
- **Legal cases**: court or inheritance divisions
- **Major investments**: thorough due diligence

The cost of a professional appraisal in Turin ranges from €300 to €600 for a standard apartment.

## Common Valuation Mistakes

### 1. Overvaluing Due to Attachment
"My house is worth more because I've done so much work on it." Caution: not all work increases value proportionally to cost.

### 2. Relying on Asking Prices
Portals show asking prices, not actual sale prices. The difference can be 10-20%.

### 3. Ignoring Market Trends
Turin's market is evolving: areas like Barriera di Milano or Aurora are appreciating, while others are stable.

### 4. Forgetting Sale Costs
From the sale price you must subtract:
- Possible agency commission (3-4%)
- Energy certification (€150-300)
- Any cadastral regularizations
