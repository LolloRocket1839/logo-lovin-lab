# Rental contracts and tenant default in Italy: the complete guide 2026

**Italy has six main types of residential lease agreements.** Each has different tax rules, durations, and constraints. Choosing the wrong contract can cost thousands of euros in taxes. Or years of litigation with a defaulting tenant.

In 2025-2026 the regulatory framework changed significantly. The CIN became mandatory. The business threshold for short-term rentals dropped to 2 apartments. The Security Decree 2025 introduced accelerated eviction timelines.

This guide analyses every contract type and every landlord protection tool. The focus is on Turin and student housing.

---

## The six residential lease contracts compared

Italy's rental system revolves around **Law 431/1998**. It distinguishes between free-market and regulated-rent contracts. Special types exist for transitional needs and students. The foresteria (corporate housing) contract is governed by the Civil Code.

### The 4+4 free-market contract

This is the standard contract under Art. 2(1) of Law 431/1998. The minimum term is **4 years** with automatic renewal for another 4.

At the first expiry the landlord can refuse renewal only for specific reasons. Personal use, renovation, or sale to third parties. Notice must be at least **6 months** via registered letter or certified email (PEC). A notice without valid grounds is void. It entails compensation to the tenant of at least **36 monthly rents**.

Only at the second expiry (year 8) can either party not renew without giving reasons.

The rent is freely agreed between the parties. It can be adjusted annually with the ISTAT index if stipulated in the contract. Tax options: **flat tax (cedolare secca) at 21%** or standard IRPEF from 23% to 43% on 95% of the rent.

The main disadvantage for the landlord is the binding 8-year minimum. No specific tax benefits.

### The 3+2 regulated-rent contract: the most tax-efficient

The regulated-rent contract (Art. 2(3), Law 431/1998 and Ministerial Decree 16/01/2017) offers Italy's best tax package. The rent is bound by local Territorial Agreement tables. The term is **3+2 years** (also 4+2, 5+2, 6+2 possible) with automatic biennial renewal.

Tax benefits are cumulative and very significant:

- **Flat tax reduced to 10%** instead of 21% (confirmed permanently by Art. 1(6), Law 160/2019)
- **IMU reduced by 25%** automatically (tax calculated on 75%)
- **IRPEF taxable base reduced by 30%** for properties in high housing-demand municipalities
- **Registration tax** calculated on 70% of the rent

The key requirement is the **attestazione di rispondenza** (compliance certification). It must be issued by at least one organization that signed the Territorial Agreement. Without it, no benefits are accessible.

**In Turin** the current Territorial Agreement (signed 06/12/2022, updated 15/04/2024) divides the city into 4 areas. Values range from **€2.50/sqm** in the outskirts to **€8/sqm** in prime areas. The effective IMU rate for regulated-rent contracts with resident tenants is **4.31‰**. About half the standard rate for second homes.

A concrete example: with annual rent of €10,000, the 10% flat tax costs **€1,000** instead of €2,100 at 21%. Net savings of **€1,100/year** on the flat tax alone, plus the IMU discount. Certification costs €50-150.

### The transitional contract lasts 1 to 18 months

Governed by Art. 5(1) of Law 431/1998 and Art. 2 of the 2017 Decree. It serves documented temporary needs: work transfer, renovation, internship, family care. Missing the motivational clause triggers **automatic conversion to a 4+4 contract**.

No automatic renewal. The contract ends at expiry without notice. In municipalities with over 10,000 inhabitants and for contracts longer than 30 days, the rent must be regulated. Below 10,000 it is free.

Tax regime: **flat tax at 10%** if regulated (in high housing-demand municipalities), 21% if free-market.

### Student contract: 6-36 months with automatic renewal

This is the key type for student housing. Provided by Art. 5(2-3) of Law 431/1998. The tenant must be enrolled at a university in a different municipality from their residence. The property must be in the university's municipality or an adjacent one.

Duration ranges from **6 to 36 months** with automatic renewal for an equal period. Only the tenant can give notice (1-3 months). The rent is **always regulated**, with values set by local agreements.

Tax benefits are identical to the 3+2: **10% flat tax**, 25% IMU reduction. The student (or parent) also gets a **19% IRPEF deduction** on rent paid up to **€2,633/year** if the university is at least 100 km from the student's residence.

**In Turin**, with UniTo, PoliTo and Campus Einaudi, student demand is very high. The Territorial Agreement includes specific student tables (Annex 3) and a dedicated contract template (Annex 6). The effective IMU rate is **4.31‰**. Partial rentals (single rooms) are allowed under Annex 7.

### The foresteria (corporate housing) contract

When the tenant is a **corporation** (S.p.A., S.r.l.) housing employees on assignment, the Civil Code applies (Art. 1571 ff.). Law 431/1998 does not apply. Maximum freedom on rent, duration, and deposit (no 3-month cap).

The **flat tax is not applicable** because the tenant is a legal entity. The landlord pays standard IRPEF. The main advantage is lower default risk and no minimum duration constraints.

## Short-term rentals in 2026: CIN, flat tax, and new business threshold

Short-term rentals (up to 30 days, Art. 4, DL 50/2017) underwent the most significant regulatory changes in 2025-2026.

### The CIN has been mandatory since January 1, 2025

The **National Identification Code** (Art. 13-ter, DL 145/2023) is obtained through bdsr.ministeroturismo.gov.it with SPID/CIE. Self-certification of safety requirements is mandatory. The CIN must be displayed outside the building and included in every listing.

Fines for non-compliance range from **€800 to €8,000**. For missing it in listings, from **€500 to €5,000**. By April 2025, 86% of properties were compliant.

### Flat tax and business threshold

The tax regime allows the flat tax at **21% on one property** of the taxpayer's choice and **26% on the second**. The **2026 Budget Law** (L. 199/2025, Art. 1(17)) lowered the threshold: **from the third property, business activity is presumed**. VAT registration and Chamber of Commerce enrollment become mandatory. The previous threshold was 4 apartments.

### Safety requirements and regional rules

Since January 1, 2025, **combustible gas detectors, carbon monoxide detectors, and portable fire extinguishers** are mandatory in all short-term rental units. Platforms like Airbnb and Booking withhold 21% as advance tax.

In Piedmont, Regional Resolution 7-748 of February 3, 2025 approved new forms. Regional Law 9/2025 aligned regional with national rules. In **Turin**, non-business rentals do not require a SCIA filing. Communication via Ross 1000 and the tourist tax remain mandatory.

### The Constitutional Court confirms regulatory power

**Constitutional Court ruling 186/2025** confirmed the power of Regions and Municipalities to regulate and limit short-term rentals. Emilia-Romagna has already legislated accordingly (Regional Law 10/2025), although the law was challenged by the Government.

## Eviction for non-payment in 2026: procedures, timelines, and the Security Decree

Tenant default remains the structural problem of Italy's rental market. In **2024, 40,158 eviction orders** were issued nationwide. **75% for non-payment** (30,041 orders). Execution requests reached **81,054**. But only **21,337 evictions** were actually carried out with public force: just 26% of requests.

**Turin ranks third nationally** for evictions with **2,350 orders** in 2024. Piedmont with 4,058 is the fourth region.

### The eviction process from notice to execution

The procedure begins with serving the eviction notice (Art. 658 CPC) with simultaneous summons for validation. For residential leases, default kicks in after non-payment of **1 month's rent plus 20 days** from the due date. Or for ancillary charges exceeding 2 months' rent.

At the hearing, if the tenant is absent or does not object, the judge issues an **immediately enforceable** validation order. The tenant can request a **grace period** of up to 90 days (Art. 55, Law 392/1978). This can be granted no more than **3 times in 4 years**. The tenant must pay everything: back rent, interest, and legal costs.

If the tenant objects, the case converts to ordinary proceedings lasting 1-2 years.

Costs for the landlord range from **€600** (simple case) to **€3,000-5,000** (with opposition and forced execution).

### The Security Decree 2025 speeds up timelines

**DL 41/2025, converted into Law 80/2025** is the most significant intervention. New rules require eviction validation within **30 days** of the request. Execution within **20 days** of the order. Squatting is now a criminal offense (Art. 634-bis Criminal Code, 2-7 years imprisonment).

Certified email (PEC) is valid for all notifications. Suspension of execution is limited to documented cases of proven vulnerability.

**In practice**, court workloads remain a bottleneck. Real timelines range from **2-4 months** in the best case to **over 1 year** in complex cases.

### The payment order is a complementary weapon

Art. 664 CPC allows requesting a payment order for back rent in the same filing. **Without additional court fees.** Two enforcement titles are obtained simultaneously: one for property release, the other for debt recovery through seizure. This combination is **always recommended**.

### Legislative proposals under discussion

**Bill 1610** (Sen. Marcheschi, FdI, July 2025) proposes an Eviction Execution Authority (AES). Enforcement title issued in 7 days and clearance within 30 days. A further bill provides for eviction within 10 days for squatters and defaulters. Both are **still unapproved proposals**.

## Preventive tools: from security deposit to digital screening

Default prevention operates on multiple levels.

### Security deposit

The deposit (Art. 11, Law 392/1978) cannot exceed **3 months' rent**. It must accrue legal interest annually for the tenant. The legal rate for 2025 is **2.00%**, for 2026 **1.60%**.

### Bank or insurance guarantee

A **bank guarantee** offers superior protection. Its amount is not subject to the 3-month limit. It can cover 6-12 or more months' rent. Cost: **2-3% per year** of the guaranteed amount. Payable on first demand, it is the safest form.

The insurance guarantee (cost: **2-5% per year**) is less bureaucratic. It lapses if the tenant does not pay the premium.

### Rent default insurance

In H1 2025, pecuniary loss policies reached **€416 million** in premiums (+11% YoY). Policies typically cover up to **12 months** of unpaid rent plus legal costs up to €2,500. Costs for the landlord range from **2% to 10%** of annual rent (€250-400/year).

Platforms like Zappyrent, Extracasa and Rent2Cash pay the landlord even in case of default.

### Fund for blameless default

The fund (DL 102/2013) was refinanced by the 2025 Budget Law with **€10 million for 2025 and €20 million for 2026**. It covers up to **€12,000 per beneficiary** (ISEE ≤€26,000). Disbursement can go directly to the landlord.

### Italy has no official defaulting tenant registry

Unlike Germany (SCHUFA) or the UK, **Italy has no public registry of rental defaulters**. Systems like CRIF collect data only on mortgages and loans. FIMAA-Italy proposed creating a public database in September 2025. About **6 million homes** remain vacant because owners fear default.

The only operational private database is **BDMI (Banca Dati Morosità Immobiliare)** by idealista. It allows landlords to report defaulting tenants and screen potential ones. Consultation requires the tenant's signed authorization (GDPR). Data is kept for 5 years.

Best practices for screening include: last **3 payslips + tax certificate + employment contract** for employees. **Tax returns** for the past 2 years for self-employed. A guarantor with identical documentation. Verification through BDMI, Controllo Inquilino (ReGold), or specialized proptech platforms.

## Conclusion: a market that rewards structure

Italy's 2026 rental regulatory framework shows two converging trends. The legislator is **speeding up eviction procedures** (Security Decree 2025). And tightening the tax perimeter for short-term rentals (threshold at 2 properties).

The regulated-rent contract remains the most efficient tool for student housing operators. In Turin, the 10% flat tax, 75% IMU base, and contractual stability form an unbeatable package. The effective IMU rate drops to 4.31‰.

The most telling figure remains the gap between **81,054 execution requests** and just **21,337 evictions carried out** in 2024. Even with new accelerated rules, prevention beats cure. Digital screening, first-demand guarantees, and rent insurance policies are essential tools for professional management.
