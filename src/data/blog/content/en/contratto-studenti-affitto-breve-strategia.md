# Student contract + summer short-term rental: what Italian law actually says

Renting to students during the academic year and to tourists in summer is one of the most profitable strategies in Italian real estate. But is it legal? How does it work from a contractual and tax perspective? This guide answers every question with precise legal references.

The model is simple: [cedolare secca at 10%](/blog/cedolare-secca-2026-investitori) on a student contract from October to July. Then cedolare secca at 21% on short-term tourist rentals in August and September. Two distinct tax regimes on the same property in different periods.

---

## The transitional lease for university students

### Legal basis

The university student contract is governed by Art. 5, paragraphs 2 and 3, of Law 431/1998. Implementation rules are in Ministerial Decree of 16 January 2017. This is a special contract, distinct from both the standard 4+4 free-market lease and the generic transitional contract.

### Duration

Minimum duration is 6 months, maximum 36 months (3 years). At the first expiry, the contract renews automatically for an equal period. The tenant can withdraw for documented "serious reasons" with 3 months' notice via registered mail.

The landlord cannot terminate early. Art. 13 of Law 431/1998 voids any clause that derogates from the duration limits. A non-compliant contract would be reclassified as a standard 4+4.

### Requirements

Three conditions must be met for a valid student contract:

- **The tenant** must be enrolled at a university (bachelor's, master's, PhD, specialization). The enrollment certificate must be attached. The student must reside in a different municipality from the university.
- **The property** must be in the municipality where the university is located or in a neighbouring one.
- **The contract** must use the ministerial template (Annex F of Ministerial Decree 16/01/2017). Subletting is prohibited. If one student leaves a joint contract, replacement is possible with the landlord's consent.

### Agreed rent (canone concordato)

Rents are set by territorial agreements between landlord and tenant associations. They are expressed in €/m² within minimum and maximum bands per homogeneous zone. For non-assisted contracts, certification from a signatory organisation is required (AE Resolution 31/E/2018).

Registration at the Agenzia delle Entrate is mandatory within 30 days via Form RLI.

## Cedolare secca at 10% for student contracts in Turin

### Turin qualifies: definitive confirmation

The [10% cedolare secca](/blog/cedolare-secca-2026-investitori) is provided by Art. 3 of Legislative Decree 23/2011. The reduced rate applies to agreed-rent contracts in municipalities with high housing demand.

Turin is explicitly listed among the 11 major municipalities in Decree Law 551/1988: Bari, Bologna, Catania, Florence, Genoa, Milan, Naples, Palermo, Rome, Turin, Venice. Turin is also a metropolitan city capital and appears in CIPE Resolution 87/2003.

### How to exercise the option

The option is exercised when registering the contract (Form RLI). It requires waiving the ISTAT rent adjustment for the entire option period. It must be confirmed at each renewal within 30 days.

### Cumulative advantages

With the 10% cedolare secca on a student contract in Turin, the investor benefits from:

- 10% flat tax on 100% of rent (versus IRPEF at 23-43% on 95%)
- No registration tax or stamp duty
- 25% IMU reduction on agreed-rent properties

On annual rent of €10,000, savings compared to ordinary taxation (35% IRPEF bracket) exceed €2,000/year.

## CIN: national identification code for short-term rentals

### What it is and who needs it

Introduced by Art. 13-ter of Decree Law 145/2023 (Anticipi Decree), the CIN is a national alphanumeric code for every property used for tourist rentals, short-term lets (≤30 days), and hospitality. It is managed by the Ministry of Tourism through the BDSR (national database).

The CIN is mandatory for all operators since 1 January 2025. This includes occasional owners with a single property.

### How to get it

1. Access the BDSR portal (bdsr.ministeroturismo.gov.it) via SPID, CIE, or CNS
2. Submit an application with cadastral data, sworn declaration, and safety compliance documentation
3. In Piedmont: first obtain the CIR through Piemonte Dati Turismo (ROSS 1000), then the CIN

### Penalties

Penalties are severe:

- Operating without CIN: €800-€8,000
- Failure to display CIN in listings: €500-€5,000 plus immediate removal
- Missing safety devices: €600-€6,000 per violation
- Missing SCIA for commercial activity: €2,000-€10,000

## SCIA requirements and communications in Piedmont

### For non-commercial operators in Turin

SCIA is required only for commercial activity. Non-commercial operators must comply with:

1. Initial communication via Piemonte Dati Turismo (ROSS 1000), Form LT_2018, within 10 days of the first rental
2. CIR (Regional Identification Code) assigned upon communication
3. CIN from the Ministry of Tourism
4. Monthly statistical report via ROSS 1000 by the 10th of the following month
5. Police notification (AlloggiatiWeb) within 24 hours of guest arrival
6. Collection and payment of tourist tax

### Commercial threshold: lowered to 2 properties from 2026

Under Law 199/2025 (2026 Budget Law), managing more than 2 properties for short-term rentals creates a presumption of commercial activity from tax year 2026. The previous threshold was 4. A VAT number, Chamber of Commerce registration, and SCIA are required.

## Taxation: combining 10% and 21% cedolare secca

### The fundamental principle

The student contract (Art. 5, Law 431/1998) and the short-term lease (Art. 4, Decree Law 50/2017) are distinct legal instruments. The cedolare secca option is exercised separately for each contract: at registration for student contracts, in the tax return for short-term leases.

There is no explicit prohibition on applying different regimes to the same property in different periods.

### How it works in practice

During the academic year, student rental income is taxed at 10%. During summer months, short-term rental income is taxed at 21% on the first property (or 26% from the second property onwards).

The first/second property count for the 21%/26% differentiation applies only to short-term leases. The student contract is a separate regime and does not count.

### Worked example

Three-room apartment purchased for €90,000 in Cenisia (near Politecnico):

| Period | Contract type | Income | Rate | Tax |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oct-Jul (10 months) | Students (3 rooms × €400) | €12,000 | 10% | €1,200 |
| Aug-Sep (2 months) | Short-term (€80/night, 70% occupancy) | ~€3,360 | 21% | €706 |
| **Annual total** | | **€15,360** | | **€1,906** |

Net yield after taxes: €13,454, equal to 14.9% on the purchase price. Even accounting for IMU (~€350 with 25% reduction), TARI (~€300), and management costs (~€1,500), net yield settles around 11-12%.

## Can you legally alternate students and tourists?

The answer is yes, with important practical limits. There is no explicit legal prohibition. But the structure of student contracts imposes real constraints.

### Option A: 6-month student contract

Contract from 1 October to 31 March. The student gives notice before automatic renewal. From April to September the property is available for short-term lets (6-month tourist window).

**Risk:** if the student does not give notice, the contract renews automatically for another 6 months. The landlord cannot terminate.

### Option B: 10-month student contract (most realistic)

Contract from 1 October to 31 July. The student gives notice. August and September for short-term lets (2-month tourist window).

**Advantage:** more realistic because it follows the natural academic calendar. The student has a genuine reason not to renew: exams finished, summer internship, going home.

### Option C: 12-month student contract

Full academic cycle, no summer switch. The simplest solution legally but gives up summer tourist income.

### Critical warnings

The landlord cannot include early termination clauses or unilaterally terminate the student contract. Consensual termination is possible but depends on the student's willingness.

If the alternation becomes too systematic, the Agenzia delle Entrate could challenge the genuineness of the transitional need. The contract would be reclassified as a standard 4+4, losing the 10% benefit.

**Practical advice:** structure the contract with a duration that follows the student's actual academic calendar. Always document university enrollment and out-of-town residency. The transitional nature must be genuine, not artificial.

## Municipal regulations in Turin for short-term rentals

### No cap on rental days

As of March 2026, Turin does not impose a maximum number of short-term rental days. Only the national threshold applies: maximum 2 properties without a VAT number from 2026.

### Tourist tax: rates from 1 April 2026

Updated by City Council resolution of 15 December 2025:

| Accommodation | Rate per person/night |
|---|---|
| Hotels 1-2 stars | €3.00 |
| Hotels 3 stars | €3.50 |
| Hotels 4 stars | €4.50 |
| Hotels 5 stars | €5.00 |
| B&B | €3.80 |
| Tourist rentals / guesthouses | ~€3.80 |

Maximum 7 overnight stays per calendar year subject to the tax (changed from quarterly to annual basis in 2026). Exempt: minors, persons with severe disabilities plus one companion, on-duty law enforcement.

### Police notification

Mandatory for all short-term leases via AlloggiatiWeb within 24 hours of guest arrival (6 hours for stays under 24 hours). Applies to Italian and foreign guests.

## Safety requirements for short-term rentals

Art. 13-ter, paragraph 7, of Decree Law 145/2023 requires for all short-term lets:

- **Combustible gas detectors:** compliant with EN 50194 standard. Exempt only for properties without a gas system.
- **Carbon monoxide detectors:** compliant with EN 50291-1:2010, mandatory if combustion appliances are present (boilers, stoves, fireplaces).
- **Portable fire extinguishers:** certified and approved. Minimum 1 per 200 m² of surface, minimum 1 per floor. Minimum capacity 13A, 6 kg/6 L. Maintenance per UNI 9994-1:2013.

Penalties for missing safety devices: €600-€6,000 per violation.

## Operational checklist for the dual-season strategy

1. Purchase a property in a university area with tourist appeal ([Vanchiglia, San Salvario, Cenisia, Centro](/blog/dove-vivere-torino-studenti-politecnico))
2. Sign a student contract at agreed rent (6-10 months) with [10% cedolare secca](/blog/cedolare-secca-2026-investitori)
3. Obtain certification from a signatory organisation of the territorial agreement
4. Register the contract at the Agenzia delle Entrate (Form RLI)
5. Before the student contract ends: verify the student communicates notice
6. For the summer period: obtain CIR (Piemonte Dati Turismo) and CIN (BDSR)
7. Install mandatory safety devices (gas, CO, fire extinguishers)
8. Register the property on AlloggiatiWeb (Questura)
9. Publish listings with visible CIN on all platforms
10. Collect and pay tourist tax
11. Report guests to the Questura within 24 hours of arrival
12. In tax return: 10% cedolare secca (students) + 21% (short-term)
13. Send monthly statistical reports via ROSS 1000
