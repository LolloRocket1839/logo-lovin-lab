# The historic Ivrea Carnival: complete guide to the Battle of Oranges from Turin

The **Ivrea Carnival**, officially the **Historic Carnival of Ivrea**, ranks among Italy's oldest and most distinctive carnival celebrations. Its fame rests on the **Battle of Oranges**, a controlled citrus fight that draws thousands of visitors each year. This guide covers everything tourists and students need to know: history, getting there from Turin, what to expect, and how to prepare. The 2025 edition runs **from March 1-5**, with five days of historical ceremonies, processions, and the spectacular battle itself.[1][2]

## The History of the Ivrea Carnival

### Medieval Origins and the Legend of Violetta

The Carnival's origins stretch back centuries, but the story most deeply rooted in popular tradition is the legend of **Mugnaia Violetta**. According to this tale, medieval Ivrea suffered under a cruel tyrant who enforced **ius primae noctis**, a feudal "right" allowing the lord to spend the first night with every newlywed bride. The population endured this abuse until one young woman changed history. Violetta, a miller's daughter, refused to submit. Instead of yielding to the tyrant, she struck him with a sword and decapitated him. Legend says she showed his head to the people, sparking a revolt.[3][4]

The citizenry rose up, destroyed the tyrant's castle, and regained their freedom. Though not confirmed by documentary evidence, the story has become an **emblem of resistance to oppression and popular courage**. The figure of the **Mugnaia**, commemorated annually as the Carnival's central protagonist, continues to embody this spirit of defiance.[4][3]

### The Battle of Oranges: Historical Evolution

Documented origins of the modern Ivrea Carnival date to the **16th century**, when the city's various districts (the parishes of San Maurizio, San Lorenzo, Sant'Ulderico, San Salvatore, and San Grato) organized competing festivities. This rivalry still characterizes the event. Early carnivals already included rituals practiced today, such as the parade of the **"Abbà"**, young revelers who assumed mock command of the Free Commune militia in the carnival tradition of the "world upside down."[5][6]

The **19th century** brought new symbolic elements. The figure of the **Mugnaia** emerged around 1858, inspired by the Violetta legend. The **Battle of Oranges** also took shape during this period. Historical sources suggest even older roots: in the Middle Ages, the local sovereign reportedly offered citizens a paltry alms of **beans**. Offended, the people threw the beans from their windows in protest. This tradition evolved over centuries, with oranges gradually replacing beans in the 19th century. The exotic fruit made for a more spectacular and visible display.[7][6][4]

Today, the **Battle of Oranges** symbolizes this popular struggle: **foot orange throwers** (representing the people in revolt) face off against **orange throwers on horse-drawn carts** (representing feudal tyranny). Every orange thrown references that medieval rebellion and the citizens' claim to freedom and dignity.[3][7]

## Complete program for the 2025 carnival

### Initial events (January 6 - end of February)

The 2025 Ivrea Carnival is not concentrated only in the five days of March. Celebrations officially begin on **January 6, 2025** with the emergence of the **Pifferi e Tamburi** (Fifes and Drums) in Piazza di Città at 09:00. These traditional musicians in historical costume, playing traditional folk instruments, symbolically mark the beginning of the carnival season. Their arrival is accompanied by rituals and ceremonies that evoke the medieval and Renaissance customs of the city.[1]

In the months preceding March, numerous preparatory events take place: among these the historic investiture of the **General** (a figure of Napoleonic origin representing civil authority), a mass at the Cathedral, and various meetings of the historical components of the Carnival to prepare for the grand finale.[2][1]

### Carnival Saturday (March 1, 2025)

The Saturday that inaugurates the main week (March 1) is primarily dedicated to the **presentation of the Lovely Mugnaia**, the culminating and most evocative moment of the preliminary celebration. In the evening:

- **9:30 PM**: March of the Historical Procession with torchlight parade and parade of the foot orange thrower teams. The route crosses the main streets of the city: starting from Piazza di Città, via Arduino, Piazza Maretta, via Guarnotta, Ponte Vecchio, via Gozzano, Piazza Lamarmora, corso Nigra, corso Cavour, corso Gallo, corso Botta, Piazza Balla, via Palestro, Piazza Castello, returning to Piazza di Città[2]

- **10:30 PM**: Fireworks show on Lungo Dora in honor of the Lovely Mugnaia. Fireworks illuminate the night sky creating a magical and evocative atmosphere[2]

- **11:00 PM**: End of the Historical Procession with the solemn dance of the Mugnaia and the General in Piazza di Città, a ritual moment representing the symbolic alliance between the people (represented by the Mugnaia) and civil authority (the General)[2]

### Carnival Sunday (March 2, 2025)

Sunday represents the first of the three days dedicated to the actual **Battle of Oranges**. The program is intense and complete:

- **9:00 AM - 11:00 AM**: Bean distributions in various district locations (Castellazzo, San Lorenzo, via Dora Baltea, San Bernardo). The traditional **faseuj grass** (fat beans) are distributed, a typical dish made with Saluggia beans, pork, and rich seasonings, a tradition rooted in the Middle Ages[8][1]

- **9:30 AM**: Oath of the Magnificent Podestà in Piazza Castello, a ceremony emphasizing the civic aspect of the event

- **10:00 AM**: Visit of the Mugnaia, the General, and their entourages to the Castellazzo Bean Festival, a moment of communion between the symbolic authorities of the Carnival and the population[8]

- **10:30 AM**: Ceremony of the **Preda in Dora**, an ancient rite in which something is symbolically thrown into the Dora River as a tribute to the waters

- **11:30 AM**: Ceremony of the Prise du Drapeau (the "Taking of the Flag") in Piazza di Città[1]

- **1:00 PM**: Formation of the Throwing Carts in Corso Massimo d'Azeglio. The nine carts, drawn by magnificent caparisoned horses, are lined up in formation for battle[1][2]

- **2:00 PM**: Start of the Historical Procession March from Piazza di Città and **beginning of the Battle of Oranges**. The battle takes place in five main squares: **Piazza Ottinetti, Piazza di Città, Borghetto, Piazza del Rondolino, and Piazza Freguglia**. According to estimates, approximately **900 tons of oranges** are thrown during these three days, mainly from Sicily and Calabria, fruits not suitable for food consumption but still perfect for this traditional battle[9][10][1][2]

- **5:30 PM**: Generala in Piazza di Città, closing ceremony of the battle day

### Carnival Monday (March 3, 2025)

Monday is dedicated to the **Zappate degli Scarli**, a ritual charged with symbolic meaning related to fertility and ancient pagan customs:

- **9:30 AM**: Zappate degli Scarli. The General personally accompanies the designated married couples for each district to the place where a **scarlo** will be raised, a large wooden pole completely covered with dry heather that symbolically represents a wish for prosperity and fertility. Each couple tills the earth with pickaxes in a rite rooted in ancient agricultural traditions[6][2]

- **2:15 PM**: March of the Historical Procession on foot and official visit to the Orange Thrower Teams. The nine historical teams are visited by the General and the official company: **Tuchini del Borghetto, Mercenari, Diavoli, Pantere, Credendari, Scacchi, Arduini, Asso di Picche, and Morte**. Each team has a centuries-old history, its own identity, and friendly rivalry with the others[1]

- **5:30 PM**: Generala in Piazza di Città

### Shrove Tuesday (March 4, 2025)

Tuesday is the climax of the event, the moment of maximum battle intensity:

- **11:00 AM**: Orange Throwers' Palio in Piazza Ottinetti. Preliminary competition where orange throwers from different teams compete in throwing skills[2]

- **1:00 PM**: Final formation of the Throwing Carts in Corso Massimo d'Azeglio

- **2:00 PM**: Start of the final Historical Procession March and final day of the **Battle of Oranges**[1][2]

- **5:45 PM**: Solemn award ceremony for the Foot Orange Thrower Teams and Throwing Carts in Piazza di Città, with recognition of winning teams and best fighters[1]

- **8:00 PM**: Burning of the Parish Scarli. The procession goes to the various parishes (San Maurizio, Sant'Ulderico, and San Lorenzo) to burn the scarli raised on Monday. The fire consuming these wooden poles symbolically marks the transition from the festive carnival season to the austere Lent

### Ash Wednesday (March 5, 2025)

The last day of Carnival is dedicated to a moment of spiritual and culinary transition:

- **11:00 AM**: Traditional distribution of **polenta and cod** organized by the Croatia Committee in Piazza Lamarmora. This ancient custom dates back to the **second half of the 16th century**, when the Counter-Reformation established the obligation of fasting and "eating lean" on the first day of Lent. On this occasion, approximately **1,200 kg of polenta, 900 kg of cod, and 1,100 kg of onions** are prepared. The Mugnaia, the General, and the procession, now in civilian clothes, visit the Committee to taste this traditional dish, marking the symbolic detachment from the carnival period towards the Lenten period[11][12][2]

## The Battle of Oranges: how it works

### General organization

The **Battle of Oranges** is a strictly regulated event, despite the appearance of chaos and medieval battle. Contrary to what one might think, it is not an event open to anyone who wishes to participate spontaneously. **Only people regularly registered as Orange Throwers with one of the nine historical teams can participate in throwing oranges**. Registrations must be made in advance at the headquarters of the team of choice.[13][14]

The **nine historical teams** of the city are:[1]

1. **Tuchini del Borghetto** - with throwing zone at Borghetto
2. **Mercenari** - with throwing zone at Piazza del Rondolino
3. **Diavoli** - historic rivals of the Tuchini
4. **Pantere** - well-known city team
5. **Credendari** - representatives of historical merchant classes
6. **Scacchi** - known for their checkered emblem
7. **Arduini** - from the Arduinic tradition of the city
8. **Asso di Picche** - one of the oldest teams
9. **Morte** - the most feared team, with the skull symbol[14]

Each team has a history spanning centuries, its own coat of arms, traditional uniforms, and a firm identity that distinguishes it from the others.

### Battle rules

The Battle of Oranges takes place in the **five throwing squares** of the city: Piazza Ottinetti (most central square), Piazza di Città (main square where the Credendari are located), Borghetto (Tuchini zone), Piazza del Rondolino (Mercenari zone), and Piazza Freguglia.[13][9][1]

The **foot orange throwers**, representing the people in revolt, position themselves in their assigned squares and maintain a **static position without protection**, relying on agility, tactics, and speed of movement. They wear no armor or protection (apart from the traditional red Phrygian cap), and the bruises on their faces at the end of the three days of battle are considered by participants as a badge of honor, proof of their dedication and ardor in defending their team's honor.[15][14]

The **throwing carts**, drawn by majestic caparisoned horses, are manned by professional throwers who launch oranges against the ground-level orange throwers. The riders and throwers on the carts wear **heavy armor and full helmets** for their protection, as they are exposed to counter-attacks from orange throwers who in turn launch oranges upward trying to hit the carts. Orange throwing is permitted exclusively in Piazza Freguglia according to official rules.[15][6][9]

Each cart takes an average of about **5-8 minutes** to cross a square during its battle turn, and the carts follow each other with entries every 5 minutes or so, creating a frenetic and continuous rhythm of confrontations. **On average, each throwing cart uses about 240 euros worth of oranges per day** in terms of volume and quantity, for a total of approximately **900 tons over the three days**.[16][14]

### The spirit of battle

Despite the intensity and violence of the orange throwing, the Battle of Oranges is governed by **unwritten rules inspired by mutual respect and protection of the horses' health**. It is not permitted to throw oranges at the horses themselves, only at the riders and carts. At the end of the three days of battle, the foot orange throwers recognize the valor of those on the carts and vice versa, in a ritual of reconciliation and mutual respect after having thrown "without holding back".[14]

The battle is accompanied by **music, chanting, and an atmosphere of great enthusiasm** that transforms the squares of Ivrea into arenas of a celebration that unites sport, history, legend, and community. It is a modern chivalric duel where physicality and tradition meet.

During Carnival days, **the historic center of Ivrea is completely closed to vehicular traffic**. All parking during the Carnival is **free**, although you need to walk a bit from the parking area to the center.[20]

**Recommended parking**:[20]
- Piazza del Mercato
- Ivrea Cemetery (parking on the grass is also allowed)
- Piazza Pistoni and multi-story car park

Traffic is regulated through **temporary interruptions managed by the Municipal Police**, and alternative routes and access-controlled areas are provided. A paper map with all access information is available at the paid entrances.[13]

### Safety tips and behavior

To ensure a safe experience respectful of tradition, it is essential to follow these rules:[13]

- **Always wear the red Phrygian cap**: This traditional hat identifies spectators and symbolically protects them from orange throws during the battle. It can be purchased in the city during the Carnival days[5][6][13]

- **Keep a safe distance from the horses**: Do not get too close to the magnificent horses pulling the carts, so as not to frighten them[13]

- **Do not cross the Historical Procession**: The processions move according to a precise schedule; do not interfere with their routes[13]

- **Do not enter Battle areas without protecting your face with an arm**: If you approach the battle area, protect your face because oranges are thrown with force[13]

- **Do not lean on the protective nets**: These delimit the battle areas and should not be used as support[13]

- **Do not throw oranges at horses or unauthorized riders**: Only registered orange throwers can throw oranges according to the rules[13]

- **Wear comfortable clothing and non-slip shoes**: The ground will become slippery due to orange pulp and juices[20][13]

- **Strollers and prams are strictly prohibited in battle areas**: Via Palestro, Piazza Ottinetti, Piazza di Città, and Borghetto are no-stroller zones[13]

- **Prohibited items**: Weapons, dangerous materials, sprays, fireworks, sharp or cutting objects, sticks (except umbrellas and medical devices), bicycles (except for people with disabilities), beverages in glass containers or cans, suitcases, trolleys or carts (except for those with accommodation reservations)[13]

## What to eat at the Ivrea Carnival

The food and wine tradition of the Ivrea Carnival is an integral part of the event itself, passed down from generation to generation according to secret recipes guarded by local communities.

### Faseuj grass (fat beans)

The **faseuj grass** (in Canavese dialect means "fat beans") are the most iconic and celebrated dish during the Ivrea Carnival. This recipe has origins lost in the Middle Ages, when Saluggia beans (a renowned local variety) were considered a precious food and were distributed free to the city's needy as alms.[11]

The preparation of faseuj grass is a colossal undertaking of coordination and dedication. During the 2025 Carnival, approximately **12 quintals (1,200 kg) of beans** will be prepared by the community. The process begins a week before, when:[11]

- The beans are soaked overnight to soften
- **400 kg of preive (pork rinds)** are cleaned, cut, and breaded in a "secret mixture" by expert volunteers
- Everything cooks in enormous aluminum pots for hours

The result is a rich and hearty dish composed of boiled beans, pork, bacon, salami, wine, bread. Approximately **25,000 bowls** are filled and distributed free during the Carnival, of which **600 portions are specifically destined for the city's poor**. Distribution begins the night before the faseuj day and continues until noon.[11]

### Polenta and cod

While the faseuj represent the "fat" of Carnival, the **polenta and cod** represents the moment of transition to the austerity of Lent. This traditional dish is consumed on **Ash Wednesday** (March 5, 2025) and is a symbol of Christian spirituality and penance.[12][11]

The custom dates back to the **second half of the 16th century**, when the Counter-Reformation established the obligation of fasting and "eating lean" on the first day of Lent. Over the centuries, the tradition has been preserved and has become a moment of civic and spiritual communion.[12][11]

For Ash Wednesday 2025, the **Croatia Committee** will prepare:[12]
- **1,200 kg of polenta** (cooked in the traditional method with vegetable broth and butter)
- **900 kg of cod** (high quality, previously placed in salted water)
- **1,100 kg of onions** (peeled and cut by the skilled hands of ten volunteer women)

Preparation begins Monday evening with cooking that continues **non-stop for 24 hours** divided into three shifts. The polenta is prepared directly in the square on Wednesday, creating a festive atmosphere even in this moment of spiritual reflection. Approximately **400 portions** are distributed in less than three hours.[12]

### Mulled wine and other specialties

During all Carnival days, in every corner of the city you can taste **mulled wine** (vin brulè - hot spiced wine) prepared by the district associations. This hot drink is perfect for warming up between one battle and the next during the March days that can still be cold and humid.[11]

Additionally, in the **Orange Village** (food and wine area set up in Piazza Ottinetti from 11:00 AM to 5:00 PM), visitors can discover and taste **flavors of Canavese**, local specialties, Piedmontese wines, and typical regional products. It's an opportunity to savor authentic Piedmontese cuisine in a festive and convivial setting.[8][1]

## Ivrea beyond the carnival: what to visit in the city

Ivrea, even outside the carnival period, is a city rich in history, culture, and tourist interest. If you arrive a few days before or stay a few days after the Carnival, there are numerous attractions to explore.

### The Castle of Ivrea

The **Castle of Ivrea**, also known as the **"Castle of the Red Towers"**, is the emblem of the city. This **medieval castle with a trapezoidal plan** was built in **1358 by Amedeo VI of Savoy**, Duke of Piedmont. The structure is characterized by **four cylindrical corner towers 34 meters high** in red brick color that rise magnificently in the urban landscape.[27][28][7]

The castle is located in the heart of the historic center and is surrounded by the main streets where the Battle of Oranges also takes place. From inside the castle, you can enjoy **splendid panoramic views** of Ivrea and its surroundings. The monument was celebrated by the poet Giosuè Carducci and is open to visitors throughout the year.[27]

### The Cathedral of Santa Maria Assunta

The **Cathedral of Ivrea**, dedicated to Santa Maria Assunta, is an architectural masterpiece of great importance. This majestic building was constructed in the **4th century AD on the remains of an ancient Roman temple** dedicated to the god Apollo. The cathedral combines architectural elements from various historical periods, reflecting the different eras in which it was built and renovated.[7]

The interior of the Cathedral is decorated with evocative frescoes and contains notable works of religious art. It is a place of great spirituality where you can understand the profound religious and cultural heritage of Ivrea.[7]

### The Tecnologic@mente Museum Laboratory

Ivrea is closely linked to the history of **Olivetti**, the technological innovation company that transformed the city and had a profound impact on 20th-century Italian industrial history. The **Tecnologic@mente Museum Laboratory** is a fascinating place where you can discover and touch:

- **Historic typewriters** that revolutionized the way of working
- **Innovative prototypes** that represented the future of technology
- **Historical documents** that tell the rise and importance of Olivetti
- **Industrial design objects** that demonstrate the company's commitment to combining beauty and functionality

The museum also has an **educational laboratory** where children and young people can learn through games and interactive experiments.[27]

### MAAM - Open Air Museum of Modern Architecture

The **MAAM (Open Air Museum of Olivetti Modern Architecture)** is a **two-kilometer route** that can be visited independently and free of charge at any time. This "open-air museum" showcases the **architecturally significant buildings built by Olivetti** in the post-war period:

- Buildings dedicated to social services
- Research and development facilities
- Production buildings
- Civilian housing for employees

Walking along the route, you can appreciate **Olivetti's commitment to architecture, industrial design, urban planning, and advertising graphics**. Each building represents a phase of the Olivetti corporate philosophy that united enterprise and culture, production and beauty, profit and solidarity.[28][7]

### Masino Castle

Just over **20 minutes from Ivrea**, **Masino Castle** represents one of the most important architectural and historical treasures of the Canavese region. Managed by the **Italian Environment Fund (FAI)**, the complex includes the castle, a magnificent park, and the carriage museum.[28]

The castle was built in the **second half of the 11th century** and became the property of Pietro Masino in 1070, then remained inhabited by the **Counts Valperga di Caluso for ten centuries**. The interior rooms are decorated with **splendid frescoes**, fine furniture, and house the **Carriage Museum** with historical specimens of great value. From the castle you can dominate the entire Canavese landscape: from the hills of the Caluso lands to Lake Viverone, from the Moraine Ridge to the Eporediese to Valchiusella.[28]

### Lakes and nature

Ivrea is surrounded by beautiful lake nature. **Lake Sirio**, the largest and best known, is swimmable and equipped, with access to the nearby **Dancing Lands** (a former peatland with elastic ground that sways under visitors' steps) and the ancient **Roman Aqueduct**.[28]

For those who love hiking and nature, the **Five Lakes Park** offers evocative routes such as the Lake Campagna Path, which offers beautiful views of San Giuseppe Castle, Mombarone, the Serra, and the village of Chiaverano.[28]

## Conclusion: experiencing the Ivrea Carnival

The **Historic Carnival of Ivrea** represents much more than a simple popular festival: it is an immersive experience that unites **history, legend, tradition, community, and freedom**. The medieval origins intertwined with the heroic figure of Mugnaia Violetta, the spectacular Battle of Oranges with nearly a ton of fruit thrown, the ancient rituals of the scarli, the bean festivals, and the polenta of Ash Wednesday: everything converges to create a unique event in the world.[3][4][5][6]

For **Turin university students**, the Ivrea Carnival is an extraordinary opportunity for cultural learning and fun at a short distance from the city. With the train reachable in less than 50 minutes and with a ticket cost under 15 euros, it is a perfect destination for a weekend excursion. The **Piemove** subscription can help reduce urban transport costs to reach the station.[23][24][22][21]

For **Italian and international tourists**, the Carnival offers an authentic immersion in Piedmontese culture, an opportunity to understand the historical roots of Italy and participate in celebrations that are rooted in centuries of tradition. The dates of **March 1-5, 2025** should be marked in red on the calendar for those who wish to experience this extraordinary event.[2][1]

Put on your red Phrygian cap, prepare to dodge oranges, savor the faseuj grassi and authentic flavors, immerse yourself in the festive crowd and the living history of this extraordinary city.
