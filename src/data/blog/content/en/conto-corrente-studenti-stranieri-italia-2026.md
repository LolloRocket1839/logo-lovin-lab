# Bank Account for International Students in Italy 2026: Complete Guide

> **TL;DR:** The best strategy for an international student just arrived in Turin: open a Revolut account immediately (free, Italian IBAN, 100% online setup in 10 minutes) for immediate needs. Then, within the first month, open an Intesa Sanpaolo XME account (free under 35) or Widiba for a full traditional Italian bank account. Use Wise for international transfers.

---

Opening a bank account is one of the first practical necessities for an international student in Italy. You need it to receive funds from home, pay rent, access EDISU scholarships, and manage daily expenses. The good news: since late 2024, with Revolut opening its Italian branch, the process has become much simpler even for those without Italian residency.

## General requirements

Each bank has its own specific requirements. The most commonly requested documents are:

- **Passport or valid ID** (mandatory for everyone)
- **Codice fiscale** (tax code — almost universally required. Get it before opening any account)
- **Permesso di soggiorno** (residence permit) or receipt of application (required by most traditional banks for non-EU students)
- **Proof of address in Italy** (not always mandatory for digital banks)
- **University enrollment letter** (often required for student-specific accounts)

**Key distinction:** resident accounts offer more services and better rates, but require Italian residency (anagrafe registration) or a residence permit. Non-resident accounts are more accessible immediately but have reduced services and often higher costs.

## Bank comparison: the complete 2026 guide

### Revolut — The immediate choice for international students

- **Monthly fee:** €0 (Standard plan)
- **IBAN:** Italian (IT) — since December 2024/January 2025
- **Italian residency required:** No (just an Italian address)
- **Opening:** 100% online, app, ~10 minutes
- **Documents:** passport + codice fiscale + address
- **Standard limits:** free ATM withdrawals up to €200/month, free currency exchange up to €1,000/month

The most important update in recent years for international students. Since December 2024/January 2025, Revolut opened its Italian branch and now issues Italian IBANs (IT prefix) to all new clients in Italy. This eliminates the historic "IBAN discrimination" problem (many Italian payers refused non-Italian IBANs). Revolut is now the top choice for anyone needing a working account from day one.

### Intesa Sanpaolo XME Account — The complete free Italian bank account

- **Monthly fee:** €0 for under 35
- **IBAN:** Italian (IT)
- **Italian residency required:** Yes for online opening; no for in-branch opening with residence permit
- **Opening:** online (with SPID/CIE) or in branch (with physical documents)
- **Branches in Turin:** >40
- **SEPA transfers:** free

The most recommended account for international students who want a complete Italian banking experience. Free for under 35, extensive branch and ATM network, utility direct debit services, access to mortgages and financial products in the future. The in-branch procedure requires residence permit (or receipt), codice fiscale, passport, and university letter.

### Widiba (Banca Widiba) — Digital, free under 30

- **Monthly fee:** €0 under 30
- **IBAN:** Italian (IT)
- **Italian residency required:** Yes for online opening (requires Italian digital identity or video identification)
- **Opening:** online or via app
- **Strengths:** modern interface, excellent app, integrated investments

### N26 — For students who prefer English

- **Monthly fee:** €0 (Standard plan)
- **IBAN:** German (DE) — not Italian
- **Italian residency required:** No
- **Opening:** online, ~8 minutes
- **Documents:** passport
- **Strengths:** fully English app, very popular among the Erasmus community

**Important note:** N26 issues German IBANs (DE). This can still cause problems in Italy with some payers who don't accept non-Italian IBANs (rent, utilities). Since 2023, restrictions on new account openings have been lifted.

### UniCredit — For those seeking a large traditional bank

- **Monthly fee:** ~€4/month (free online promotions available)
- **IBAN:** Italian (IT)
- **Italian residency required:** Yes (with residence permit)
- **Opening:** online or in branch
- **Strengths:** partnerships with many Italian universities, extensive ATM network

### Fineco Bank — For those who want banking and investments combined

- **Monthly fee:** €0 for the first 12 months, then €3.95/month (waivable with active usage)
- **IBAN:** Italian (IT)
- **Italian residency required:** Yes
- **Strengths:** integrated trading, excellent digital platform, unlimited free transfers

## The 3-phase strategy for international students

### Week 1 — Just arrived

1. Get your **codice fiscale** at the Agenzia delle Entrate (15 minutes, free)
2. Open a **Revolut account** (10 minutes, 100% online, Italian IBAN immediately)
3. Use Revolut for immediate payments, Italian SIM card, small expenses

### Within the first month

With your residence permit or receipt (non-EU students), open an account at a **traditional Italian bank**. Recommended: **Intesa Sanpaolo XME** (free under 35) in branch. Set up direct debits for rent and utilities on the Italian account.

### For international transfers

Always use **Wise** (wise.com) to receive funds from parents or foreign accounts. International transfers from traditional banks can cost €10–30 per transaction. Wise applies only the real interbank exchange rate with a 1–2% fee.

## Opening an account without a residence permit

**For EU students:** Italian banks are required to accept you as clients with just an EU ID card/passport. No residence permit is required.

**For non-EU students just arrived (without the permit yet):** Revolut is the immediate solution. It doesn't require a residence permit — just an address in Italy and the codice fiscale. Some banks also accept the receipt of the residence permit application (issued at the post office or Questura).
