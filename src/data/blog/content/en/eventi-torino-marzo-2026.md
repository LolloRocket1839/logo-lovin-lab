# March 2026 in Turin: the complete guide to every event

If you're thinking about visiting Turin in March, you picked the right month. A Vermeer arrives in Italy for the first time ever. Renato Zero fills Inalpi Arena. Seeyousound brings 68 films from around the world. Cherry blossoms light up the evenings at the Venaria Reale. And Subsonica celebrate thirty years in their home city.

This guide covers every confirmed event: concerts, festivals, exhibitions, markets, theatre, sport and nature. With dates, prices, venues and contacts.

## Exhibitions and museums

**Free first Sunday:** March 1, 2026: free entry to all state museums (Royal Museums, Gallerie d'Italia and more). **March 8 (International Women's Day):** free or 50% reduced entry for women at Museo Egizio, GAM, MAO, Palazzo Madama, MAUTO and Centro Storico FIAT.

### Vermeer: Woman in Blue Reading a Letter

March 5 to June 29, 2026 at Palazzo Madama, Piazza Castello. A historic first: Vermeer's celebrated "Woman in Blue Reading a Letter" travels from the Rijksmuseum in Amsterdam to Turin. First time ever in Italy. The painting inaugurates the new "Incontro con il capolavoro" series at Palazzo Madama. The exhibition includes contextual panels on Dutch Golden Age cartography and daily life.

**Tickets:** included in permanent collections ticket. Info: palazzomadamatorino.it | tel. 011 4433501 | ftm.prenotazioni@coopculture.it

### Chiharu Shiota at MAO: "The Soul Trembles"

This exhibition alone justifies a trip to Turin. MAO gave Chiharu Shiota free rein. The Japanese artist, famous for her interwoven thread installations that fill entire rooms, presents over 70 works across multiple halls. Curated by Mami Kataoka (director of the Mori Art Museum in Tokyo) and Davide Quadrio (MAO director).

This isn't a look-and-walk-past kind of show. You walk inside it. Red and black threads create labyrinths, corridors, human silhouettes suspended in the void. The most powerful installations:

- "The Key in the Hand": thousands of keys hanging from red threads raining down from the ceiling. Each key represents a memory.
- "In Silence": a burnt piano wrapped in black threads. Visual silence that speaks louder than any sound.
- "The Corridor": you walk through it, literally. Layers of material filtering the light.
- "Accumulation": suitcases, shoes, clothes. Real people's objects woven into massive compositions.

Drawings, photographs, sculptures and video art complete the exhibition. Go on Thursday if you can: the museum stays open until 20:00 and it's less crowded.

**Where and when:**
- Via San Domenico 11 (Palazzo Santo Stefano)
- Tuesday-Sunday 10:00-18:00, Thursday until 20:00
- Full €10, reduced €7 (under 26, over 65, students), free under 14
- Guided tours on Sundays at 15:00 (book ahead)
- +39 011 4436927 | info@maotorino.it

### Zanabazar: The Wisdom of the Steppes

February 27 to April 7, 2026 at MAO (Via San Domenico 11). Two extraordinary 17th-century works by the Mongolian tulku Zanabazar, shown in Europe for the first time. Co-produced with the Galleria Borghese in Rome.

### Futurism at the Royal Museums

If you like art that screams speed and rupture, this one's for you. At the Chiablese Halls of the Royal Museums, Italian Futurism is reinterpreted through four sections that show just how radical this movement was.

1. **Classic Futurism:** the bombs of Marinetti, Boccioni, Severini and Sant'Elia. The works that changed the rules.
2. **Aeropainting:** painting as if you were flying. Flight as obsession, speed as religion.
3. **Ceramics and sculpture:** Futurism didn't stop at canvas. Forms exploding in three dimensions.
4. **Period photography:** original manifestos, performance photos, documents capturing the energy of an avant-garde that wanted to burn down museums (and now ends up in them).

**Where and when:**
- Piazza Castello 191 (Royal Museums)
- Monday 14:00-19:00, Tuesday-Sunday 08:30-19:00
- Full €12, reduced €8 (18-25 years, over 65), free under 18
- +39 011 5611696 | info.museireali@beniculturali.it

### Orazio Gentileschi: "A painter on a journey"

Same building, different exhibition. Orazio Gentileschi was a Pisan painter from the 1600s who in his lifetime stood at the same level as Caravaggio, Rubens and Van Dyck. Almost nobody knows him. This exhibition corrects that.

Curated by Annamaria Bava and Gelsomina Spione, it traces the artist's journeys from Rome to London, via Genoa and the Savoyard courts. The centrepiece is the 1623 Annunciation, painted for the Duke of Savoy. Loans from the Louvre, the Prado and the Vatican Pinacoteca. It's rare to see works of this calibre together in one place.

The exhibition is part of the Milano Cortina 2026 Cultural Olympiad.

**Where and when:**
- Piazza Castello 191 (Chiablese Halls)
- +39 011 5611696 | info.museireali@beniculturali.it

## Markets, food and wine

### Salone del Vino (Piedmont Wine Fair)

February 28 to March 2, 2026. A showcase of Piedmont's finest producers. Meet winemakers, taste the best Barolo, Barbaresco and beyond.

### Fiera del Disco e del Fumetto

Vinyl, comics and collectibles at the covered market of Piazza Madama Cristina. Over 60 exhibitors from across Italy. Check guidatorino.com for exact dates.

### Gran Balon and Balon

Turin's legendary flea market in Borgo Dora. Gran Balon (monthly antiques): 2nd Sunday of the month, so March 8. Regular Balon (weekly vintage/second-hand): every Saturday. Piazza Borgo Dora / Via Borgo Dora.

### Mercato Sabaudo: Women's Day Edition

March 8, Mercato Centrale Torino, Spazio FARE, 10:00-19:00. Dedicated to women in wine and food entrepreneurship. Free entry.

### Chinese New Year: Year of the Fire Horse

Closing celebration: March 2. Music, dance and poetry at Teatro Valdocco and around the city.

### Dishes to try

- **Santena asparagus** with fried eggs and Fontina fondue. The taste of Piedmontese spring on a plate.
- **Piedmontese mixed fry** spring version: with artichokes, asparagus and fresh vegetables.
- **Agnolotti del plin:** fresh pasta filled with meat, herbs and parmesan. The dish every Turin restaurant makes its own way.
- **Finanziera:** ancient recipe with veal offal, sweetbreads, liver and mushrooms. Not for everyone, but those who love it never go back.

### Where to eat

- **Quadrilatero Romano:** historic trattorias and traditional restaurants. Steps from MAO. Exhibition in the morning, lunch here, tram to the theatre in the evening.
- **[San Salvario](/blog/san-salvario-guida-studenti):** international cuisine, young venues, sunset aperitifs.
- **Piazza Vittorio Veneto:** aperitifs overlooking the Po and the hills. Turin's most beautiful spot at sunset.
- **Vanchiglia:** traditional osterias and authentic wine bars. The bohemian atmosphere you won't find anywhere else.

For a full guide to affordable spots, read [where to eat in Turin on a budget](/blog/dove-mangiare-torino-studenti).

## Nature and outdoor activities

### Cherry blossom season at the Venaria Reale

March 21 to April 6, 2026 (approximate dates, weather-dependent). The 2025 edition drew over 105,000 visitors. This year's new feature: evening openings ("Una sera sotto i ciliegi in fiore") from 19:00-23:00 every day including Mondays. 100 cherry trees illuminated by 100 light points recreate the Japanese yozakura tradition of night-time hanami.

**Where:** Giardini della Reggia di Venaria, Piazza della Repubblica 4, Venaria Reale (10 km from Turin centre).

**Tickets:** Day €6 | Evening €8 | Combined €10 | Online reservation mandatory at lavenaria.it. Capacity limited each day.

### Castello del Valentino: free guided tours

Every Saturday from February 28 to March 28, 10:00-13:00. Explore the UNESCO-listed Valentino Castle's noble rooms, the Sala delle Colonne and the ground-floor chapel. Led by Politecnico di Torino experts. Max 2 children under 12 per adult (no booking needed for them).

**Book at:** castellodelvalentino.polito.it | Entrance: Viale Pier Andrea Mattioli 39.

### Parco del Valentino

Sixty hectares of green along the Po. After months of grey, the park fills with people. Jogging on dedicated paths, picnics in equipped areas, walks along the river. The perfect place to decompress between an exhibition and a concert.

### Cycling along the Po

The cycle path along the Po covers over 50 km in the Turin area. Rent a bike with ToBike, Dott or Lime. For advanced routes, read our [cycling guide for Turin](/blog/cicloturismo-avanzato-torino).

Three recommended routes in March:

1. **Murazzi, Parco del Valentino, Borgo Medievale (5 km):** easy, 30 minutes. Perfect for warming up with panoramic stops.
2. **Turin centre, Moncalieri, Superga (25 km):** medium, 2-3 hours. The climb to Superga rewards you with a view you won't forget.
3. **Complete hill circuit (50 km):** hard, 5-6 hours. Full loop for serious cyclists.

## How to plan your Turin March

The calendar is packed. Here's how not to get lost in the details:

1. **Start from your interests.** Contemporary art? Shiota and Zanabazar at MAO, Electric Dreams at OGR. Classical art? Vermeer at Palazzo Madama, Futurism and Gentileschi at the Royal Museums. Live music? 19 concerts across Inalpi Arena, OGR and Hiroshima Mon Amour. Film and music? Seeyousound. Nature? Cherry blossoms at the Venaria.

2. **Book now.** Subsonica are already sold out. Renato Zero and Giorgia sell fast. Cherry blossoms at the Venaria require online booking.

3. **Think in zones.** The Quadrilatero Romano groups MAO, Royal Museums and the best restaurants. Piazza Castello concentrates Palazzo Madama, Teatro Regio and Carignano within walking distance. Inalpi Arena is a metro ride from Lingotto.

4. **Take advantage of free days.** March 1: free state museums. March 8: discounts and free entry for women.

5. **Use public transport.** The GTT daily pass costs €5.50 and gets you everywhere. For short distances, grab a sharing bike.

The perfect plan: exhibition in the morning, lunch in the neighbourhood, walk along the Po, concert or show in the evening.

March 2026 is the month Turin brings out its best. A Vermeer making its Italian debut at Palazzo Madama. 19 concerts spanning Renato Zero to Jethro Tull. The cultural programme this month is something few European cities can match. Check the calendars, book the tickets and come. To discover Turin throughout the year, read our guide to [Turin in every season](/blog/torino-ogni-stagione-turisti).
