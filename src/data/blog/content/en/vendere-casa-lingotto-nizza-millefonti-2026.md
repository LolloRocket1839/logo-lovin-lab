# Selling a flat in Lingotto and Nizza Millefonti in 2026: real prices, timelines, and when direct purchase makes sense

*Updated May 2026. Reference for owners of a 1- or 2-bedroom flat, or an inherited apartment, in the Lingotto / Nizza Millefonti / Borgo Filadelfia area.*

Selling a home in southern Turin today is harder than it looks on the listings sites. Ads sit online for six, eight, ten months. Agencies charge sellers 3% plus VAT, and usually charge the buyer the same. Preliminary contracts fall apart when the buyer's mortgage isn't approved. Meanwhile you keep paying IMU, condominium fees and utilities on a property you don't even use.

This guide tells you what to actually expect if you own an apartment in the Lingotto / Nizza Millefonti area, and in which cases a direct purchase like ours can make more sense than the traditional channel.

## Why Lingotto and Nizza Millefonti are not like the rest of Turin

Two features make this area's sales dynamic different.

First: the Molinette hospital cluster — with Sant'Anna, Regina Margherita, CTO, Dental School — is one of the largest in Italy. That means anyone who buys a flat here to rent out finds a solid tenant: medical residents, nurses, PhDs, with steady income. The property practically never sits empty.

Second: the building stock is dominated by 1960s and 1970s blocks built for FIAT workers. Many flats have passed through two or three generations and now sit with heirs, often not living in Turin. For them, selling means coordinating viewings remotely, dealing with expenses they didn't remember they had, and managing a slow negotiation without the ability to judge the offers.

## Real prices per square metre, May 2026

Benchmarks for the area, properties in average condition (liveable, cosmetic work needed, or small interventions):

- **Via Nizza, stretch 200–400**: €1,500–1,900 per m²
- **Via Spotorno, via Passo Buole, via Pio VII**: €1,400–1,700 per m²
- **Borgo Filadelfia / southern corso Sebastopoli**: €1,500–1,800 per m²
- **Corso Bramante / near Molinette entrance**: €1,700–2,100 per m² (hospital-proximity premium)
- **Northern Mirafiori**: €1,200–1,500 per m²

A 50 m² one-bedroom in Nizza Millefonti, liveable, is typically worth **€70,000–95,000**. A 70 m² two-bedroom in the same condition: **€105,000–135,000**. Renovation-ready takes off 20–30%.

Prices above the benchmark exist only for fully renovated flats, with lift, autonomous heating and parking. Prices below the benchmark almost always mean: high floor with no lift, flat-rate central heating, or structural work needed (electrical, windows, bathroom).

## Real selling timelines via a traditional agency

Data we collected from sellers who tried the traditional route first, for the Lingotto / Nizza Millefonti area in 2025:

- **Average time from mandate to deed**: 6–9 months
- **Average viewings before an offer**: 12–18
- **Probability the first accepted offer reaches the deed**: about 60%
- **Agency commission**: 3% + VAT from the seller (€3,294 on a €90,000 sale)
- **Extra marketing costs**: often passed to the seller (photography, floor plan, energy certificate)

Add the holding costs during the wait: IMU (if not primary residence), condominium fees, minimum utilities, possible waste tax, unplanned extraordinary maintenance. Over 8 months on a one-bedroom, that's easily €1,500–3,000 extra.

## When it makes sense to sell directly to us

A direct purchase from Jungle Rent isn't the right choice in every case. It is when one or more of these apply:

- **You inherited the flat and don't live in Turin**. Managing viewings, agents, buyers who change their mind is a practical and emotional burden. A single counterparty making you a written offer in 7 days simplifies all of it.
- **You need certain timelines**: you're buying another home, you need liquidity in a few months, you have a tax deadline. A private buyer with a mortgage adds 30–60 days of uncertainty every time.
- **The flat needs renovation** and you don't want to manage the work before selling. On the standard market, "needs renovation" stretches timelines significantly.
- **You want to zero out commission**. On an €85,000 one-bedroom, 3% is €2,550 plus VAT — often more than a month of saved salary.

It doesn't make sense (and we'll tell you) if the flat is already perfect, you already have a direct buyer in hand, or you have the time and patience to manage a months-long negotiation hoping to extract another €5,000.

## What we look at when we make an offer

For transparency, here are the main criteria. Not rigid rules but almost always in this order:

1. **Precise location** (street, number, distance from the Molinette hospital cluster or Politecnico Mirafiori)
2. **Real walkable square metres** (not commercial)
3. **Floor and presence of lift**
4. **Heating type** (autonomous > central with individual metering > flat-rate central)
5. **System condition** (electrical up to code? recent boiler?)
6. **One or two bathrooms**
7. **Exposure and natural light**
8. **State of repair** (liveable, cosmetic work, full renovation)
9. **Balcony, cellar, parking**
10. **Ordinary and approved extraordinary condominium expenses**

The offer is written, justified, and does not change at the deed except in serious undisclosed cases (unauthorised building work, serious hidden defects).

## Sale taxes: what you pay (little) and what we pay

For a private seller of a property owned for more than 5 years or used as primary residence: **capital gain not taxed**. The difference between sale price and historical purchase price doesn't trigger income tax.

What you pay:
- **Energy performance certificate (APE)**: €100–250
- **Any building amnesty** if there are cadastral discrepancies — variable cost, we verify before the offer
- **Notary expenses**: only for documents specific to you (powers of attorney, etc.); the deed notary is paid by the buyer

Registration tax, mortgage tax, cadastral tax, VAT on the deed, and the deed notary's fees are paid by us as buyers.
