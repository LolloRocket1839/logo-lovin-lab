# Tax relief for out-of-town students in Italy: 2026 guide {#intro}

Students and families keep asking us whether rent for a room or a bed can be deducted from Italian taxes. It can. Italian law grants a 19% IRPEF deduction on rent paid by out-of-town university students (art. 15 §1 lett. i-sexies TUIR, DPR 917/1986).

This guide is updated to the 2026 tax return (2025 tax year) and cites official sources only.

---

## 1. What it is worth in real money {#value}

The deduction removes 19% of the rent paid during the year from gross IRPEF, calculated on a maximum spend of 2,633 euro. The Italian Revenue Agency puts it plainly: out-of-town students deduct 19% of rent on a maximum of 2,633 euro per year, for a maximum deduction of 500.27 euro.

Maximum tax saving is therefore around **500 euro a year**, however high the rent.

The 2,633 euro cap applies **per taxpayer**, not per child and not per contract. One parent with two dependent children on two different contracts still shares a single 2,633 euro cap. Two parents with two dependent children and two separate contracts each get their own cap (Circular 34/E/2008; Circular 20/E/2011, answer 5.10).

The deduction only works against gross tax. Any excess is not refunded and cannot be carried forward (Circular 14/E/2023).

## 2. Which contracts qualify {#contracts}

This is the question we get most. Almost every regular, registered residential lease qualifies.

**Eligible contracts:**

1. **Leases signed or renewed under Law 431/1998**, meaning any registered contract on a residential unit. No student-specific contract is required: the free 4+4 (art. 2 §1), the 3+2 controlled rent (art. 2 §3), the transitional lease and the transitional student lease (art. 5 §§2 and 3) all work. Resolution 200/E/2008 confirms every residential lease falls under Law 431/1998.
2. **Contracts for a single room or a single bed**, provided they are regular and registered. This is the typical Jungle Rent case.
3. **Hospitality contracts and assignment deeds** with right-to-study bodies, universities, legally recognised colleges, non-profits and cooperatives.

**Not eligible:**

- **Subletting.** A sublease is not among the schemes listed by the rule, which cannot be read extensively (Circular 21/E/2010, answer 4.3, confirmed by Circular 14/E/2023). Renting a room from another tenant instead of the owner or operator kills the deduction.
- **Unregistered contracts.** No registration, no deduction.

The contract can be in the student's name or in the name of the parent or relative claiming the student as a dependant (Circular 34/E/2008).

With a joint contract, typical in a shared flat, rent is split pro rata. Only tenants who meet the requirements deduct, each within 2,633 euro.

## 3. Student requirements {#requirements}

All of them must apply together:

- **Enrolment on a degree course.** Higher Technical Institutes (ITS) and the new courses at conservatories and equivalent music institutes under DPR 212/2005 also qualify (Circular 20/E/2011, answer 5.3). Postgraduate courses are excluded: masters, PhDs and specialisation schools, in Italy and abroad. Public or private university makes no difference (Circular 14/E/2023).
- **Minimum distance.** The university must be in a different municipality from the student's residence, at least **100 kilometres** away and in a **different province**. Both conditions apply together. Distance is the shortest route on any existing rail or road connection: one connection of 100 km or more is enough (Circular 34/E/2008, answer 8.4). Where there is no railway line, road or mixed connections count (Circular 7/E/2018).
- **Mountain and disadvantaged areas: waiver gone.** The reduced 50 km threshold ended with the 2020 tax return. The 100 km and different province rules now apply in full.
- **Universities abroad.** Eligible only if the institution sits in an EU member state or an EEA state with active tax information exchange.

## 4. Who claims it and income limits {#who}

**Who claims.** The student directly, if they have enough income of their own. Otherwise the parent or relative, if the student is a dependant (art. 12 §2 and art. 15 §2 TUIR). A child is a dependant with total income up to 2,840.51 euro, raised to 4,000 euro up to age 24.

**Split between parents.** If the contract is in the name of a child dependent on both parents, the cost splits 50/50: each parent deducts on a maximum of 1,316.50 euro. A different split must be noted on the document. If one spouse is a dependant of the other, the latter takes the whole cost (Circular 34/E/2008).

**Income limits.** Three layers:

- *General rule (art. 15 §3-bis TUIR).* Full deduction up to 120,000 euro of total income, tapering to zero at 240,000 euro.
- *New from 2025 (art. 16-ter TUIR).* The 2025 Budget Law (L. 207/2024, art. 1 §10) sets an overall cap on deductible expenses above 75,000 euro of income. The cap is a base amount (14,000 euro between 75,000 and 100,000 euro of income; 8,000 euro above 100,000) times a coefficient tied to dependent children: 0.50 with none, 0.70 with one, 0.85 with two, 1.00 with more than two or with at least one child with certified disability (L. 104/1992). Student rent relief falls inside this cap. Healthcare costs, investments in start-ups and innovative SMEs, and mortgage or insurance charges signed before 31 December 2024 sit outside it. Guidance is in Circular 6/E of 29 May 2025. Total income is measured net of the main home and its appurtenances (art. 16-ter §6 TUIR).
- *The 260 euro reduction: 2024 only.* The flat reduction for incomes above 50,000 euro (Legislative Decree 216/2023, art. 2) applied to the 2024 tax year alone. For 2025 art. 16-ter TUIR replaces it.

Below 75,000 euro of income none of this bites: the deduction applies in full.

## 5. How to pay and what to keep {#documents}

**Traceable payments are mandatory.** Since 1 January 2020 the 19% deduction requires traceable payment (art. 1 §§679-680, L. 160/2019): bank or postal transfer, debit, credit or prepaid card, bank or cashier's cheque, postal slip, MAV, PagoPA, authorised e-money institutions. **Cash forfeits the deduction.**

**Only the rent share counts.** Excluded:

- the security deposit;
- building and heating charges, even if bundled into the rent;
- utilities;
- agency or brokerage fees.

If accessory costs are bundled into the rent, list them separately so the deductible share stays identifiable. In a hospitality contract, cleaning and meals with no separate price stay inside the deductible amount; charged separately, they fall outside.

**This is why Jungle Rent keeps rent separate from the service fee: the deductible share is clear from signature day.**

**Documents to keep:**

- a copy of the registered lease (or the hospitality contract / assignment deed);
- receipts showing the traceable payment method;
- the student's self-certification of enrolment and requirements, distance included.

The cost goes in form 730 (lines E8 to E10, code 18) or in Redditi PF (section RP).

## 6. Mistakes that cost you the 500 euro {#mistakes}

- Paying in cash or by untraceable top-ups.
- Signing a sublease without realising it.
- Accepting an unregistered contract to speed things up.
- Bundling rent and charges, so the deductible share cannot be identified.
- Keeping the contract but none of the payment receipts.
