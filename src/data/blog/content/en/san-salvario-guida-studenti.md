# San Salvario: the ultimate guide to Turin's most vibrant neighborhood for students

*Updated: November 2025 | Reading time: 10 minutes*

If you're looking for accommodation in Turin as a university student, you've probably already heard of San Salvario. It's the neighborhood where most international students live, where IED has its main campus, and where nightlife is among the liveliest in the city.

But is San Salvario really the right neighborhood for you? In this guide I'll explain everything you need to know, based on real 2025 data and testimonies from students who live there.

## Why San Salvario is so popular among students

**Approximately 15,000 students live in San Salvario**, making it the most student-populated neighborhood in Turin. But why?

### The numbers speak clearly

- **10 minutes walk** from central UniTO (Via Po, Palazzo Nuovo)
- **5 minutes walk** from IED main campus (Via San Secondo)
- **15 minutes walk** from Medicine and Physics faculties
- **20 minutes** from Campus Einaudi (tram 4 direct)
- **120+ ethnic restaurants** (the highest concentration in Turin)
- **60+ venues** between bars, pubs and clubs
- **Piazza Madama Cristina Market** twice a week (the cheapest in the city)

### The "community" factor

The truth is that San Salvario is popular because here you find other students. Many. Especially internationals (about 40% of Erasmus students choose this neighborhood). This means that:

- It's easy to make friends
- You always find someone to go out or study with
- Venues are designed for students (affordable prices, convenient hours)
- You feel less alone if you're away from home or foreign

## The different areas of San Salvario

San Salvario is not all the same. There are quieter areas, noisier areas, cheaper areas. Here's the practical map:

### Area 1: San Salvario "core" (Via Nizza - Via Madama Cristina)

**Characteristics:**
- The liveliest and most multicultural
- Many venues and restaurants
- Noisy in the evening/night (especially weekends)
- Prices: €360-480/month

**You'll like it if:** You love social life, noise doesn't bother you, you want to be in the center of the action

**It'll weigh on you if:** You need silence to study, you go to bed early, you're sensitive to noise

### Area 2: Parco Valentino side (Via Ormea - Lungo Po)

**Characteristics:**
- Quieter and greener
- Park at home (perfect for studying outdoors)
- Fewer venues but still connected
- Prices: €330-420/month

**You'll like it if:** You love running/sports, you want tranquility but stay in San Salvario

### Area 3: South San Salvario (beyond Via Madama Cristina)

**Characteristics:**
- The cheapest
- Very multicultural
- Market very close
- Less "instagrammable" but authentic
- Prices: €300-400/month

**You'll like it if:** You have a limited budget, you love ethnic food, you don't care about aesthetics

### Area 4: Porta Nuova side (Via San Secondo)

**Characteristics:**
- Very close to the station (convenient for commuters)
- IED campus in this area
- More "passing through"
- Prices: €370-500/month

**You'll like it if:** You study at IED, you're a commuter, you want to be near the center

## Pros and cons (the real ones)

### Pros

**University proximity:** If you study at central UniTO, IED, Medicine or Physics, you're 5-15 minutes walk. Zero time wasted, zero transport costs.

**Social life:** Easy to make friends, always something to do, international atmosphere.

**Cheap food:** The market and ethnic restaurants really save you money. Difference €60-100/month compared to the center.

**Student community:** You don't feel alone. You always find someone in your situation.

**Connections:** Even if you don't study nearby, you're still well connected with all of Turin.

### Cons

**Noise:** Some areas are very noisy at night (Via Nizza especially). If you're a light sleeper, choose internal streets.

**Variable accommodation quality:** There's everything. You need to visit well before signing.

**Competition for rooms:** It's the most requested neighborhood. If you search in September, you only find leftovers.

**Some areas still to be redeveloped:** Not all of San Salvario is "instagrammable". Parts of the neighborhood are still dated.

**Impossible parking:** If you have a car, forget it (or pay €60-80/month for a garage).

### Timing

**May-June:** Start searching. Maximum choice.

**July:** Still good. Normal prices.

### Red flags (scams to avoid)

❌ **Never pay before seeing:** If someone asks you for a deposit before the visit, it's 99% a scam.

❌ **Prices too low:** Room at €200/month in the heart of San Salvario? Too good to be true.

❌ **"I'm abroad, I'll ship the keys":** Classic scam. Block and report.

❌ **Immediate pressure:** "You have to decide today or I'll give it to others". Forget it.

### What to check during the visit

✅ **Natural light:** Essential not to get depressed in winter

✅ **Noise:** Visit the house in the evening to understand the real noise level

✅ **Utilities:** Ask explicitly what's included and what's not

✅ **Furniture:** What's there? What do you need to bring?

✅ **Roommates:** Ask to meet them before signing

✅ **Contract:** Must be registered. No registration = no protection.

### Documents you'll need

- ID card/passport
- Italian tax code
- University enrollment certificate
- Possibly: parental guarantee (pay slips)

## Is San Salvario the right neighborhood for you?

**YES, if:**
- You study at central UniTO, IED, Medicine or Physics
- You love social life and international atmosphere
- You want to save on food and transport
- Night noise doesn't bother you (or you choose quiet streets)
- You're looking for a strong student community

**NO (consider elsewhere), if:**
- You study at Politecnico (Cenisia is better)
- You need absolute silence (Crocetta or Borgo Po are better)
- You prefer "finished" and elegant neighborhoods (Centro is better)
- You have a very limited budget (Aurora is better)

## Frequently asked questions

**Is San Salvario safe?**
Yes, it's safe by big city standards. Like anywhere, normal caution at night is needed (don't show valuables, return in a group if possible). Thousands of students live there without problems. For a complete guide on safety in Turin and practical advice specifically for students, read our in-depth article on [Turin's safest neighborhoods](/blog/quartieri-sicuri-donne-torino).

**Single or double room better?**
Depends on budget. The double costs €200-300/month (save €100-150), but you have less privacy. For most students, the single is worth the extra cost.

**How much is the GTT subscription?**
€25/month (under 26) or €255/year. Includes unlimited metro, tram and bus.

**Where can I wash clothes?**
Many apartments have washing machines. Otherwise there are 3 self-service laundries in San Salvario (€3-5/wash).

**Can I park?**
It's difficult and expensive. San Salvario is almost all blue stripes (€1.50/hour) or ZTL. If you have a car, consider a more peripheral neighborhood or sell the car (transport works well).
