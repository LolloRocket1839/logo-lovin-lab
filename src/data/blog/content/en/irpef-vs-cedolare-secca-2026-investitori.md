# IRPEF vs Cedolare Secca (Flat Tax) 2026: When it Pays Off for Property Investors

> **TL;DR:** Italy's rental flat tax (cedolare secca) is advantageous in almost all scenarios in 2026. At 21% for free-market contracts: average saving of €500–900/year vs standard IRPEF. At 10% for regulated contracts (canone concordato): savings always exceed €1,000/year for those with employment income. The 2026 Budget Law increased rates only on short-term rentals (2nd property onwards: 26%). Long-term contracts are unchanged.

Rental taxation is the topic that generates most confusion among Italian small property investors. The end-of-2025 news about the cedolare secca increase made it even murkier. After the passage of the 2026 Budget Law (Law no. 199 of 30 December 2025, in force from 1 January 2026), the reality is simpler than it seems.

This guide cuts through the noise with real calculations for three typical investor profiles in the Turin student housing market.

## The 2026 rates: what actually changed

### Long-term contracts (>30 days): no changes

| Contract type | Cedolare secca rate 2026 | Change from 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Free market (4+4 years) | 21% | Unchanged |
| Regulated (canone concordato, 3+2 years) | 10% | Unchanged |
| Transitional and university student contracts | 10% | Unchanged |

### Short-term rentals (≤30 days): changes from 1 January 2026

| Number of properties | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1st property (owner's choice) | 21% | Including Airbnb/Booking.com |
| 2nd property | 26% | — |
| 3rd property onwards | Mandatory P.IVA | Absolute presumption of commercial activity |

The original Budget Bill proposed 26% on all short-term rentals including the first property. This was rejected in Parliament in November 2025. The final law maintains 21% on the first property. Platforms (Airbnb, Booking.com) must apply a 26% withholding from 1 February 2026.

### The 2026 IRPEF (income tax) brackets

| Bracket | Rate 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Up to €28,000 | 23% | Unchanged |
| €28,001–€50,000 | 33% | −2pp (was 35%) |
| Over €50,000 | 43% | Unchanged |

Under ordinary IRPEF, rental income is taxed on 85% of the annual rent (15% flat deduction for expenses) for free-market contracts. For canone concordato, the taxable base drops to 66.5% of rent (additional 30% deduction).

On top of IRPEF: regional surtax (1.23%–3.33%), municipal surtax (0.5%–0.9%), registration tax (2% of annual rent, split 50/50 with tenant), stamp duty (€16 per 4 pages of contract).

## Three real scenarios: cedolare secca vs IRPEF

### Scenario 1: mid-income employee + first rental property (free market)

**Profile:** Teacher, employment income €28,000/year. Single room in San Salvario, rent €480/month = €5,760/year. Free-market 4+4 contract.

**Cedolare secca 21%:**
€5,760 × 21% = **€1,210/year**

**IRPEF ordinary regime:**
- Taxable base: €5,760 × 85% = €4,896
- IRPEF (23% bracket): €4,896 × 23% = €1,126
- Surtaxes (~2%): €4,896 × 2% = €98
- Registration tax (owner's share, 50%): €5,760 × 2% × 50% = €58
- **Total ordinary regime: €1,282/year**

**Annual saving with cedolare secca: €72.** Modest, but the flat tax also eliminates all contract renewal filing requirements and annual registry declarations. At this income level, the real benefit is administrative simplicity.

### Scenario 2: upper-mid income professional + student flat (regulated contract)

**Profile:** Lawyer, employment income €45,000/year. Two-room apartment in Crocetta rented to a student on a regulated transitional contract, €700/month = €8,400/year.

**Cedolare secca 10%:**
€8,400 × 10% = **€840/year**

**IRPEF ordinary regime (canone concordato):**
- Taxable base: €8,400 × 66.5% = €5,586
- IRPEF (33% bracket): €5,586 × 33% = €1,843
- Surtaxes (~2%): €5,586 × 2% = €112
- Registration tax (owner's share): €8,400 × 2% × 70% × 50% = €59
- **Total ordinary regime: €2,014/year**

**Annual saving with cedolare secca: €1,174.** Strongly favorable. This is the optimal structure for student housing investment in Turin.

### Scenario 3: high-income manager + three-bedroom student flat (free market)

**Profile:** Manager, employment income €65,000/year. Three-bedroom flat in Vanchiglia rented to 3 students, total rent €1,350/month = €16,200/year.

**Cedolare secca 21%:**
€16,200 × 21% = **€3,402/year**

**IRPEF ordinary regime:**
- Taxable base: €16,200 × 85% = €13,770
- IRPEF (43% bracket): €13,770 × 43% = €5,921
- Surtaxes (~2.5%): €13,770 × 2.5% = €344
- Registration tax: €16,200 × 2% × 50% = €162
- **Total ordinary regime: €6,427/year**

**Annual saving with cedolare secca: €3,025.** Highly advantageous. The differential grows proportionally with employment income because the flat tax neutralizes the progressivity effect of IRPEF.

## The winning combination: canone concordato + 10% flat tax in Turin

For Turin's student housing market, the most tax-efficient structure is the **university student transitional contract with 10% cedolare secca**. Requirements:

1. Turin is classified as a high housing tension municipality. The preferential rate applies.
2. Rent must fall within the bands of the Turin Territorial Agreement (updated April 2024), which sets min-max €/m² ranges by OMI microzone.
3. The contract must receive an attestazione di conformità (bollinatura) from a signatory association (ANIAT, APE, ASPPI, etc.). Typical cost: €40–80.
4. Cedolare secca option must be made at contract registration. It cannot be changed retroactively.

The combined benefits of canone concordato + 10% cedolare secca in Turin:

- **Flat tax:** 10% (vs 21% free market)
- **IMU:** automatic 25% national reduction
- **Turin IMU** (resident or student tenant): effective rate 4.31‰ (vs 10.6‰ for free-market)
- **No registration tax, stamp duty, or IRPEF surtaxes**

## When cedolare secca may not be the best choice

**Large pending renovation deductions.** Income taxed under cedolare secca doesn't generate ordinary imposta lorda against which to apply renovation deductions (50% or 36%) or mortgage interest deductions. Verify with an accountant.

**Low-income pensioners.** Cedolare secca income enters the calculation for pension deductions, potentially raising the effective rate to or above 21%.

**Very high documented actual expenses.** Ordinary IRPEF allows deduction of actual expenses. Cedolare secca only allows the flat 15% deduction.

## The rule of thumb

If your employment income exceeds €20,000/year and you have no large pending deductions, cedolare secca almost always wins. For canone concordato at 10%, the advantage is structural in every scenario. Consult an accountant (commercialista) for your specific situation before signing a new contract. The consultation fee is tax-deductible.
