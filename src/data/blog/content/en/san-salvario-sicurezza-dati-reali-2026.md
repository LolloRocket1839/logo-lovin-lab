# San Salvario Turin: Safety and Real Data 2026 {#intro}

San Salvario divides opinions like no other neighborhood in Turin. Some call it dangerous. Others say it's the beating heart of the city. The data tells a different story from both versions.

This guide analyzes real crime numbers, how red zones work, and the state of safety in 2026. Everything is based on official sources: the Prefecture, the Interior Ministry, and the Rota Report.

---

## Crime in Turin: the big picture {#crime-turin}

The Province of Turin recorded 128,666 reported crimes in 2024. That's 5,828 reports per 100,000 residents. Sixth position nationally, with a 2.7% increase over 2023.

The first half of 2025 reversed the trend. Crimes dropped 4.9% compared to the same period in 2024.

Turin ranks first in Italy for vandalism (1,267 per 100,000). Second for cyber fraud (732 per 100,000). Third for muggings (56 per 100,000).

## San Salvario in the data {#data-san-salvario}

San Salvario is among the 10 neighborhoods with the highest crime concentration. It shares this with Falchera, Barriera di Milano, Porta Palazzo, Aurora, Vallette, Valdocco, and Rebaudengo.

The most frequent crimes in the neighborhood are drug dealing, theft, robbery, assault, and vandalism. In the first half of 2025, law enforcement conducted 92 fixed patrol operations. Result: 18 arrests and 40 formal charges.

The intersection of Via Berthollet and Via Saluzzo is the main drug dealing hotspot.

### The surprising finding from the Rota Report

San Salvario's actual danger level is classified as "intermediate." Lower even than areas perceived as safer, like Crocetta.

The gap between reputation and reality stems from the visibility of street-level dealing and nightlife disorder. Serious crimes are less frequent than people think.

## History and gentrification {#history}

The name comes from the church of San Salvatore di Campagna (1646). The neighborhood developed from 1851 on an orthogonal grid. The construction of Porta Nuova station (1860-1868) attracted working-class residents.

The neo-Moorish Synagogue (1884) and Waldensian Temple consolidated its multicultural identity. In the 1990s, San Salvario became a national symbol of urban decay and ethnic tension.

The turning point came with the 2006 Winter Olympics. The neighborhood transformed in two years into "Turin's coolest." Gentrification pushed rents up, displacing the most vulnerable residents to the outskirts.

Today San Salvario hosts North African, Sub-Saharan, Eastern European, and Chinese communities. Alongside students, young professionals, and long-time residents.

## Red zone: perimeter and how it works {#red-zone}

On January 27, 2025, Prefect Cafagna established "enhanced surveillance zones" (art. 2 TULPS).

**San Salvario perimeter:**
- North: Corso Vittorio Emanuele II
- South: Corso Marconi
- East: Via Madama Cristina (included)
- West: Via Nizza (included)

Police can order 48-hour removal of individuals with prior records for dealing, crimes against persons, theft, robbery, or weapons possession.

The measure was extended until January 31, 2026.

**Most critical streets:** Via Berthollet/Via Saluzzo (dealing epicenter), Via Sant'Anselmo, Via Nizza, Via Valperga Caluso.

## Parco del Valentino: nighttime safety {#valentino}

Drug dealing in the park concentrates around the synthetic football pitch, the 5th Pavilion area, the rock garden, and the hillock.

Viale Virgilio is a hotspot with illegal parking attendants who become dealers at night. The Corso Massimo d'Azeglio axis (trams 9 and 16) is the main distribution route.

There have been bottle assaults, knife robberies during graduation parties, and violent thefts. The municipality plans drone surveillance by Municipal Police.

**Practical tip:** avoid the park after closing time. Use the lit paths along the Po river.

## Nightlife: venues and hotspots {#nightlife}

The epicenter is Largo Saluzzo Paesio (Via Saluzzo/Via Baretti intersection). It fills up from Thursday evening, peaking after 10 PM.

**Main nightlife streets:** Via Saluzzo, Via Belfiore, Via Berthollet, Via Sant'Anselmo, Via Baretti, Via Madama Cristina.

**Key venues:** Dash Cocktail Bar (Largo Saluzzo), Gørilla (live music), Imbarchino, Rough San Salvario, Bottega Baretti, Life Torino (disco).

The crowd is mostly under 30. Friday draws a slightly older crowd. Saturday brings students from the suburbs.

In 2022 the municipality was ordered to compensate residents for nightlife disturbance. Many minimarkets now close at 9 PM.

## Municipal safety initiatives {#safety-initiatives}

- **23 new CCTV cameras** approved June 20, 2025 (cost €250,000). Installation by mid-2026 on Via Berthollet, Via Saluzzo, Via Sant'Anselmo, Via Goito, Via Galliari.
- **168 daily patrols** across the city. 1,181 arrests in the first half of 2025.
- **Project Argo:** hundreds of AI-powered cameras installed by previous administrations.
- **High Impact Operations** with joint forces and "Safe Streets" military patrols.
- **Permanent ban** on takeaway alcohol sales after 9 PM in nightlife zones.

## Student opinions {#opinions}

Opinions are polarized. VivoTorino.it describes San Salvario as "a miniature Turin." It recommends the southern area near Via Valperga Caluso for those seeking quiet.

ResidenzaUniversitarie.org highlights the "lively, multiethnic north" and the "quieter south towards Corso Bramante."

On Tripadvisor: "Controversial but interesting. Revalued, among Turin's top youth nightlife hubs."

## Services and transport {#transport}

The neighborhood is served by metro M1 (Porta Nuova, Marconi, Nizza, Carducci-Molinette stops). Trams 9, 16, and 4. Numerous bus lines (18, 67, 5, 24, 35).

**Night buses:** S5 Viola and N8 Oro cover San Salvario.

**Services:** Piazza Madama Cristina market, Teatro Colosseo, Molinette and Valdese hospitals, Faculty of Architecture (Castello del Valentino), departments of Medicine, Chemistry, and Physics.
