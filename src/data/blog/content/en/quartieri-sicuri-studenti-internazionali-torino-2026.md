# Safe neighborhoods for international students in Turin 2026

> **TL;DR:** Turin is the third most walkable city in the world (7-minute average to essential services) and scores 48.2/100 on Numbeo's perceived safety index. For international students, the safest neighborhoods are Crocetta, Cenisia, Santa Rita, and Campidoglio. San Salvario and Vanchiglia are lively but require caution at night. Aurora is the cheapest option but carries higher risks.

---

Every year over 90,000 students attend Turin's universities. A growing number comes from abroad thanks to Erasmus+ programs (UniTo has over 1,000 exchange agreements) and international courses at Politecnico and ESCP. For anyone arriving from abroad, the first question after admission is always the same: which neighborhood is safe to live in?

This guide answers with real data, not tourist impressions, updated to Q1 2026.

## Safety in Turin by the numbers {#safety-numbers}

Turin ranks 6th nationally in the 2025 Crime Index by Il Sole 24 Ore, behind Milan, Rome, Florence, Bologna, and Rimini. Ministry of the Interior data for 2024 shows a 2.7% increase in reported crimes. However, preliminary H1 2025 data from the Prefecture indicates a 4.9% decline.

Good news for international students: the Turin Prefecture recorded a −9% drop in predatory crimes (pickpocketing, bag-snatching) and a −9% drop in home burglaries in the first half of 2025. The 168 daily patrols are concentrated in areas with high student foot traffic.

On Numbeo, the crowdsourced data platform most consulted by Erasmus students, Turin scores 31.27/100 for concern about ethnicity or gender-based attacks. That's classified as "low." An important figure for those arriving from non-European countries. The main perceived problem is drug dealing (65.41, "high"), concentrated in specific zones and not part of daily student life.

## Red zones: what they are and where they are {#red-zones}

The Turin Prefecture has designated certain areas as Red Zones through urban safety ordinances. This does not mean they are inaccessible or dangerous for residents. It means that reinforced controls and anti-degradation measures are concentrated there.

Current red zones include: San Salvario, Barriera di Milano, Aurora, Vanchiglia, Piazza Bengasi, Giardini Maiocco, Corso Principe Oddone.

The designation primarily reflects drug dealing and urban degradation, not violent crimes against residents. Many students live in these zones without issues, but it's important to choose them knowingly.

## Recommended neighborhoods for international students {#recommended}

### Crocetta — The safe choice near Politecnico {#crocetta}

- **Safety:** ★★★★★ — Not a red zone, no recurring crime reports
- **Average single room rent 2026:** €400–600/month
- **Average rent €/m²:** €12.70/m² (+0.47% yearly — the lowest growth in the city)
- **Ideal for:** Politecnico di Torino students, ESCP, students who prefer quiet

Crocetta is the most sought-after neighborhood by international students with a mid-to-high budget. Elegant, quiet, with plenty of green space (Parco Valentino is a 10-minute walk). The contained rent growth (+0.47%) makes it stable for those seeking multi-year contracts.

Almost no nightlife means almost no nighttime problems. Direct access to Politecnico via bus lines 67 and 18 or by bicycle. Supermarkets, pharmacies, post offices, banks: everything is available.

**Weakness:** it's the most expensive neighborhood in this selection.

### Cenisia / Cit Turin — The best value for money {#cenisia}

- **Safety:** ★★★★☆ — Not a red zone, residential
- **Average single room rent 2026:** €350–480/month
- **Average rent €/m²:** ~€9.50–10.00/m²
- **Ideal for:** Politecnico, IED, IAAD, IUSTO students; those seeking good price-safety ratio

Cenisia is the favorite neighborhood for Politecnico students who want to save without sacrificing safety. Walking distance to campus: 15–20 minutes. Residential atmosphere, local shops, little nightlife.

Cit Turin (northern part) is slightly more central and a bit livelier. Excellent metro (Fermi) and bus connections. Particularly recommended for female international students looking for a quiet neighborhood even in the evening.

### Santa Rita — Quiet, affordable, well-connected {#santa-rita}

- **Safety:** ★★★★☆ — Residential, no concerns
- **Average single room rent 2026:** €300–450/month
- **Average rent €/m²:** €10.01/m² (+5.48% yearly)
- **Ideal for:** Economics students (UniTo Faculty of Economics), students on a tight budget

Santa Rita is the choice for those who want to pay as little as possible without giving up safety. A family-oriented neighborhood with full services and a strong presence of elderly residents and families. A positive indicator for perceived safety.

The downside: far from the main university campuses (bus required) and less socially vibrant. Rent growth at +5.48% signals a neighborhood undergoing revaluation.

### Campidoglio / San Donato — Semi-central and underrated {#campidoglio}

- **Safety:** ★★★★☆ — Quiet, semi-central
- **Average single room rent 2026:** €350–480/month
- **Average rent €/m²:** ~€10.50/m²
- **Ideal for:** students at all universities, those who want to be near the center without paying for it

Campidoglio is often overlooked in international students' apartment searches, as they tend to focus on San Salvario or Crocetta. It's semi-central (15 minutes on foot from Porta Nuova), well served by bus and metro, residential yet not anonymous.

No problematic designations, good social mix. An excellent lesser-known alternative highly appreciated by those who live there.

## Popular but choose carefully {#choose-carefully}

### San Salvario — The heart of student life {#san-salvario}

- **Safety:** ★★★☆☆ — Red zone; high daytime vitality, nighttime risks
- **Average single room rent 2026:** €350–500/month
- **Average rent €/m²:** €13.32/m² (+2.30% yearly)
- **Ideal for:** students seeking intense social life, near Politecnico and Valentino

San Salvario is Turin's most international and multicultural neighborhood. It hosts students from around the world, bars, ethnic restaurants, markets, and Parco del Valentino. It's the top choice for those arriving in Turin with an Erasmus spirit.

Its red zone designation reflects the concentration of drug dealing in specific corners (especially the area around Porta Nuova and Parco del Valentino at night), not the daily reality of residents.

**Practical tip:** avoid the arcades on Via Nizza at night, don't linger alone in parks after 11 PM, prefer internal streets over the park periphery. During the day it's lively and safe.

### Vanchiglia — Close to Campus Einaudi, highly sought-after {#vanchiglia}

- **Safety:** ★★★☆☆ — Red zone; nighttime degradation from nightlife
- **Average single room rent 2026:** €350–500/month
- **Average rent €/m²:** €12.21/m² (+6.64% — highest growth in the city)
- **Ideal for:** UniTo/Campus Einaudi students, those who love an artistic atmosphere

Vanchiglia is undergoing strong gentrification. Rents are up +6.64% yearly, the highest figure in Turin. Very close to Campus Einaudi (UniTo), it has become one of the favorite destinations for humanities and social sciences students.

The red zone designation mainly concerns the nightlife around Piazza Santa Giulia at night. During the day and evenings until 10–11 PM, it's a pleasant and quiet neighborhood.

### Aurora — The budget option for those who accept higher risks {#aurora}

- **Safety:** ★★☆☆☆ — Red zone; more visible street crime
- **Average single room rent 2026:** €250–400/month
- **Average rent €/m²:** €10.74/m² (+4.88% yearly)
- **Ideal for:** students on a very tight budget; those who already know Turin

Aurora is the neighborhood undergoing the greatest transformation in Turin. Lungo Dora hosts markets, bars, and trendy venues, but certain areas (especially Lungo Dora Napoli at night) have issues with drug dealing and degradation.

Not recommended as a first choice for international students in their first year in Italy. For those in their second or third year who know the city and seek the lowest semi-central prices, it can work. Choose the specific building carefully.

## Practical tips for international students {#tips}

1. **Visit before signing.** Listing photos don't always reflect the surrounding environment. An in-person visit (or a video call with someone who knows the neighborhood) is essential.

2. **Prefer apartments in buildings with a concierge or video intercom.** The presence of building services significantly improves perceived safety.

3. **Evaluate transport connections.** With the GTT Under 26 pass at €25/month (or free with Piemove if your ISEE is ≤ €85,000 and you're enrolled at UniTo/PoliTo), even more peripheral neighborhoods like Santa Rita become accessible.

4. **Avoid carrying excess cash.** Pickpocketing on public transport and in squares is the most common crime in areas with high tourist and student concentration.

5. **Emergency number: 112.** It works throughout Italy, in any language.
