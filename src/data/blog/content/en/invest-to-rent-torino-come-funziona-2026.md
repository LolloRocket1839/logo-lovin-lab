# Invest-to-rent: property exposure in Turin without buying a whole flat {#intro}

Buying a 2-room apartment in Turin means putting 60,000-130,000 euro on the table, then handling the notary, the renovation, the contracts, the tenants and the tax paperwork. That is why many people who would like property exposure never start.

Invest-to-rent starts from a different idea: you take part in a single, already structured property operation instead of buying and running the flat yourself. This guide explains how the model works, what the alternatives are in Italy, and which questions to ask before moving.

---

## What invest-to-rent means {#definizione}

The term describes an approach where capital is exposed to a property meant for renting, while the day-to-day work sits with the operator. Three components are always present:

1. **A real, identifiable property.** Not an index, not an abstract basket: a flat with an address, a purchase price and a defined use.
2. **A value plan.** Buying below market value, renovation, furnishing, putting it on the rental market.
3. **Ongoing professional management.** Contracts, tenant selection, maintenance, tax compliance, dealings with the building.

The investor follows how the operation performs. The operator does the daily work.

## Why Turin, and why student housing {#perche-torino}

Turin combines two conditions that rarely coexist: entry prices that are still moderate and structurally high rental demand.

| Factor | Situation in Turin |
|---|---|
| Average price per m² | Around 2,150 euro, well below Milan |
| University population | Over 100,000 students across UniTo and Politecnico |
| Seasonality | Students from September to June, tourism in summer |
| Most liquid property type | 2- and 3-room flats near universities and hospitals |

The student + tourist combination is what we call the dual-season model: the flat does not sit empty during the months when universities close. For market figures area by area, see the guide on [student housing yields in Turin](/blog/rendimento-student-housing-torino-2026).

## Four ways to get property exposure {#alternative}

| Route | Typical capital | Who manages | What you hold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buying on your own | 60,000-130,000 euro plus costs | You | The whole property |
| Listed property funds | From a few hundred euro | The fund manager | Units in a basket of properties |
| Property crowdfunding | From 500 euro | The platform and developer | A claim or a share in the project |
| Invest-to-rent on a single operation | Defined operation by operation | The operator running the property | Economic exposure to that operation |

None of the four is better in absolute terms. What changes is control, liquidity, transparency on the underlying property and how much effort is required from you.

## Questions to ask before investing {#domande}

They apply to us as much as to anyone else proposing a property operation.

- Which exact property is it, and where?
- Who is the contractual counterparty, and in what legal form?
- How is the purchase price calculated, and who checks it?
- What happens if the property sits empty for a few months?
- What are the management costs, and how do they affect the result?
- What is the time horizon, and how do you exit?
- What reporting do I receive, and how often?

If any of those answers is vague, it is worth stopping.

## The risks, stated plainly {#rischi}

Property is not a guaranteed product. The main risks of an operation like this are:

- **Market risk.** Property prices can fall.
- **Vacancy risk.** A period without tenants reduces income.
- **Execution risk.** Renovations can take longer or cost more than planned.
- **Liquidity risk.** Property does not sell in a day like a listed security.
- **Regulatory and tax risk.** Rules on short lets and taxation change.

A serious operator explains these risks up front, not afterwards.
