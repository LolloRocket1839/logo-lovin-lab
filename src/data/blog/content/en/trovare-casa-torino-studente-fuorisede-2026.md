# How to find housing in Turin as a student: practical guide 2026 {#intro}

Finding housing in Turin from outside the city — especially from abroad — is one of the most concrete challenges of the first university year. The market has changed significantly since 2021: rents have risen 56%, demand structurally exceeds supply, and scams have multiplied. This guide provides a concrete, not optimistic, procedure.

On average it takes **2–4 weeks** if you start searching at least 6–8 weeks before arrival. The most effective portals are Immobiliare.it, Idealista, Bakeca and Uniplaces. The best zones for students are [San Salvario](/blog/san-salvario-guida-studenti), Vanchiglia, Cenisia and Aurora. The market is tight but not impossible — with the right strategy you can find a single room at €350–530/month.

---

## Before searching: understand the market {#market}

Turin has **107,000+ students** (UniTo + PoliTo) and less than **6.5% coverage** from institutional housing (EDISU + private student residences). The math is simple: demand massively exceeds supply. In practice:

- Good rooms in central zones are taken within **24–72 hours** of the listing going up
- September and October are the most competitive months, followed by February
- Arriving without a reservation is a real problem

The solution is the same across nearly all European student rental markets: **start early**, have your documents ready, and move fast when you find something that works.

## The portals: where to actually search {#portals}

### For rooms in shared apartments (the most common solution)

**Immobiliare.it** — the largest Italian database, with precise filters for zone, price, and contract type. Email alerts available. Many private listings (no agency).

**Idealista** — good coverage in Turin, modern interface, useful for comparing average prices by zone. Alerts also available.

**Bakeca.it** — aggregator with many private listings at often lower prices than Immobiliare/Idealista. Lower quality filtering — watch out for scams.

**Uniaffitti** — portal dedicated to students, with verified listings from landlords used to renting to university students. Lower coverage but higher quality.

### For international students

**HousingAnywhere** — international platform with Turin listings, contracts in English, online payment. 8% commission on contract value. Higher average prices but lower scam risk.

**Uniplaces** — similar to HousingAnywhere, more oriented to short-term. Useful for Erasmus semester stays.

**Spotahome** — virtual video tours, standardized contracts. Premium prices but high transparency.

**Erasmusu** — Erasmus student community with peer-to-peer listings. Lower prices, fewer guarantees.

### Informal channels (often the fastest)

**Facebook groups:** search "Affitti studenti Torino", "UniTo affitti", "PoliTo alloggi", "Torino stanze studenti". Many landlords post only here, often before listing on portals.

**Telegram:** faculty and student association groups. Those who register before enrollment can already start searching through these channels.

**Physical university bulletin boards:** on UniTo and PoliTo campuses there are still boards with paper listings. Counterintuitive, but often free of online competition.

## The zones: where to live based on your university {#zones}

### If you study at Politecnico di Torino

PoliTo is concentrated in the **Crocetta** district (Corso Duca degli Abruzzi). The best zones by distance and price:

- **Crocetta / San Paolo / Cenisia:** 0–15 minutes on foot, prices from €380–500 single. The most obvious and most competitive choice
- **[San Salvario](/blog/san-salvario-guida-studenti):** 15–20 minutes on foot or 10 by bike/tram. Good mix of price (€400–530) and neighborhood life
- **Centro:** 20–25 minutes on foot, higher prices but excellent connections to everything

### If you study at Università di Torino

UniTo is spread across multiple locations:

- **Campus Luigi Einaudi (CLE)** — economics, law, political science: **Vanchiglia / Aurora** zone (5–15 min on foot), prices €300–480
- **Palazzo Nuovo** — humanities, philosophy, languages: **Centro storico** (Via Po, Via Roma), prices €420–600
- **Via Pietro Giuria** — natural sciences, chemistry, physics: **Crocetta / Centro** zone, prices €380–530
- **Medicine (Molinette):** **[San Salvario](/blog/san-salvario-guida-studenti) / Lingotto** zone, prices €380–480

## Documents you need: prepare them before searching {#documents}

Showing up to a meeting with a landlord without documents is one of the fastest ways to lose an apartment. Prepare in advance:

**For Italian students:**
- Valid ID
- Codice fiscale
- University enrollment certificate (downloadable from the university)
- Parent's guarantee (often required): income, pay slip or recent tax return

**For international students (non-EU):**
- Valid passport
- Residence permit or postal kit receipt
- [Codice fiscale](/blog/codice-fiscale-studenti-stranieri-torino-2026) (obtainable online via Agenzia delle Entrate or at the office)
- University admission letter
- Proof of financial means ([bank account](/blog/conto-corrente-studenti-stranieri-italia-2026) statement or guarantor letter)

**For EU students:**
- ID or passport
- Codice fiscale
- University enrollment certificate
- Possible guarantee

## Contract types: which to choose {#contracts}

**Student university contract (3+2):** duration 6–36 months, automatically renewable. Reserved for students enrolled in universities, master's or post-graduate programs. Tax benefits for the landlord ([cedolare secca](/blog/cedolare-secca-2026-investitori) at 10%), so rents are often lower than free market. The best choice for stays of at least one year.

**Transitory contract (1–18 months):** for those staying a few months. Requires documented motivation (e.g., internship, specialist course). Not the standard student contract.

**Free-market lease ([canone libero](/blog/guida-affitto-italia-stranieri-2026) 4+4):** more tenant protections in case of problems, but higher rents and higher taxes for the landlord. Less common in the student segment.

**Room in shared apartment without own contract:** in some arrangements the main contract is in one tenant's name and others live as "guests." Legally fragile for the guests — avoid.

**Security deposit:** by law maximum 3 months' rent. Not a lost expense — returned at end of contract if the apartment is in the same condition.

## How to avoid scams: warning signs {#scams}

36% of students searching for housing in Turin encounter at least one suspicious listing. The most common scam signals:

❌ **Price too low:** a central single room at €250 all-inclusive does not exist. If it seems too good to be true, it's fake.

❌ **Landlord abroad:** "I'm outside Italy for work, I'll send you the keys after the wire transfer." Classic scam.

❌ **Payment requested before visit:** no legitimate landlord asks for money before showing the apartment.

❌ **Stock photos:** search the images with Google Lens. If they appear on dozens of other sites, the apartment doesn't exist.

❌ **Contract via WhatsApp/email without meeting:** can be real but high risk. Before paying any amount, visit the property in person or through someone you trust.

✅ **The golden rule:** always visit before paying. If not possible (e.g., students from abroad), request a live video call from the apartment with the landlord showing ID.

## Institutional housing: applying to the EDISU call {#edisu}

For those with ISEE ≤ €26,306, the **EDISU call** offers beds in university residences in Turin at rates far below market. The 2025–2026 call assigned roughly **2,750 beds** in Turin.

- **When to apply:** the call typically opens in **May–June** for the following year. Missing the deadline means losing the opportunity for the year.
- **Score:** depends on ISEE, academic merit and distance from municipality of origin
- **Monthly fee:** from roughly €100 to €350 depending on income and room type

Even without winning the call, it's possible to get on the waiting list. Many students obtain a spot in October or November.

## Checklist before signing {#checklist}

Before signing any contract:

☐ Have you visited the apartment in person?
☐ Does the contract state: duration, rent, deposit, return method, expenses included/excluded?
☐ Does the [canone concordato](/blog/canone-concordato-torino-2026-guida-completa) have the conformity attestation (bollinatura) from a signatory association? (mandatory for tax benefits)
☐ Is the security deposit ≤ 3 months' rent?
☐ Have you checked the apartment's condition (utilities, humidity, heating)?
☐ Do you know who to contact for urgent repairs?
☐ Will the contract be registered at the Agenzia delle Entrate within 30 days of signing?
