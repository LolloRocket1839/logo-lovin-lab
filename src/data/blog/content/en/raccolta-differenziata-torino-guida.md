# Waste Sorting in Turin: Complete Practical Guide

Waste sorting in Turin is a mandatory and well-structured service managed by **Amiat Gruppo Iren** across nearly the entire urban territory. With a current rate of **57.2%**, the city aims to reach **65% by 2027**, aligning with European standards. This comprehensive guide explains how to properly sort waste in Turin, what collection methods exist, where to take special materials, and how to avoid penalties. Reading this information carefully is essential to contribute to an efficient and sustainable recycling system.

## History and Evolution of Waste Sorting in Turin

Turin's waste collection has a fascinating history dating back to the origins of the modern city. Until the 1940s, **local families privately managed collection**, taking waste to farms around Turin where valuable materials like metals, paper, and rags were sorted. Only in **1945 did the City of Turin assume exclusive responsibility** for collection, street cleaning, and landfill disposal. In 1958, Urbiochimica S.p.A. won the first modern service contract, introducing **the first rationalization of waste sorting**.

True waste sorting began in the **1980s**, first introduced through street collection using bins placed on public roads. A major qualitative leap occurred in **2004 when the city introduced the "door-to-door" system**, an innovation that revolutionized waste management by significantly increasing sorting percentages. This method was based on a principle of gradual expansion from the city's outer perimeter toward the historic center, allowing **much higher waste sorting percentages** compared to the street system.

In **2019, Amiat introduced a further innovation: smart eco-islands**, a residential system based on large-sized bins with controlled access via electronic card. This solution represents a middle ground between street collection and door-to-door, perfect for central and densely populated areas. Today, **waste sorting is active and mandatory throughout the city of Turin** with different methods depending on the area.

## The Three Waste Collection Methods in Turin

Waste sorting in Turin is organized into three different methods, depending on the area where you live. It's essential to know which system is active in your neighborhood to properly follow the rules.

### Door-to-Door Collection

The **door-to-door method is the most widespread** and currently covers nearly two-thirds of Turin's territory, particularly in the following neighborhoods: Madonna del Pilone, Borgo Po, Cavoretto, Barca Bertolla, Regio Parco, Rebaudengo-Pietra Alta, Falchera, Villaretto, peripheral areas of Borgo Vittoria and Madonna di Campagna, Lucento, Vallette, Parella, Borgo Campidoglio, Pozzo Strada, Mirafiori Nord, Mirafiori Sud, Nizza Millefonti, Crocetta, San Salvario, Santa Rita, Vanchiglietta, Borgo Rossini, Lingotto, Filadelfia, Aurora, San Paolo, and Cenisia.

In this system, **large street bins are replaced by smaller containers** placed inside courtyards or condominium premises. Each building receives **specific wheeled containers for each waste fraction**, which are put out according to a **precise collection calendar** provided by Amiat. It's essential to **use exclusively the bins assigned to your building** and strictly respect the days and times indicated in the calendar.

### Smart Eco-Islands System (Proximity Islands)

The **smart eco-islands consist of intelligent bins with controlled access**, accessible exclusively via a **personal electronic card**. Each household receives a card enabled to unlock the bins of specific eco-islands, allowing waste disposal **on the days and times you prefer**, without specific time constraints.

Eco-islands have been progressively installed since 2019 in various city neighborhoods, including the central part of San Salvario (Quadrilatero), Traiano, San Secondo, San Donato, Spine, Barriera di Milano, Borgata Vittoria-Madonna di Campagna, and Falchera. **Eco-islands don't always include paper and cardboard collection**: for these materials, the door-to-door method usually remains with yellow containers placed in courtyards.

The fractions that can be disposed of at eco-islands are: **glass and metal packaging, plastic packaging, organic waste, and non-recoverable residue**, each distinguished by a specific color. **Each fraction is identified by a uniform color throughout the city**, easily recognizable.

### Street Collection

Street collection remains **still present in Turin's historic center**, where **large containers placed on public ground** are used. These bins are accessible to anyone and grouped in **eco-stations** where different waste fractions can be disposed of. However, this system is progressively being reduced: the City of Turin has planned to **eliminate street collection from the center by 2026**, replacing it with complete door-to-door service. The last eco-islands in the center perimeter will be installed in the Falchera, Rebaudengo, and Regio Parco neighborhoods at the beginning of 2026.

## The Five Main Waste Fractions in Turin

### Paper and Cardboard (Yellow Container)

Collection of **paper and cardboard in Turin is exclusively residential** and takes place **using yellow-closure containers**. This category includes:

**What to dispose of correctly:**
- Newspapers, magazines, and periodicals
- Notebooks and paper sheets
- Books and pamphlets
- Paper and cardboard packaging
- Boxes and cartons
- Beverage cartons (tetrapak and multi-layer)
- Non-plasticized wrapping paper
- Dismantled archives and paper sheets

**Important rules:**
- **Material must be clean and free of organic waste**
- **Boxes and cartons must be flattened** to save space
- **Don't use plastic bags**: materials must be disposed of loose or in cellulose bags
- **Don't dispose of wax paper, parchment paper, aluminum foil, receipts**, tissues, greasy or very dirty paper
- Remove adhesive tapes and other materials from boxes before disposal
- Don't put paper in plastic containers together with other materials

Paper collection service is performed by **Cooperativa Arcobaleno with weekly frequency**, both in areas served with residential door-to-door system and at eco-islands. If you encounter service problems, you can contact **Customer Care Ambientale Amiat at 800.017.277**.

### Glass and Cans (Blue/Brown Container)

In Turin, **glass collection is combined with cans and all metal packaging**. This system was chosen to rationalize the collection and recycling process. In the bins and appropriate blue or brown colored containers you can dispose of:

**What to dispose of:**
- Glass bottles for beverages and food
- Glass jars
- Glass containers
- Aluminum cans
- Metal cans
- Metal packaging in general

**Important rules:**
- **Containers must be empty** and it's recommended to **rinse them briefly** to avoid bad odors
- **Bottles and flasks should be crushed horizontally**, not from cap to bottom
- **Crystal and ceramics (glasses, broken vases) DON'T go in glass**, but in unsorted waste, as they have different melting temperatures
- **Metal caps and glass jars must be separated** and disposed of in respective collections
- Don't dispose of mirrors, thermometers, or light bulbs, which go to collection centers
- Don't throw any other material that isn't packaging in this container

### Plastic Packaging (Light Blue/Yellow Container)

**Plastic packaging** is collected in specific containers, usually light blue or yellow. It's important to remember that **only plastic packaging goes in this collection**, not all plastic objects.

**What to dispose of:**
- Plastic bottles for beverages (water, soft drinks, juices)
- Detergent and household product bottles
- Food plastic packaging
- Plastic trays
- Plastic bags (preferably transparent)
- Films and wrappings

**Important rules:**
- **Clean containers and remove food or liquid residues** before throwing them away
- **Bottles and containers must be crushed** horizontally to save space
- **Screw the cap back on** the bottle before crushing it
- **Plastic objects like pens, balls, toys, consoles, and sunglasses DON'T go in plastic collection**, as they aren't packaging
- Don't dispose of bioplastic or broken compostable bags
- Don't dispose of irrigation pipes, construction material, or rubber objects

According to CONAI Consortium, all plastic packaging with the acronyms **PE, PET, PVC, PS, PP** can be disposed of.

### Organic Waste (Brown/Green Container)

**Organic or wet waste** is collected in dedicated containers, usually brown or green. This is a particularly important fraction for composting and reducing the load on waste-to-energy plants.

**What to dispose of:**
- Kitchen scraps (fruit and vegetable leftovers, coffee and tea grounds)
- Bread, pasta, and rice leftovers
- Eggshells and fruit pits
- Dry leaves and grass clippings
- Wilted flowers and cut plants
- Pruning branches
- Dirty paper napkins and tissues
- **Compostable items** such as shopping bags, produce bags, compostable films, compostable trays, dishes, cutlery, and compostable coffee capsules

**Important rules:**
- **Material must be clean and free of inorganic waste**
- **Containers must be empty** and clean
- **Don't dispose of**: plastic, used oils, diapers, hygiene products, cigarette residues, liquids, metal, glass, porcelain, terracotta, medicines, gauze and bandages, absorbents
- Use **compostable bags**, never traditional plastic bags
- Don't throw wet waste in non-compostable plastic containers

**Important note**: where dedicated organic collection isn't active, waste must be disposed of in residual (unsorted) waste.

### Unsorted Waste - Non-Recoverable (Black/Gray Container)

**Unsorted or non-recoverable waste** is the residual fraction where all materials that don't fit in other containers should be disposed of. This is the fraction with the least sustainable fate (landfill or waste-to-energy plant), so it's important to minimize it.

**What to dispose of:**
- Waste composed of multiple inseparable materials (brushes, toothbrushes, razor blades, pens, ballpoints)
- Broken ceramics and porcelain
- Broken glass objects (not bottles)
- Wax paper, parchment paper, aluminum foil, receipts
- Medicine boxes and packaging
- Toys (unless recyclable)
- Plastic cutlery
- Clean rags and cloths
- Household sweeping residues
- Adhesive tape
- Diapers and absorbent hygiene products
- Pet litter and pet waste
- Photographs
- Empty print cartridges

**Important rules:**
- **Close bags well** before putting them out
- **Place sharp or biohazardous objects** (broken glasses, ceramics, used syringes) **in an additional protective film/bag** to protect workers' safety
- **Minimize waste bulk**
- **Expired medicines, batteries, and cartridges DON'T go in unsorted waste**, but in specific containers or collection centers

## Special Waste: Where to Take It

Besides the five main fractions, Turin has **dedicated systems for special waste** that requires specific disposal and cannot be placed in ordinary bins.

### Expired Medicines

**Expired or unused medicines must be deposited in appropriate containers** located at pharmacies and medical clinics in the city. **They should never be thrown in unsorted waste or down the drain**, as they contain active ingredients that can alter natural environmental balances. Only **medicine cardboard packaging** (without the medicine) can be disposed of in paper collection.

### Batteries

**Spent batteries are returned to battery retailers** or deposited in **containers present in some municipalities** and at collection centers. Regarding **car batteries**, they don't fall under Amiat-managed collection: they must be given directly to the retailer or specialized centers. **Small electronic objects with integrated batteries** (remote controls, watches, toys) must be taken to RAEE collection centers.

### Electrical and Electronic Equipment (WEEE)

**WEEE (Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment)** includes refrigerators, washing machines, televisions, computers, printers, and all electrical appliances and electronic devices at end of life. **They should never be abandoned or thrown in ordinary bins**. In Turin, WEEE can be disposed of in different ways:

**Store pickup (one to one)**: For small-sized equipment, retailers are required to collect a similar object that has become waste for each purchase of new equipment.

**WEEE collection centers**: Turin has several specialized centers, including **Transistor S.r.l** (via Paolo Veronese 202, Turin North), partnered with over 100 municipalities in the province.

**Home pickup**: Amiat offers free home pickup services for large bulky items and WEEE.

### Used Cooking Oils

Amiat has introduced a **new project for street collection of used domestic cooking oils**, in collaboration with **CONOE (National Consortium for Collection and Treatment of Used Vegetable and Animal Oils and Fats)**. This service is completely **free**. Used oils should never be poured down drains or thrown in unsorted waste, as they cause serious environmental pollution.

### Bulky Waste

**Bulky waste** (furniture, mattresses, bed frames, armchairs, deck chairs, mirrors, ironing boards, etc.) can be:

- Delivered **free and without annual pickup limits** at one of the city's **collection centers**
- Booked for **free home pickup** via the Iren Ambiente app or by calling 800.017.277

This is an important innovation introduced by the new Municipal Regulation 280 in 2025, which eliminated free pickup limits to encourage proper disposal.

### Used Clothing and Textiles

**Used clothing, shoes, and accessories** can be taken to collection centers or disposed of in **appropriate street containers** located throughout the city. They must be placed in **well-closed bags, not too bulky, clean, washed, and well folded**.

## Most Common Mistakes to Avoid

One of the main problems with waste sorting is that **disposal errors significantly reduce the quality of collected waste**, compromising recycling and causing damage to recovery chains. Amiat estimates that **errors represent about 20% of separate collection**, equal to about 100,000 tons per year of waste that cannot be recycled.

### Common Paper Mistakes

- **Disposing of paper in plastic bags**: if you use a plastic bag to collect paper, you must empty it before disposal, not put it together in collection
- **Not flattening boxes and cartons**: they take up unnecessary space and prevent fully filling containers
- **Throwing receipts in paper**: receipts are actually made with thermal paper and go in unsorted waste
- **Disposing of wax paper, parchment paper, and aluminum foil**: these materials aren't recyclable and go in unsorted waste
- **Adding adhesive tapes and other materials**: they must be removed from boxes before disposal

### Common Plastic and Glass Mistakes

- **Throwing plastic objects that aren't packaging**: pens, balls, toys, consoles, and sunglasses should go in unsorted waste, not plastic
- **Not crushing bottles correctly**: they must be crushed horizontally, not from cap to bottom, and the cap must be screwed back on
- **Throwing crystal and ceramics in glass**: broken glasses and vases, which are crystal and ceramics, have different melting temperatures and ruin the recycling process. They go in unsorted waste
- **Separating metal cap from glass jar only after disposal**: they must be separated before and disposed of in respective collections
- **Throwing containers with uncleaned food residues**: they must be rinsed before disposal

### Common Organic Mistakes

- **Using traditional plastic bags**: organic waste must be disposed of in compostable bags, as the composting process will destroy the bags too
- **Throwing non-compostable materials**: raw meat and fish can attract rodents if not properly managed, while liquids, metal, glass, ceramics, medicines, and gauze compromise composting
- **Inserting material contaminated by toxic substances**: the resulting compost should be usable in agriculture, so no contamination

### Common Unsorted Waste Mistakes

- **Throwing medicines in unsorted waste**: they go in pharmacy containers
- **Not protecting sharp or hazardous objects**: broken glasses, ceramics, syringes must be placed in additional protective film
- **Not compressing waste**: they take up unnecessary space in containers

## Penalties for Incorrect Sorting

The City of Turin applies **significant administrative penalties** for those who don't follow waste sorting rules. **The most common violations include**:

- **Use of non-compliant containers** (unsuitable bags, containers not assigned to the building)
- **Disposal of waste in incorrect methods, days, or times**
- **Improper sorting** of materials

**Penalties range from 25 to 500 euros** depending on the violation's severity. Specifically, **waste abandonment** carries amounts ranging from **300 to 3,000 euros for non-hazardous waste** and from **300 to 6,000 euros for hazardous waste**.

In **2024, Amiat issued over 1,140 fines** to Turin citizens for sorting errors. The City of Turin is also **working on a modification to the urban police regulation** that will provide **even more important penalties for those who abandon waste outside bins**.

Local police can **analyze abandoned waste to identify the offender and fine them**: indeed, folders, documents, or other identifiers are often found in waste. It's therefore crucial **not to put waste outside assigned containers and strictly respect disposal times**.

## Home Composting: A Conscious Alternative

If you own a garden or vegetable garden in Turin, **home composting represents an excellent alternative** to further reduce organic waste production and obtain free natural fertilizer. Home composting is a **simple and economical** practice that transforms kitchen scraps and plant residues into **compost**, a dark soil rich in nutrients ideal for vegetable gardens, gardens, and potted plants.

### Benefits of Home Composting

- **Organic waste reduction**: less wet waste to be collected and treated by municipal operators
- **Greenhouse gas emission reduction**: less methane produced by landfill decomposition
- **Free natural fertilizer**: completely sustainable nutrient-rich soil
- **More fertile soil**: compost improves soil structure and deeply nourishes plants
- **Waste tax discount**: in many municipalities, those practicing home composting are entitled to a reduction on the variable part of the tariff

### How to Start

Starting home composting is simpler than you think:

1. **Get a composter**: choose a container suitable for the space you have (garden, balcony, or terrace)
2. **Prepare material**: always remember to **balance wet and dry materials**. Alternate layers of about 20 cm, mixing nitrogen-rich scraps (cut grass, fruit and vegetable leftovers) with carbon-rich scraps (foliage, branches, paper)
3. **Shred material**: if too large, use a shredder to speed up decomposition
4. **Maintain humidity**: the pile must be neither too wet nor too dry
5. **Stir periodically**: speeds up the process and promotes aeration

### What to Compost and What to Avoid

**You can compost:**
- Fruit and vegetable leftovers
- Coffee and tea grounds
- Dry leaves and straw
- Branches and small twigs
- Wilted flowers
- Non-plasticized paper and cardboard
- Dirty paper tissues and napkins
- Extinguished fireplace ashes
- Cork stoppers

**Don't compost:**
- Raw meat and fish (can attract rodents)
- Cooked food
- Oils and fats
- Any type of plastic
- Metal, glass, ceramics
- Medicines
- Pet litter
- Any material contaminated by toxic substances

Many provincial municipalities offer **home composting courses** that are sometimes linked to **waste tax reductions**. We recommend checking with the City of Turin or Amiat to find out if training initiatives and incentives exist in your area.

## Practical Tips for Conscious Waste Sorting in Turin

### Read Symbols on Packaging

Many packages display **environmental labels indicating where to throw them**. Always read the instructions on packaging before disposal.

### Clean Containers

**Empty and briefly rinse** bottles, jars, and trays before throwing them away to avoid bad odors and contamination. This simple action **significantly improves collection quality**.

### Reduce Bulk

Flatten boxes and bottles, screw caps back on, and compress waste to reduce space occupied in containers. This allows collecting more material in fewer visits.

### Clippings and Pruning

If you have a garden, instead of throwing grass clippings and branches in organic waste, it's better to use them for home composting or take them to collection centers, where they're sent to specific recovery circuits.

### Use Interactive Search

If you have doubts about specific waste, use the **"Waste Dictionary"** available on the Amiat website or download the **"Rifiutologo"**, a complete PDF guide in A5 format you can always carry with you.

### Report Problems

If you notice **waste abandonment situations or container problems**, you can **report directly via the Iren Ambiente app** to request cleaning interventions.

## Future Perspectives and System Evolution

Turin has planned an **important evolution of waste sorting in the coming years**. The city's goal is to **reach 65% waste sorting by 2027**, thus exceeding European standards and approaching the **70% provided by national legislation by 2025**.

For **2025, door-to-door service will progressively extend** to still uncovered central and peripheral neighborhoods, particularly in the Vanchiglia and Piazza Vittorio area. About **2,900 Vanchiglia households**, currently at **45.1% waste sorting** (one of the city's lowest rates), will switch to door-to-door system, with the goal of significantly increasing sorting percentages.

At regional level, Piedmont reached **67.9% waste sorting in 2023**, exceeding the 65% target and approaching the 70% goal for 2025. Turin, with its current **57.2%**, still has room for improvement and represents a priority for the Region's and City's environmental investments.
