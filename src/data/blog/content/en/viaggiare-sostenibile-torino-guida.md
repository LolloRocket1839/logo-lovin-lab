# How to reach Turin sustainably: a practical guide

Turin sits at a crossroads of European rail networks, making it one of Italy's most accessible cities for low-carbon travel. This guide covers trains, coaches, and sustainable options for getting around once you arrive.

## Getting to Turin by Train

### European rail connections

From **London**, take Eurostar to Paris (2 hours), then TGV from Gare de Lyon to Turin. The entire journey generates about 2.0 kg of CO₂ per passenger, compared to 61.5 kg by plane. That is a 97% reduction in emissions.

From **Rome**, Frecciarossa and Italo trains take 4.5-5 hours. The route passes through scenic stretches near La Spezia and Cinque Terre. Intercity trains cost less but take longer.

From **Milan**, trains depart hourly and arrive in one hour. **Genoa** is 1 hour 45 minutes away. Other major Italian cities offer frequent high-speed connections.

### The Frecciarossa 1000

This train holds the first environmental impact certification (EPD) for high-speed rail. It generates just **28 micrograms of CO₂ per passenger-kilometer** and uses materials that are **97.1% recyclable**.

## Budget-Friendly Coach Travel: FlixBus and Sustainable Alternatives

For cost-conscious travelers willing to spend more time traveling, **FlixBus** offers an exceptional sustainability profile. In 2024, FlixBus passengers collectively avoided **1.5 million tonnes of CO₂** simply by choosing buses over alternative transportation methods. On a per-person-kilometer basis, FlixBus coaches emit **five times less CO₂ than private cars and ten times less than planes**, while performing almost as well as intercity trains. This makes coach travel particularly attractive for budget travelers from France, Germany, Belgium, and other nearby countries.

When booking FlixBus journeys, passengers can voluntarily offset their remaining emissions through donations to **atmosfair**, an environmental NGO supporting climate projects certified by the UN and Gold Standard. FlixBus has already tested innovative **solar panel technology** on buses across Europe and North America, and through the Future of Mobility Fund financed by passenger donations, partners can adopt these solar solutions to further reduce emissions.

## Flying to Turin: The Less Sustainable But Necessary Option

For intercontinental travelers, flying to **Turin Airport** (Sandro Pertini) may be unavoidable, but the airport itself has become a sustainability leader. In December 2024, Turin Airport achieved **Level 3+ Neutrality certification** from the Airport Carbon Accreditation programme, representing the highest level of carbon management. Over the past five years, the airport reduced CO₂ emissions under its direct control by **over 80% compared to 2019 levels**. In 2023 alone, the airport saved more than **10,000 tonnes of CO₂**—equivalent to planting 330,000 trees.

The airport's sustainability initiatives include a **100% electric vehicle fleet** for ground handling operations, a hydrogen production smart grid system (the first at an Italian airport), and an extensive **photovoltaic system** on the terminal roof that exceeds the airport's annual electricity consumption by 12%. Remarkably, the airport has accelerated its net-zero emissions target from 2050 to **2040**, bringing forward its commitment by a full decade.

### From Airport to City Center: Sustainable Connections

Multiple eco-friendly options connect the airport to Turin's city center. The **train option** is the most sustainable: a direct rail link reaches the GTT Dora Railway Station in just **19 minutes**, with departures every 30 minutes from 5:03 AM to 11:08 PM. A single ticket costs approximately **€3**, and from GTT Dora, travelers can connect to the city center by bus in 19 minutes.

**ARRIVA bus service** provides both standard and express options, with express buses completing the journey in approximately 45-50 minutes with stops at Porta Nuova and Porta Susa railway stations. One-way fares cost **€7.50**, with round-trip promotions available at €11.00. The service operates every 15-30 minutes depending on the time of day.

For those preferring traditional transportation, **SADEM buses** also operate airport-to-city service with one-way fares of €6.50 (plus €1.00 extra if purchased aboard). Multiple departure locations in the city center (Porta Nuova, Porta Susa, and Via Stradella) provide flexibility for onward travel.

## Moving Through Turin Sustainably

### Public Transportation System

Turin possesses **an extensive public transportation network** combining subway, bus, and streetcar services that efficiently reach all major city destinations. The **metropolitan railway** (metro) connects Collegno in the western suburbs with Lingotto Expo in approximately 20 minutes—a distance that would require more than an hour by ground-level transportation.

The city is undergoing a significant transformation toward **electric transit**. GTT (Gruppo Torinese Trasporti), the municipal transport operator, has ordered **225 fully electric buses** from IVECO BUS and ENEL X, representing an investment of more than €150 million in sustainable infrastructure. Additionally, GTT has signed a contract for **22 electric Indcar minibuses** capable of transporting up to 33 passengers with a range exceeding 200 kilometers. These vehicles feature advanced safety technology including e-mirrors, high-definition cameras, and systems for speed control and lane departure warnings.

### The Torino+Piemonte Card: Maximum Value for Visitors

The **Torino+Piemonte Card** represents exceptional value for sustainable visitors prioritizing walkable exploration and public transit use. Available in **1-day (€29), 2-day (€39), 3-day (€45), and 5-day (€50) versions**, the card provides free admission to approximately **80 museums, exhibitions, monuments, castles, and Royal Residences** throughout Turin and Piedmont. Each version includes **unlimited public transportation** within Turin and reduced-price access to iconic services including the panoramic lift in Mole Antonelliana and the Sassi-Superga rack tramway.

For visitors planning extended stays, the **Abbonamento Musei Piemonte** (€52) provides year-round access to essentially all cultural institutions across Turin, Piedmont, and the Aosta Valley. Calculating the value: visiting just four major attractions (approximately €65 combined ticket price) covers the cost of a 5-day card while enabling unlimited public transit use.

### Cycling: Reclaiming Turin's Streets

Turin has become a cycling paradise with **258 kilometers (160 miles) of bicycle paths** creating a safe and relaxing cycling network. The city features **175 kilometers of dedicated cycling paths** that enable visitors to navigate from the historic center to the Po River and beyond. The flat terrain and extensive infrastructure make cycling the ideal sustainable option for exploring the city's neighborhoods and cultural attractions.

**TO-BIKE** represents the primary station-based bike-sharing system, with **200 stations** distributed across the city offering both standard and electric bicycles. E-bikes are particularly valuable for reaching landmarks like the Basilica di Superga on elevated terrain. The **72-hour Torino+Piemonte Card** already includes bike-sharing benefits.

Multiple **free-flow bike-sharing options** complement TO-BIKE, including **Dott, Lime, Voi, and RideMovi**, which allow users to locate, unlock, and return bicycles via smartphone apps. This flexibility means visitors can abandon bikes at any appropriate parking location rather than returning them to specific stations.

### Electric Scooter Sharing and Micro-Mobility

**Ten authorized operators** provide electric scooter-sharing services throughout Turin. Typical pricing involves approximately €1.00 to unlock followed by €0.15–€0.24 per minute depending on the provider. Major operators include **Bird, Dott, Lime, Tier, and Voi**, all accessible through smartphone applications. Some providers offer daily and monthly subscription options providing unlimited usage for fixed fees. Scooters are restricted to bicycle lanes, streets with 30 km/h speed limits, and Zona 30 areas, but cannot be ridden on sidewalks, in pedestrian zones, or under porticoes.

### Walking: The Ultimate Sustainable Option

Walking remains the most sustainable transportation method for exploring Turin's historic center. The city features an extraordinary **18 kilometers (11 miles) of porticoes**—covered arcaded walkways providing protection from winter rain and snow as well as summer sun. Major attractions cluster within the historic center, making walking between sites straightforward and atmospheric.

The historic center includes multiple **pedestrian zones** such as **Via Garibaldi, Via Lagrange, and Via Carlo Alberto**, the primary shopping streets. A **limited traffic zone (ZTL)** operates weekdays from 7:30 AM to 10:30 AM, restricting vehicle access and further prioritizing pedestrian movement. The city offers numerous **free or low-cost walking tours** led by knowledgeable local guides who reveal hidden courtyards, historic cafés, and conventional tourist-free routes. A comprehensive self-guided walking tour beginning at the National Cinema Museum encompasses the Mole Antonelliana, Via Po, Monte dei Cappuccini, Parco del Valentino, and Castello del Valentino.

## Sustainable Travel Within Piedmont

The broader Piedmont region features multiple sustainable destinations accessible from Turin. The **Corona di Delizie** represents a spectacular **90+ kilometer cycling loop** connecting natural parks and Royal Residences while providing opportunities to appreciate the region's architectural and environmental heritage through sustainable travel. The **Strada Panoramica Zegna** offers varied cycling routes combining natural beauty with topographic challenges. The **Po River** provides a calming cycling route away from urban congestion, offering opportunities to explore the natural landscape that gave rise to Turin's identity as the Po plain gateway.

## Environmental Impact Comparison

Understanding the sustainability advantage of each transportation option helps travelers make informed choices. When traveling from Rome to Turin, high-speed rail generates approximately **28 micrograms of CO₂ per passenger-kilometer** on the Frecciarossa 1000. This compares dramatically to flying, where typical European flights generate 100-200 grams of CO₂ per passenger-kilometer. By choosing rail over flying for Italian domestic distances exceeding 500 kilometers, travelers reduce emissions by **90% or more**.

FlixBus coaches perform nearly as well as trains while offering significantly lower costs. Private car travel generates approximately five times more CO₂ per passenger than coach transport, and flying generates approximately ten times more. For environmental impact, the ranking is clear: **trains and coaches first, bicycles second, walking third, and driving or flying last**.

## Planning Your Sustainable Turin Itinerary

Begin your planning by determining arrival preferences. International travelers from Western Europe should strongly consider Eurostar-connected routes through Paris, which combine reasonable travel times (typically 10-15 hours total) with exceptional environmental credentials. Those within Italy can leverage high-speed rail networks connecting from major cities.

Once in Turin, purchase a Torino+Piemonte Card matching your intended stay duration and use unlimited public transit to reach distant attractions while walking and cycling for central exploration. The combination of electric public transit, extensive cycling infrastructure, and pedestrian-friendly historic zones ensures minimal environmental impact while maximizing cultural exploration and authentic local experience.

Consider timing your visit to coincide with specific sustainable tourism initiatives. Turin has launched ambitious projects including the **ToMove Living Lab**, an open laboratory developing smart and sustainable urban mobility scenarios with support of over €10 million in funding. The city aims to align with the European Mission "100 Climate Neutral Cities," making it an ideal destination for environmentally conscious travelers.

## Conclusion

Reaching Turin sustainably and exploring the city through eco-conscious transportation represents more than an environmental statement—it enhances the travel experience itself. Train journeys reveal European landscapes impossible to appreciate from highway or altitude. Cycling through dedicated pathways offers intimate contact with neighborhoods, cafés, and street life. Walking beneath historic porticoes connects travelers to centuries of human passage through the same spaces. The combination of world-class sustainable transportation infrastructure, affordable transit passes, and pedestrian-friendly urban design makes Turin exceptionally accessible to travelers committed to minimizing environmental impact while maximizing cultural and authentic experience.
