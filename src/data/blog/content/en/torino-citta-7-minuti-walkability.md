# Turin: a seven-minute city

Turin is the **third most walkable city in the world**. On average, it takes just 7 minutes on foot to reach schools, shops, and essential healthcare services. This isn't an exaggeration. It's the result of a study published by *World of Statistics* based on data from *The Economist*.

For those living here, this isn't just a number. It's the difference between a car-dependent life and a neighborhood-centered existence.

## Why Turin is so walkable

### A compact, rational urban grid

Turin's historic center and 19th-century expansions follow an almost Cartesian grid. Long, straight avenues and short, regular blocks. This Savoy heritage creates:

- **Direct walking routes** with few detours
- Excellent visibility and perceived safety
- High density of corner plots with ground-floor shops

Unlike many historic Italian cities, Turin is not a maze. It combines monumental boulevards with human-scale blocks.

### Mixed-use neighborhoods

Most central and semi-central neighborhoods still follow the **mixed-use model**: residential floors above, daily commerce below. On any typical street you'll find:

- Bakeries, greengrocers, minimarkets
- Bars and cafés
- Pharmacies and small clinics
- Artisanal services (tailor, cobbler, hardware store)

This distribution means residents rarely need to travel far for daily errands. Even neighborhoods undergoing transition like Aurora or Barriera di Milano maintain this commercial density.

### 18 km of continuous porticos

Turin offers over **18 km of continuous porticos** in the central area alone. They connect Piazza Castello, Via Po, Piazza San Carlo, Via Roma and beyond. These covered walkways:

- Protect from rain in winter and strong sun in summer
- Encourage strolling and shopping in any season
- Make walking the "default" choice even in bad weather

The portico system is both a practical climate adaptation and a powerful generator of pedestrian foot traffic.

### Public transport as a multiplier

Turin's integrated transport network extends the reach of walkable living:

- **Metro line 1**: connects Lingotto to Collegno through the city center
- **Tram network**: comprehensive and reliable
- **Electric buses**: dense coverage across the territory

Most residents can reach a metro or tram stop within a 5-10 minute walk. Then cover longer distances without a car.

## The daily experience

### Daily routine without a car

A typical resident in a central or semi-central neighborhood can:

1. **Morning**: Walk 3-5 minutes for breakfast at a café
2. **Errands**: Drop children at school within 5-10 minutes
3. **Work**: Reach the office on foot, by bike, or with a quick tram/metro ride
4. **Groceries**: Shop on the way home, choosing between neighborhood markets (Porta Palazzo) and supermarkets
5. **Leisure**: Access parks like Valentino, Pellerina, or Dora in just a few minutes

The absence of car commutes simplifies life, reduces expenses, and increases spontaneous social interactions.

### Urban atmosphere: quiet streets, active ground floors

Many Turin streets, especially in restricted traffic zones (ZTL) or traffic-calmed residential areas, offer:

- Lower noise levels compared to other large cities
- Safer crossings for pedestrians and cyclists
- Terraces, outdoor seating, and al fresco dining that activate public space

This encourages the "street life" described by Jane Jacobs: people on sidewalks, eyes on the street, diversified activity at different hours.

## Implications for tourism

For travelers, especially city break tourists and digital nomads, walkability is a decisive factor. Turin's "7-minute" profile creates a compelling value proposition:

- Most attractions (Royal Palace, Mole Antonelliana, Egyptian Museum, Piazza San Carlo, Murazzi, Valentino Park) are **all reachable on foot**
- Visitors can navigate the city confidently without a car and with minimal public transport use
- Neighborhood exploration (San Salvario, Vanchiglia, Cit Turin, Campidoglio) is easy and safe, encouraging longer stays

## Implications for real estate

### The "seven-minute premium" on property values

In the residential and mixed-use real estate market, walkability increasingly correlates with property values and rental demand. In Turin, neighborhoods that best embody the "7-minute city" model display:

- **Higher occupancy rates** in both long-term and mid-term rentals
- Strong appeal for students, young professionals, and international tenants
- Greater resilience during demand shocks

### Due diligence for investors

For investors, assessing a property's **walkability radius** should become a standard part of due diligence:

- Distance to metro or tram
- Proximity to supermarkets and pharmacies
- Presence of bars, cafés, coworking spaces
- Access to parks and green spaces

A unit that genuinely fits within the 7-minute envelope of daily essentials is likely to outperform a similar unit in a car-dependent location, even within the same municipal boundary.

## Turin in the European context

The statistic that **45 of the 50 most walkable cities are in Europe** underlines a continental pattern: dense, historic European urbanism naturally supports the "15-minute city" model. Turin's position suggests that:

- It competes not only locally with Milan or Bologna, but with Copenhagen, Lyon, Munich, and Vienna on quality of daily life
- Its industrial reputation underestimates its current identity as a livable, human-scale metropolis
- There is room to leverage this ranking in European funding programs and sustainability initiatives

As EU policy increasingly rewards low-emission, compact, transit-oriented cities, Turin's existing structure becomes a competitive advantage.

## Challenges and next steps

Turin's strong walkability does not mean the work is finished. Several challenges remain:

1. **Peripheral gaps**: Some outer districts lack the same amenity density and transit quality
2. **Public space quality**: Not all sidewalks, crossings, and squares meet the same standard of comfort and accessibility
3. **Cycling integration**: While cycling is growing, the network of protected bike lanes is still fragmented compared to leading European cities

However, these are precisely the types of improvements that can be addressed through targeted policy:

- Incentivizing ground-floor services and healthcare points in underserved neighborhoods
- Expanding pedestrian priority zones around schools and markets
- Completing continuous cycling corridors that complement metro and tram lines

## Conclusion

What the World of Statistics data reveals is something residents intuitively know: **Turin is a city built at human speed**.

With an average of 7 minutes to reach essential services, it fits naturally into the emerging model of compact, sustainable, livable cities.

- **For residents**: shorter, simpler days and richer neighborhood life
- **For visitors**: a city that can be discovered on foot, without stress
- **For investors and policy-makers**: a structural asset that supports tourism, talent attraction, and long-term real estate value

In an era where many metropolises are trying to retrofit walkability into car-dominated landscapes, Turin starts from a position of strength.

The challenge now is to **recognize, communicate, and carefully enhance** this advantage. Ensuring that the seven-minute city remains not just a statistic, but a lived reality for all its neighborhoods.
