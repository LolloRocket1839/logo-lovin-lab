# Sustainable mobility in Turin for students: complete guide to public transport and bike lanes

Turin stands out as one of Italy's most committed cities to promoting sustainable mobility, with an efficient public transport system and a constantly expanding cycling network. For university students, moving sustainably not only contributes to environmental protection but also represents an economically advantageous choice.

This comprehensive guide will help you navigate the various mobility options available in Turin, with all the useful links and practical tips to optimize your travel during the academic year.

## GTT public transport system

The Turin Transport Group ([GTT](https://www.gtt.to.it)) manages the entire urban and suburban public transport network in Turin, offering a comprehensive service covering the entire metropolitan area.

### Network components

The GTT network consists of:

- **Metro M1**: Automated line connecting Fermi to Lingotto, essential for reaching the Politecnico Lingotto campus
- **7 tram lines**: 3, 4, 9, 10, 13, 15, 16 crossing the city
- **80+ bus lines**: urban and suburban ensuring comprehensive connections

To plan your trips, visit [Muoversi a Torino](https://www.muoversiatorino.it), the official platform for information on schedules, routes, and real-time updates.

### Fares and Under 26 student passes

The Piedmont Region, through the [Piemove](https://www.regione.piemonte.it/web/temi/mobilita-trasporti) portal, offers various advantageous fare solutions for students:

**Under 26 annual pass:**
- Cost: approximately €230-350/year (depending on zones)
- Validity: 365 days from activation date
- Unlimited use on the entire GTT urban network

**Piemove Program - Free transport:**
The Piemove program has registered over 60,000 enrollments among Piedmont students, offering the possibility to travel free on public transport. Check the Piedmont Region portal to see if you're eligible and how to join.

### Integrated university smart card

Many Turin universities have integrated the student smart card with the public transport system. Check with your university ([Politecnico](https://www.polito.it), [University of Turin](https://www.unito.it), or [SUEDP](https://www.suedp.it)) if this option is available for you.

## Turin cycling network

Turin boasts a cycling network of **295 km**, making it one of Italy's most bike-friendly cities. The network consists of different types of cycling paths.

### Types of routes

1. **Dedicated bike lanes**: Completely separated from vehicular traffic
2. **Cycle lanes**: Marked by horizontal signage on the roadway
3. **Zone 30**: Residential areas where car-bike coexistence is facilitated by reduced speed limits
4. **River paths**: Greenways along the Po and Dora rivers offer scenic routes

To view the complete bike lane map, visit [Ecoditorino](https://www.ecoditorino.org).

### Future projects and challenges

Despite its significant extension, Turin's cycling network still has some challenges:

- Discontinuities in routes
- Dangerous intersections not always adequately signposted
- Maintenance not always optimal

The Municipality of Turin has planned network expansion with new bike lanes that will improve connections between university campuses and residential neighborhoods.

## Micromobility sharing services

Turin offers various sharing mobility options to integrate or replace traditional public transport.

### E-scooter and e-bike sharing

The main operators in Turin are:

- **[Dott](https://www.dottbikes.com)**: E-scooters and e-bikes distributed throughout the city
- **[Lime](https://www.li.me)**: E-scooters with wide coverage of the center and university areas

Both services offer:
- Competitive student rates
- Intuitive apps to locate and unlock vehicles
- Flexible parking in designated areas

### Integration with university Smart Card

Some universities have agreements with sharing mobility operators, allowing students to access discounted rates using their university smart card.

## Integrated mobility strategies for students

Choosing the most suitable mobility strategy depends on several factors: distance from university, available budget, personal preferences. Here's a comparison of the main options:

| Strategy | Annual cost | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|----------|-------------|------------|---------------|
| **Public transport only** | €230-350 | Unlimited use, no maintenance, weather-independent | Schedule dependency, possible crowding during peak hours |
| **Personal bike + occasional PT** | €100-200 (used bike + tickets) | Maximum flexibility, physical activity, zero emissions | Maintenance needed, weather-dependent, theft risk |
| **Sharing mobility + PT** | €300-500 | Flexibility, no maintenance, combination of means | Higher cost, availability not always guaranteed |
| **Bike + PT combination (pass)** | €350-450 | Maximum efficiency, health and time savings | Need to manage both means, initial bike investment |

### Recommended option: Bike + PT pass combination

For most students, the combination of **personal bicycle + Under 26 annual pass** represents the optimal solution:

- Use the bike for good weather days and short-medium distances
- Rely on public transport in bad weather or for long distances
- Maximum flexibility and economic convenience

## Practical tips for students

### Before the start of the academic year

✅ **Check eligibility for the Piemove program** to travel free  
✅ **Buy the Under 26 pass** as soon as you arrive (save compared to single tickets)  
✅ **Consider buying a used bike** (university flea markets, Facebook groups)  
✅ **Download all mobility apps** before starting  
✅ **Study the connections** between home and university using Moovit

### During the academic year

🔄 **Alternate means of transport** according to weather and commitments  
🚴 **Use the bike** for distances under 5 km (faster than PT in the center)  
⏰ **Avoid peak hours** on public transport when possible (8:00-9:00, 5:30-7:00 PM)  
📱 **Monitor in real-time** to avoid unnecessary waiting  
🛡️ **Secure your bike** with a sturdy lock (theft risk in the city)

### Cycling safety

- Always use bike lanes where available
- Wear a helmet (recommended)
- Install front and rear lights for night riding
- Respect traffic signs and signals
- Pay particular attention at intersections and roundabouts
