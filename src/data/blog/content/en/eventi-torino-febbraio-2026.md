# February 2026 in Turin: Love, Chocolate, Art and Opera

February 2026 packs an extraordinary range of experiences into just 28 days. From surrealist photography to opera masterpieces, artisanal chocolate to traditional battles, high-level basketball to intimate museum visits, the shortest month offers a dense calendar of reasons to explore the Piedmontese capital.

## Historical Photography: Two Major Retrospectives

February 2026 marks the final month of two significant photographic exhibitions. The first is **"Lee Miller. Works 1930-1955"** at CAMERA – Italian Center for Photography, closing on **February 1st**. This retrospective, curated by **Walter Guadagnini** for CAMERA's tenth anniversary, gathers over **160 photographs from the photographer's official archives**, many published for the first time. The exhibition traces her artistic trajectory from the **late Twenties until 1955**, when she stopped shooting following the traumas of war.[1][2]

Lee Miller (1907-1977) occupies a central place in twentieth-century photography: from her Paris years as **Man Ray's** assistant, where she experimented with **solarization** techniques, to her fashion photography in 1930s New York. The exhibition documents her role as a **war photographer during World War II**, when her reportages for "Vogue" covered London during the Blitz and extended to the **German concentration camps**. A final section shows her later years in Sussex with her husband **Roland Penrose**, in domestic images that retain her characteristic poetic vision and subtle irony.[2][1]

The second exhibition, **"Jeff Wall. Photographs"** at Gallerie d'Italia, runs from **October 9, 2025 to February 1, 2026**. Canadian photographer **Jeff Wall** (born 1946, Vancouver) is renowned not for the speed of his shooting but for his extreme **slowness**. Wall works like a painter: each image requires months of preparation, directing actors, building sets, calibrating lighting. In half a century, he has produced just over **two hundred large-format photographs**.[3][4]

The exhibition presents **twenty-seven works** from the late Seventies to the present, curated by **David Campany**, the leading expert on Wall's work and creative director of the International Center of Photography in New York. The photographs explore **nature, war, gender, race and social class** through staged situations that appear familiar yet generate unease upon closer inspection. His debt to cinema and Italian neorealism emerges throughout.[4][3]

## CioccolaTò: Turin Becomes the Chocolate Capital

From **February 13 to 17, 2026**, Turin transforms into the **world chocolate capital** with the second edition of **CioccolaTò**. The event returns to **Piazza Vittorio Veneto** with an extended schedule, embracing both **Valentine's Day (February 14) and Carnival** (Mardi Gras falls on February 17). The previous edition drew more than **100,000 visitors**.[5][6]

The event centers on a **rigorous selection of master chocolatiers**, favoring craftsmanship over industriality. Only producers using traditional methods and superior ingredients gain access: pure chocolatiers, pastry chefs, gelato makers, bakers, and liquor producers who integrate cocoa into their work. The **Maestri del Gusto from Turin and Piedmont** return alongside national and international specialists.[5]

CioccolaTò goes beyond a culinary fair. The program includes **tastings, talks, masterclasses, workshops for families, and guided tours** of Turin's historical and museum heritage. The "House of Chocolate" hosts conferences, the "Chocolate Factory" offers hands-on experiences, and the **Choco Tram** (a decorated historic tram) provides an immersive ride. A dedicated **Kids Area** features creative workshops. The event is promoted by the **Chamber of Commerce of Turin, the City of Turin, the Piedmont Region, the Compagnia di San Paolo Foundation, and the CRT Foundation**.[6][5]

## Opera: Verdi's Macbeth Conducted by Riccardo Muti

The opera calendar features an event of extraordinary importance: a new production of **Giuseppe Verdi's "Macbeth"** at Teatro Regio, with **performances on February 20, 24, 26 and March 1 and 3**. **Riccardo Muti** conducts, bringing fifty years of experience with this masterpiece. **Chiara Muti** directs in a **new staging long awaited** by audiences.[7]

Baritone **Luca Micheletti** takes the title role, drawing on training in both prose acting and opera. Joining him are **Lidia Fridman**, **Giovanni Sala**, and **Ildebrando D'Arcangelo**. The production showcases Verdi's ability to evoke Gothic and terrifying atmospheres: **fiery cabalettas, demonic marches, infernal dances** create a sonic fresco of unease. A preparatory lecture-concert takes place on **February 18 at 5:30 PM at Piccolo Regio Puccini**.[7]

## The Battle of the Oranges: Ivrea's Historic Carnival

A few kilometers from Turin, on **February 15, 2026**, one of Italy's most singular events unfolds: the **Battle of the Oranges** at the **Historic Ivrea Carnival**. This centuries-old celebration commemorates the revolt of the legendary **Vezzosa Mugnaia** and the Ivrea populace against the medieval tyrant. For a full day, the historic squares, including **Piazza Ottinetti, Piazza di Città, and Piazza del Rondolino**, become a cheerful battlefield.[8][9]

The event groups **nine teams of foot orange throwers** who confront over **fifty horse-drawn carts** in a spectacle combining **historical reenactment with popular participation**. The battle begins at **2:00 PM** after morning ceremonies: the **Oath of the Magnificent Podestà** at 9:30 AM in Piazza Castello and the **Ceremony of the Stone in the Dora** at 10:30 AM on the Old Bridge, where the Podestà throws a stone into the river as a symbol of peace. The **Historical Parade**, with the Mugnaia on the Golden Carriage, the General and his Staff, departs at 2:00 PM from Piazza di Città. To attend safely, wear the **Phrygian cap**, the red hat that symbolically protects spectators. The **Orange Village** offers Canavese food and wine. The day concludes at 5:30 PM with the **Generala in Piazza di Città**, the General's final salute.[9][8]

## The Frecciarossa Final Eight: top basketball in February

From **February 18 to 22, 2026**, Turin will host for the third consecutive year the **Frecciarossa Final Eight 2026**, Italy's most prestigious basketball event. At the **Inalpi Arena** (expanded by an additional **500 seats** to reach optimal capacity) the **eight best teams of Serie A Unipol 2025/26** will compete in a series of heart-pounding matches culminating in the conquest of the prestigious trophy. The 2026 edition introduces a significant novelty: the arrival of the **IBSA NextGen Cup 2025/26**, the final phase with the best **Under 19** formations of Serie A teams, offering the public the opportunity to admire the talent of young Italian basketball promises.[10][11]

The event is expected to draw **over 46,000 spectators as in past editions** and has now established itself as one of the most followed and beloved appointments for basketball fans. The public will have the choice between two types of subscriptions: the "**Full Pass**" valid for all seven games and the "**2-Day Subscription**" dedicated to Wednesday and Thursday. The atmosphere of the Turin venue, already celebrated from previous editions, will make the week of competitions even more intense, with decisive matches and moments of great sportsmanship.[11][10]

## Shows, concerts and multidisciplinary events

Alongside the major thematic events, February 2026 offers a rich program of theatrical performances and concerts. **"Aggiungi un posto a tavola"** (Set Another Place at the Table), the musical returning to Turin theaters with special guest **Lorella Cuccarini**, and **"7 Brides for 7 Brothers"** enrich the theatrical schedule alongside comedy shows such as those by **Claudio Bisio** ("La mia vita raccontata male") and **Enrico Brignano** ("I 7 Re di Roma"). Furthermore, on **February 26, 2026** at the **Gallerie d'Italia** the concert **"Candlelight: Ennio Morricone and Film Scores"** will be held at the Palazzo della Luce, an opportunity to celebrate one of Italy's greatest composers through a masterful selection of his film music.[12][13]

On the prose theater front, shows like **"Amadeus"** (February 24 to March 1 at Teatro Carignano) and **"Disagiada"** offer dramatic variety, while various theaters will host meetings, conferences, and improvisation shows that animate the city's cultural life daily.[14]

## Practical Tips for Navigation

With such a packed calendar, plan your itinerary strategically. Photography enthusiasts should catch the Lee Miller and Jeff Wall exhibitions (closing February 1st), which offer complementary perspectives: Miller probes twentieth-century history through documentary and surrealist lenses; Wall meditates on constructed vision and staged reality. CioccolaTò (February 13-17) combines sensory experiences with cultural enrichment. Opera lovers can experience an extraordinary Verdi production; tradition enthusiasts can witness the Battle of the Oranges; basketball fans can immerse themselves in the Final Eight atmosphere.[10][6][7][5]

**Check updated schedules and book in advance**, as many events sell out quickly. The online calendars of the Municipality of Turin, city portals, and museum websites filter appointments by day and category. The **geographical distribution of events** favors compact itineraries: CAMERA and Gallerie d'Italia are within walking distance of each other, as are Teatro Regio and the Royal Museums. A single day can include a morning exhibition, lunch nearby, and an evening show.[15][16][17]

## Conclusion

February 2026 offers Turin visitors the chance to experience the city through multiple registers: from historical photography to centuries-old traditions, from artisanal chocolate to world-class opera, from professional basketball to contemporary art. There is no single way to experience February in Turin, but many paths, all worth exploring. Consult the calendars, select events that reflect your interests, book ahead, and let curiosity guide you through the beauty, flavor, tradition, and innovation that define this city.
