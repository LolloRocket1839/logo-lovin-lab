# February 2026 in Turin: Love, Chocolate, Art and Opera

February 2026 transforms Turin into a city where love, flavor, beauty, and spectacle intertwine inseparably. From the surrealist charm of photography to the masterpieces of lyric dance, from artisanal sweets to traditional battles, from the emotions of high-level basketball to the most sophisticated aesthetic journeys, the short month offers a rich calendar of opportunities to discover the Piedmontese capital from new perspectives. It's a month that manages, in the space of just 28 days, to condense an extraordinary variety of experiences: from the intimate contemplation of historical photography to the collective participation in popular traditions, from the sensory enjoyment of food to the emotional transports of performing arts.

## Historical photography: two major retrospectives

February 2026 marks the final month of two fascinating photographic exhibitions that have defined Turin's cultural face in the 2025-2026 biennium. The first is **"Lee Miller. Works 1930-1955"** at CAMERA – Italian Center for Photography, which closes on **February 1st**. This major retrospective, curated by **Walter Guadagnini** in the context of CAMERA's tenth anniversary celebrations, gathers over **160 photographs from the photographer's official archives**, many of which are published for the first time. The exhibition path is structured in thematic sections that trace the complete arc of her artistic trajectory from the **late Twenties until 1955**, when the photographer stopped shooting following the traumas of war.[1][2]

Lee Miller (1907-1977) represents a central figure in the history of twentieth-century photography and contemporary culture: from her Parisian era as assistant to **Man Ray**, where she experimented with surrealist techniques of **solarization**, to her activity as a fashion photographer and portraitist in the New York society context of the Thirties. The exhibition also documents her role as a **war photographer during World War II**, when serving "Vogue" she created extraordinary reportages that from London bombed by the Nazis reached the **German concentration camps**, capturing with acuity the horror and fragile humanity of those moments. A significant section of the exhibition is also dedicated to her retreat in later years to the tranquil Sussex, when she portrayed herself alongside her husband **Roland Penrose** in domestic images that maintain the poetic vision and dissimulated irony that characterized all her research.[2][1]

Completely different, but equally compelling, is the journey proposed by **"Jeff Wall. Photographs"** at the Gallerie d'Italia in Turin, open from **October 9, 2025 to February 1, 2026**. The Canadian photographer **Jeff Wall** (born 1946 in Vancouver) is considered one of the most **significant photographic artists of our time**, not for the speed of his shooting but, paradoxically, for his extreme **slowness in creating images**. Wall is an artist who works like a painter: each image requires months of preparation, directing actors, building sets, calibrated lighting; in half a century of activity he has produced just over **two hundred large-format photographs**.[3][4]

The exhibition at Gallerie d'Italia presents **twenty-seven works** selected from the late Seventies to the present day, curated by **David Campany**, writer and curator who is the world's leading expert on Wall's work and creative director of the International Center of Photography in New York. The photographs address with thematic depth crucial questions of contemporary society, addressing themes of **nature, war, gender, race and social class** through the staging of situations that appear familiar but which, observed closely, generate unease and reflection. His influence from cinema and literature – particularly Italian neorealism – emerges in many of his compositions, where ordinary life is elevated to aesthetic contemplation of high rank.[4][3]

## The magic of sweetness: CioccolaTò returns in February

In mid-February, precisely **from February 13 to 17, 2026**, Turin transforms into the **world chocolate capital** with the second edition of **CioccolaTò**, the grand event dedicated to the "**food of the gods**". The event, which gathered more than **100,000 visitors in the previous edition**, returns to **Piazza Vittorio Veneto** with an extended duration of one day compared to 2025, thus allowing it to simultaneously embrace **Valentine's Day (February 14) and Carnival** (with Mardi Gras on February 17).[5][6]

The event revolves around a **rigorous selection of master chocolatiers**, favoring the excellence of craftsmanship over industriality and quantity. Only producers who work with traditional methodologies and superior quality raw materials will have access to the stands: not just pure chocolatiers, but also pastry chefs, gelato makers, bakers, and even liquor producers capable of integrating cocoa into their production. The return of the **Maestri del Gusto (Masters of Taste) from Turin and Piedmont** is expected alongside national and international realities that will bring unprecedented perspectives on cocoa processing.[5]

However, CioccolaTò is not a simple culinary fair: the event proposes a true **cultural and multisensory itinerary** with **tastings, specialized talks, masterclasses, interactive workshops for families, guided tours of Turin's historical and museum heritage**. The "House of Chocolate" will serve as the hub for conferences and presentations, while the "Chocolate Factory" and the **Choco Tram** (a decorated historic tram) will offer visitors an immersive experience where chocolate, history, and music blend. For the youngest, an extensive **Kids Area** offers creative workshops and play activities. The event is promoted by important institutional actors such as the **Chamber of Commerce of Turin, the City of Turin, the Piedmont Region, the Compagnia di San Paolo Foundation, and the CRT Foundation**, thus ensuring a dimension that is not only commercial but also of deep reflection on the historical, cultural, and symbolic significance of cocoa in Turinese civilization.[6][5]

## Opera: Verdi's Macbeth conducted by Riccardo Muti

On the opera front, **February 2026** offers an event of extraordinary importance: the new production of **Giuseppe Verdi's "Macbeth"** at the Teatro Regio in Turin, with **performances on February 20, 24, 26 and March 1 and 3**. The orchestra is conducted by **Riccardo Muti**, the greatest contemporary expert on this masterpiece, who has been conducting it for fifty years, continuously refining it. The direction is by **Chiara Muti**, creator of a **new staging long awaited** that promises to offer an unprecedented interpretive perspective.[7]

In the title role is baritone **Luca Micheletti**, who being a figure trained both as a prose actor and an opera singer intrinsically carries the Shakespearean drama in his blood. Alongside him are interpreters of absolute importance in the Verdian repertoire such as **Lidia Fridman**, **Giovanni Sala**, and **Ildebrando D'Arcangelo**. The staging showcases Verdi's extraordinary ability to evoke from the Italian musical language the Gothic and terrifying atmospheres of medieval Scotland: **fiery cabalettas, demonic marches, infernal dances** follow one another to create a sonic fresco of true unease, completed by an orchestral apparatus that experiments with **unprecedented skill the darkest timbres of the symphonic ensemble**. A preparatory lecture-concert is scheduled for **February 18 at 5:30 PM at Piccolo Regio Puccini**.[7]

## The Battle of the Oranges: the historic Ivrea Carnival

A few kilometers from Turin, on **February 15, 2026**, one of Italy's most singular and fascinating events takes place: the **Battle of the Oranges** of the **Historic Ivrea Carnival**. This centuries-old event is a living symbol of **popular freedom and civil resistance**, celebrating the revolt of the legendary **Vezzosa Mugnaia** and the entire Ivrea populace against the medieval tyrant. For an entire day, the historic squares – **Piazza Ottinetti, Piazza di Città, and Piazza del Rondolino** – transform into a cheerful and adrenaline-charged battlefield.[8][9]

The event groups **nine teams of orange throwers on foot** who confront armed with oranges against over **fifty horse-drawn carts**, in a spectacle that rigorously combines **historical reenactment with popular participation**. The battle begins at **2:00 PM** after a series of morning ceremonies of absolute importance: the **Oath of the Magnificent Podestà** at 9:30 AM in Piazza Castello and the evocative **Ceremony of the Stone in the Dora** on the Old Bridge at 10:30 AM, where the Podestà throws a stone into the river as a symbol of peace. The **Historical Parade**, with the Mugnaia on the Golden Carriage, the General and his Staff, departs at 2:00 PM from Piazza di Città precisely coinciding with the start of the battle. To safely attend the event, it is recommended to wear the **Phrygian cap**, the classic red hat that symbolically protects the public from the orange battle. The **Orange Village** with food and wine stands allows visitors to discover the typical flavors of Canavese, while the afternoon continues with parades of guest groups at 11:00 AM and 3:00 PM. The day concludes at 5:30 PM with the **Generala in Piazza di Città**, the General's final salute.[9][8]

## The Frecciarossa Final Eight: top basketball in February

From **February 18 to 22, 2026**, Turin will host for the third consecutive year the **Frecciarossa Final Eight 2026**, Italy's most prestigious basketball event. At the **Inalpi Arena** (expanded by an additional **500 seats** to reach optimal capacity) the **eight best teams of Serie A Unipol 2025/26** will compete in a series of heart-pounding matches culminating in the conquest of the prestigious trophy. The 2026 edition introduces a significant novelty: the arrival of the **IBSA NextGen Cup 2025/26**, the final phase with the best **Under 19** formations of Serie A teams, offering the public the opportunity to admire the talent of young Italian basketball promises.[10][11]

The event is expected to draw **over 46,000 spectators as in past editions** and has now established itself as one of the most followed and beloved appointments for basketball fans. The public will have the choice between two types of subscriptions: the "**Full Pass**" valid for all seven games and the "**2-Day Subscription**" dedicated to Wednesday and Thursday. The atmosphere of the Turin venue, already celebrated from previous editions, will make the week of competitions even more intense, with decisive matches and moments of great sportsmanship.[11][10]

## Shows, concerts and multidisciplinary events

Alongside the major thematic events, February 2026 offers a rich program of theatrical performances and concerts. **"Aggiungi un posto a tavola"** (Set Another Place at the Table), the musical returning to Turin theaters with special guest **Lorella Cuccarini**, and **"7 Brides for 7 Brothers"** enrich the theatrical schedule alongside comedy shows such as those by **Claudio Bisio** ("La mia vita raccontata male") and **Enrico Brignano** ("I 7 Re di Roma"). Furthermore, on **February 26, 2026** at the **Gallerie d'Italia** the concert **"Candlelight: Ennio Morricone and Film Scores"** will be held at the Palazzo della Luce, an opportunity to celebrate one of Italy's greatest composers through a masterful selection of his film music.[12][13]

On the prose theater front, shows like **"Amadeus"** (February 24 to March 1 at Teatro Carignano) and **"Disagiada"** offer dramatic variety, while various theaters will host meetings, conferences, and improvisation shows that animate the city's cultural life daily.[14]

## Practical tips for navigation

With such an articulated calendar, it is advisable to plan your itinerary with a targeted strategy. Those who love photography and critical thinking will find in the two major photographic exhibitions (Lee Miller and Jeff Wall) two complementary universes: the first probes twentieth-century history through the documentary and surrealist gaze; the second proposes a contemporary meditation on constructed vision and the staging of reality. Those who love taste and artisanal tradition cannot miss CioccolaTò, which combines sensory experiences with cultural deepening. Opera lovers will have the opportunity to experience an extraordinary appointment with Verdi; enthusiasts of popular traditions can participate in the Battle of the Oranges; basketball fans will immerse themselves in the adrenaline atmosphere of the Final Eight.[10][6][7][5]

It is advisable to **always check updated schedules and book in advance**, as many events sell out quickly, especially those with high-visibility artists. The online calendars of the Municipality of Turin, city portals, and museum websites allow filtering appointments by day and category, facilitating logistical coordination. Furthermore, the **geographical distribution of events on the urban map** is favorable to compact itineraries: CAMERA and Gallerie d'Italia are easily reachable on foot, as are Teatro Regio and the Royal Museums; the planning of a day can include a morning exhibition, a lunch break in the area, and an evening show.[15][16][17]

## Conclusion

February 2026 offers Turin an extraordinary opportunity to experience the Piedmontese capital through multiple expressive languages: from the intimate contemplation of great historical photography to the joyful participation in centuries-old traditions, from the sensory enjoyment of artisanal food to the emotion of world-class opera, from the adrenaline of top-level sports to the paths of contemporary reflection. There is no single way to experience February in Turin, but rather many possibilities of discovery and wonder, all equally legitimate and fascinating. The invitation is to consult the calendars, select the events that reflect your passions, book in advance, and let yourself be guided by curiosity through the multiple manifestations of beauty, flavor, tradition, and innovation that characterize this extraordinary city. February 2026 in Turin is therefore an opportunity not to be missed to renew the bond with cultural heritage, with popular traditions, and with contemporary artistic languages that continue to define the identity and charm of the Piedmontese capital.
